Poker hand-review practice 01: a decision without a revealed result

Fictional six-handed no-limit hold'em tournament exercise. This is a new, invented case, not an episode from NolanPoker, an opponent read, or an actual tournament hand. Its numbers are stipulated for practice; no solver or optimal action is supplied.

The entire fictional case

  • A six-handed table in a tournament with 14 players remaining, 10 paid. The exact payouts, field stacks, bounty terms and deal terms have not been provided.
  • Small blind 2,000, big blind 4,000, big-blind ante 4,000. The ante is a separate, dead contribution posted by the big blind. No other antes, rake, side pots or previous voluntary wagers.
  • Hero is in the big blind, holding Q♠ J♠, with 80,000 chips at the start of the hand, before posting either the live big blind or the ante.
  • The cutoff starts the hand with 96,000 chips, raises to 10,000 total, and has paid no forced blind or ante. All earlier players folded.
  • The button folds. The small blind folds, leaving its posted 2,000 in the pot. Hero now faces the cutoff's raise.
  • You observed the cutoff raise twice in the preceding six hands. You have no verified long-term opening or call-versus-reraise frequencies.
  • One possible alternative is a big-blind reraise to 24,000 total. For an arithmetic-only branch, assume the cutoff then folds immediately. There is no showdown or runout in the case.

All unmentioned cards, future board cards, opponents' actual ranges and tournament-payout values are unknown. The cutoff's hypothetical fold to 24,000 is an assumption for one branch, not a prediction.

1. Situation: separate evidence types

Copy the known game state. Label each of the following given fact, derived calculation, assumption, or unknown: the reported hero stack, the amount still behind after forced posts, the cutoff's full opening range, the cutoff folding to a 24,000 reraise, and the ICM cost of elimination.

2. Action: rebuild the chip ledger

Without looking at the key, compute:

  • Chips hero has behind after posting the live big blind and separate big-blind ante.
  • The pot before hero responds, including all dead and live contributions.
  • The additional chips to call the 10,000 raise-to amount, and the pot and chips behind after a call.
  • If hero reraises to 24,000 total, how much additional money is placed in the pot from the hero's stack?
  • If the cutoff folds to that reraise, what uncalled portion is returned, and what amount remains in the awarded pot? Separate the refund from the pot awarded to hero.

Write the arithmetic, not just the final totals.

3. Decision: identify alternatives without choosing a winner

Which action families are legally available from the big blind? Name the information required to compare folding, calling and reraising. Explain why the cutoff's opening range need not equal its calling or reraising response range.

4. What was known during the hand?

Write an observation based on the reported two raises in six hands. Then write a separate hypothesis about cutoff tendencies. Which is a stated observation, and which would require a larger or better sample to support?

5. Later review: identify what the model lacks

What exact inputs would a credible chip-EV analysis require? What extra inputs would an ICM or tournament-prize-value comparison require with 14 remaining and 10 paid? Could the known call price alone prove that calling with Q♠ J♠ is optimal when future betting remains possible?

6. Result: state only what is actually provided

Is the cutoff's actual hand or a real showdown given? Does an assumed fold in a hypothetical branch establish how often the cutoff folds in actual play? Explain why there is no outcome-based verdict.

7. One next study step

Choose one missing assumption to investigate. Record how you would obtain or test it, without turning that assumption into a reported fact. Stop if the required inputs cannot be established.
