## 2. Action in order, with distinct decision points

### Decision A: button faces the original raise

- Early-position player raises **to 15,000**. A middle-position player calls.
- Nolan has **about 61,000 before his voluntary contribution** and A-K on the button.
- Legal action families include folding, calling 15,000 or moving all-in; the narration specifically discusses the call-versus-jam choice.
- **Reported choice A:** flat-call **15,000**.
- Derived chips remaining: **61,000 - 15,000 = approximately 46,000**. The initial 10.17 BB label describes the earlier state, not the new stack behind.
- **Unknown at A:** exact covering stacks, blinds still to act, opener/caller ranges, all pot contributions and payout structure.

**Pause the review at A.** The subsequent big-blind action must not be retroactively treated as certain when evaluating the original flat.

### Decision B: new action after the button flat

- The big blind then **shoves**. The original early-position raiser **reshoves**. The middle-position caller **folds**.
- The narrated account says both all-in players covered Nolan's remaining chips. No flop is dealt before his new choice.
- **New alternatives:** fold and preserve approximately **46,000** chips, or call approximately **46,000 more** and risk elimination.
- The **15,000 previously committed** is already in the pot. It is not an additional cost of calling now or, on its own, a reason to continue.
- From the reported 6,000 posted live big blind, matching the original 15,000 raise would have required the big blind to add **9,000**, not 15,000. Its separate **6,000 big-blind ante** is dead money, not live credit toward that raise.
- **Unknown at B:** each opponent's full stack and all-in amount, the final contested pot and any side pots, the complete range of either shove, tournament payouts and any deal terms.

**Do not assign an exact pot-odds percentage.** A correct call-price calculation requires the exact contested pot and contribution levels, not just the roughly 46,000 hero must add.

## 3. What would need to be compared?

| Decision | Candidate actions | Main unresolved variables |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A, before flat** | Fold, flat, shove | Original raiser's and caller's ranges, big-blind squeeze possibilities, remaining effective stacks, antes and payout objective |
| **B, after two shoves** | Fold, call about 46,000 more | The two all-in ranges, exact pot and coverage, other tournament stacks, payouts and risk premium |

The action trees are different. At A, a shove might win through folds. At B, calling **cannot make already all-in opponents fold**. A push/fold chart for one opponent does not settle a multiple-opponent call after a prior flat. Use the [10-20 BB guide's chip-EV/ICM comparison](https://nolankido.com/poker/short-stack-decisions/#icm-call) to learn how the objective changes, not to claim it solves this particular hand.

## 5. Later review and sensitivity questions

The subsequent narration reports that, immediately after elimination, Nolan felt he could not fold A-K. Later, at the hotel, he reconsidered the **second** decision: why did the big blind shove after having the option to complete the original raise for just 9,000 more, and why did the original raiser then reshove?

These are useful questions about possible ranges, **not verified range measurements**. A defensible study would test several explicitly stated alternatives:

- What if the big blind squeezes a wide set of hands versus only premium holdings?
- How does the original raiser's reshove range change after a squeeze?
- How does the answer change when other players' stacks, payout steps, a bounty, or the objective change?
- Which inputs can be recovered from contemporaneous records, and which must remain unresolved?

**No solver or ICM calculation is claimed here.** If reliable stacks, payouts and ranges cannot be obtained, stop at a conditional comparison instead of publishing a definitive action ranking.
