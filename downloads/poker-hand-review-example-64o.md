**Source labels:** Position, action, stage, blinds and the approximate 148,000 chips are **reported recollections**. The 9.87 BB conversion is **arithmetic based on the report**, not a measured effective stack or a profitability result. The opponent's range, every effective stack, complete prize ladder and exact pot contributions are **unknown**.

**Forced-posting convention matters.** If 148,000 was the stack **before** posting the 8,000 small blind, only approximately **140,000** would remain behind after posting. That is a conditional subtraction, not an additional fact about the actual stack. The 15,000 big-blind ante is **dead money**, separate from the 15,000 live big blind. Do not combine the posted blind and ante into one live wager.

## 2. Action in order, stopping before the outcome

- The public narration states the **button folds** and the hero **shoves from the small blind**.
- The big blind **calls**. The published account does not establish the exact effective stack at the moment the shove is called, nor every earlier chip contribution.
- **Decision to review:** before pushing, would shoving, folding, completing the small blind, or using a different raise size best serve the relevant tournament objective? Which branches are actually available depends on reconstructing the complete action.
- The reported shove is **not** an independently recorded betting slip. The amount wagered and contested pot cannot be reconstructed exactly from the quoted 148,000 stack alone.

**Freeze the decision before the call.** The actual caller's holdings, runout and eventual outcome are not inputs available to the small blind when deciding whether to shove.

## 3. Compare alternatives under explicit assumptions

| Action family | Question to investigate | Evidence still required |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Fold** | What tournament value remains when the small blind declines to continue? | Exact hero stack after posting, remaining field stacks, payout schedule |
| **Complete or smaller raise** | What postflop or additional preflop decisions might follow? | Available action history, opponent frequencies, effective stacks and later-play model |
| **Shove** | How often would the big blind fold, and what happens across the hands that call? | A justified full calling range, correct pot and forced contributions, effective stacks |
| **Compare tournament value** | Does maximizing chips coincide with maximizing expected prize value near 10 paid places? | All relevant remaining stacks, exact payouts, any bounties or deal terms, objective |

The opponent might call tighter because of payout pressure, or wider because of stack coverage, personal tendencies or other incentives. **Neither frequency is established by the single observed call.** The short stack depth alone does not determine which option is best.

A claim such as "shoving 6-4 offsuit is profitable when the big blind calls tightly" is an **untested conditional hypothesis** until a precise model, input ranges and comparison method are supplied. No fold-equity, chip-EV, pot-odds, risk-premium or ICM percentage is calculated in this example.

## 5. Review the assumptions and identify a stopping point

A reasonable post-session study would vary **one input at a time**, recording the alternative assumptions rather than claiming to know them:

1. If the big blind's calling range is materially tighter or wider, how do the fold and called branches change? The actual range has not been measured.
2. If the big blind **covers** the hero versus is **covered by** the hero, how does elimination risk change? Coverage is not established by the record.
3. How do the other 12 players' remaining stacks and the payout jumps near 10 paid places affect a tournament-prize-value comparison? These data were not supplied.
4. Did the quoted 148,000 stack precede or follow posting the small blind? Would the correct effective stack change the test?
5. If we compare a shove with completing or raising smaller, does the tool actually allow those alternatives and model later streets?

Do not present a push/fold chart for a different number of players, stack convention or payout configuration as a solution to this hand. The [6-10 BB small-blind guide](https://nolankido.com/poker/six-to-ten-bb-tournament-decisions/#bridge-sb) explains the study framework but **does not solve this recorded hand**.

**Stopping point:** without exact relevant stacks, payouts, full pot contributions and an explicitly configured opponent range, **no defensible numerical chip-EV or ICM verdict is available**. Record the open questions rather than filling the gaps with plausible guesses.

## 6. Result, recorded only after the decision analysis

The big blind called with **A-K suited**, as narrated. The hero's **6-4 offsuit won** when a four appeared on the board. The source does not establish the full five-card board, the street of the four, the individual suits of 6-4, or an independently verified hand history.

The win confirms the **outcome of this reported showdown**. It does **not** prove the shove was the best choice. The revealed A-K suited establishes the caller's cards in this one hand, **not the full calling range**. Nolan's later statement that he would shove again is a personal assessment, not a validated solver recommendation.

The result must be kept separate from what could have been known at the small blind's decision.
