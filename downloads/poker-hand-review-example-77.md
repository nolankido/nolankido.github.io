## 2. Action in order, stopping before the outcome

- The **cutoff folds**. The **button opens to 75,000 total**. The **small blind folds**.
- With pocket sevens in the **big blind**, Nolan must decide how to respond to the button's raise.
- Available action families include **folding, calling 45,000 more, making a smaller reraise, or moving all-in**. The narration specifically considers calling, a smaller raise, and shoving; mentioning a legal fold does not claim he considered it aloud.
- **Reported action:** Nolan **reshoves all-in**. The button must then respond. At this point the review deliberately stops before revealing that response or any showdown.

**Freeze the decision here.** Before acting, the big blind did not know the button's exact cards or whether the button would call. The public source does not give verified stacks for every player, total pot contributions, a complete action-tree model or independent button-frequency observations.

## 3. Compare the decision branches, not just a single range

| Branch | Main mechanism | Missing inputs that could change the conclusion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Fold** | Preserve the uncommitted stack and avoid additional risk | Exact chips behind, payout schedule, remaining player stacks, tournament value of survival |
| **Flat-call** | Pay the additional **45,000** if the reported live wagers are exact, then play out of position | Button's opening range and later-play tendencies, effective stack, postflop model, tournament incentives |
| **Smaller reraise** | Apply pressure without necessarily committing everything immediately | Raise size, button's response frequencies, stack behind, whether another decision remains |
| **All-in reshove** | May win when the button folds, or face a calling range when the button continues | **Button opening range AND distinct call-versus-reshove range**, fold frequency, equity when called, correct contribution ledger and ICM objective |

**The button's opening range is not its calling range.** For example, a button might open many hands yet continue against an all-in raise with only a subset. An aggressive *opening* description does not establish a wide *calling* range. The exact subset cannot be deduced from one observed showdown.

**Chip EV versus final-table prize value:** A chip-maximizing reshove need not maximize expected prize money when four players remain and payouts differ by finishing position. Another short stack, exact payout jumps, coverage, deal terms and any bounty structure could alter the risk comparison. No payout figures, ICM risk premium, fold-equity percentage or solved range is supplied for this hand.

## 5. Later review, sensitivity checks and an honest stopping point

Compare alternative assumptions **one at a time** before reaching a conditional view:

1. **Button opens widely but calls tightly:** Would an all-in reshove win enough uncontested pots to offset its risk when called? No such frequencies have been measured here.
2. **Button opens and calls more widely:** Which additional hands continue, and how would they change the called branch? A speculative range is a scenario to test, not an observed fact.
3. **Small blind is materially shorter:** How would accurate four-handed stacks and payout jumps affect the tournament-prize-value ranking of fold, flat, smaller raise and shove?
4. **Hero's 585,000 already excludes some posted chips:** What changes if the actual effective stack differs from the conditional **19.5 BB** pre-posting conversion?
5. **Flat or smaller reraise:** Does the reference tool model these actions and later streets, or only a push/fold decision? Excluded branches must be stated explicitly.
