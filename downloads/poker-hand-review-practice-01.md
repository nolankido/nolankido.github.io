## The entire fictional case

- A six-handed table in a tournament with **14 players remaining, 10 paid**. The exact payouts, field stacks, bounty terms and deal terms have not been provided.
- Small blind **2,000**, big blind **4,000**, **big-blind ante 4,000**. The ante is a *separate, dead* contribution posted by the big blind. **No other antes, rake, side pots or previous voluntary wagers.**
- Hero is in the **big blind**, holding **Q♠ J♠**, with **80,000 chips at the start of the hand, before posting** either the live big blind or the ante.
- The **cutoff** starts the hand with **96,000 chips**, raises **to 10,000 total**, and has paid no forced blind or ante. All earlier players folded.
- The button folds. The small blind folds, leaving its posted **2,000** in the pot. Hero now faces the cutoff's raise.
- You observed the cutoff raise twice in the preceding six hands. You have no verified long-term opening or call-versus-reraise frequencies.
- One possible alternative is a big-blind reraise **to 24,000 total**. For an *arithmetic-only* branch, assume the cutoff then folds immediately. There is no showdown or runout in the case.

All unmentioned cards, future board cards, opponents' actual ranges and tournament-payout values are **unknown**. The cutoff's hypothetical fold to 24,000 is an assumption for one branch, not a prediction.

## 2. Action: rebuild the chip ledger

Without looking at the key, compute:

- Chips hero has **behind after posting** the live big blind and separate big-blind ante.
- The **pot before hero responds**, including all dead and live contributions.
- The **additional chips to call** the 10,000 raise-to amount, and the pot and chips behind **after a call**.
- If hero reraises **to 24,000 total**, how much additional money is placed in the pot from the hero's stack?
- If the cutoff folds to that reraise, what **uncalled portion is returned**, and what amount remains in the **awarded pot**? Separate the refund from the pot awarded to hero.

Write the arithmetic, not just the final totals.
