AI in real life / Inspect the work

What does practical AI look like in real work?

I use AI to help build, draft, and check work that has an actual purpose. Start with these three cases. One is a documented website repair; the others are bounded tests and editorial proposals. Each shows the human checks and what the evidence does not prove.

01 / Recorded website release

A passing test still missed a website defect.

The task: Improve a public website using AI-assisted edits. The human check: A separate HTML5 parser found an extra closing tag after the earlier checks passed. The defect was repaired before release. The limit: This documents one real repair, not measured productivity or a guarantee that AI code is safe.

02 / Small-business AI prototype

Keep a business owner in charge of AI-written replies.

The task: Draft a response to a customer reporting a duplicate charge. The human check: Compare the customer's words, the owner's policy, and the exact draft before approval. The limit: The customer and tests are fictional examples, not reported outcomes from a real cafe.

03 / Video fact-checking exercise

Turn a tournament hand into a defensible caption.

The task: Propose concise graphics from a published firsthand hand record. The human check: Recompute the chip figures and separate the advertised prize from the reported payout. The limit: These are proposed captions, not verified graphics in the current YouTube video or a solver verdict.

See the published video, companion, and source-to-screen evidence chain. The latest audiovisual cut still requires its own review.

A completed public AI project: RightsReceipt has a Devpost hackathon submission, open-source code and a demonstration video. It separates actual local AI inference from simulated payment responses and does not claim a completed real transaction.

What about results? A 25-turn website improvement record distinguishes verifiable releases from search traffic and AI citations that have not been measured. Publishing a tool is not the same as proving that it improved a business outcome.
