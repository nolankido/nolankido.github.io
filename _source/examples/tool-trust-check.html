Fictional example. A duration-summary tool claims to report the median of a nonempty list of nonnegative whole-minute observations. Invalid or missing observations should produce a clear error, not a silent substitution.

Independent expectation: for 2, 2, 2, 2, and 22 minutes, the sorted middle value is 2. This is worked out from the definition rather than a second call to the same tool.

Observed answer: the tool reports 6 as the median. Another implementation also reports 6. Both are computing the mean: 30 / 5 = 6. Their agreement does not repair the shared misunderstanding.

Action: stop relying on the median label until the calculation is repaired. Check an unsorted case and an even-length case too. For 2, 4, 6, and 8, the median is (4 + 6) / 2 = 5. Test empty input and invalid observations separately.

Conclusion after repair: successful checks support those tested behaviors. They do not prove all input handling, the representativeness of the observations, or the duration of a new task. The 22-minute observation may be important when making a commitment even though the median is 2.

Limit: this worksheet helps structure questions. It is not an independent audit, a mathematical proof, or permission to use the tool for a higher-consequence purpose.
