Fictional example. A learner wants a stronger mathematical foundation. The next capability is adding fractions with different denominators and explaining the result, not completing an entire course this week.

First attempt: 1/2 + 1/3 is answered as 2/5. The missing idea is that the pieces being added must have the same size.

Repair: divide a drawing into sixths. One half is three sixths and one third is two sixths, so the sum is 5/6. Close the explanation and try 1/4 + 1/2. Explain why the answer is 3/4.

Check: compare with an independently worked answer or a reliable explanation, including the reason for choosing a common denominator. A copied arrangement is not yet evidence that the idea transfers.

Later review: try 1/3 + 1/6 without the guide. The sum is 3/6, or 1/2. Record any help needed and the next misunderstanding to address.

Smaller-week option: explain one earlier example or deliberately pause, leaving “draw sixths and explain 1/2 + 1/3” as the next action. Do not spend the session finding a replacement tracking system.

Limit: these examples are a starting point. Correct answers here do not establish mastery of fractions, long-term retention, or readiness for every later topic. Adjust the next task to what the learner can explain.
