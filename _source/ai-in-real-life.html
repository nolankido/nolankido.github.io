What does using AI in real life actually look like?

I use AI to build software, check source material, and prepare work that still needs a human decision. These examples are not interchangeable: a repaired live website is evidence of actual delivery, while a fictional customer reply, proposed video graphic, and cooking simulation prove narrower things. Follow the public records and judge the work for yourself.

My rule: AI Works, Human Decides. Define the task, check the result against independent evidence, and keep approval, permissions, and consequences with people.

Method / Measure the real task

What would it take to prove AI actually helped?

Measure preparation, generation, checking, repair, maintenance, and human approval. The first successful output is not the whole task, and an estimate is not an observation.

  1. Define the task and permitted inputs. Create an AI task brief.
  2. Preserve a credible non-AI baseline. Use a controlled workflow pilot.
  3. Verify important claims and numbers. Apply the source and arithmetic checks.
  4. Count human review and rework. Compare full workflow time.
  5. State the evidence level. Distinguish simulation, repeatable prototype, public release, repeated real-world use, and independently measured impact.

Transparency / Scope

Evidence before expertise claims.

These are selected public artifacts, not a professional certification. The website repairs document real delivered work. The customer-reply and cooking examples remain prototypes or simulations. The publishing and creator examples show review methods with missing evidence stated openly. None is offered as a measured client return on investment.

The next useful evidence would be repeated use of a specific workflow with a comparable baseline, quality checks, and observed costs. Until then, I will label the limits instead of inventing results.
