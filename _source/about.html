My interests connect technology, decision-making, and creative work. I am drawn to the point where an unfamiliar idea becomes understandable, a rough version becomes usable, or a good question turns out to need a different answer.

Building and writing are two ways of exploring that. A tool has to meet the demands of an actual task. An explanation has to make sense to someone who does not already share its assumptions. Both give an idea somewhere to be tested beyond the moment when it first sounds convincing.

Careful does not have to mean complicated.

I am interested in practical AI and useful software, but the newest tool is not automatically the right one. A small, clear solution can be more valuable than an impressive system that adds another thing to manage.

The same goes for learning. I like difficult questions, but I do not think the basics are something to hide. There is a difference between following an explanation and being able to work something out without it.

There is a creative side to this, too.

Writing, design, and visual storytelling offer different ways to make an idea land. I am interested in the small choices: what to leave out, where to slow down, and when an example can do more than another paragraph of explanation.

Not every interest needs to become a project or a public achievement. Some things are worth exploring for their own sake. A useful life has room for curiosity, other people, and time that does not need to prove its productivity.
