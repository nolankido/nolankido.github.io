AI is part of how I build, write, and explore ideas. What interests me is not just what a tool can produce, but whether the result helps with something that matters. I want technology to make more room for life, not give every part of it another dashboard.

That is the connection between the different parts of this site. Practical technology, thoughtful living, tournament poker, and creative work ask different questions, but all involve attention, judgment, and the willingness to learn.

What matters to me in the work

Usefulness: start with a real purpose, not a demonstration in search of one. Clarity: give the reader enough context to understand the choice. Judgment: be willing to question a polished result, change direction, or stop when the work has done its job.

Those are priorities, not a claim that every attempt succeeds. Let AI do more. Keep the judgment. develops the distinction behind them.

Wine, games, and the rest of life

I'm learning about wine and building games, alongside food, travel, and other unfamiliar things. A wine note starts with what I actually taste; a game needs playtesting. AI might help organize observations or suggest questions, but it cannot supply the experience or make the judgment for me.

For wine, I've published a practical guide to writing tasting notes without guessing the bottle, with a free blank worksheet and a clearly fictional example. It separates what someone notices from a grape or production-method inference. The guide is a learning resource, not a personal tasting log or a claim to certification.

These are personal interests, not claims to wine credentials, public game releases, or verified AI outcomes. I share examples when there is something readers can inspect. Some work and much of ordinary life belong outside a public website.

Three things you can inspect directly

The best introduction is a small set of public examples rather than an impressive-sounding list of claims. These three starting points do different jobs: one is a tool, one is a recorded release, and one explains the judgment behind the work.

The practical method: AI Works, Human Decides means choosing the right task, bounding AI's role, checking the result independently, and leaving the final call with a person. The examples below let you inspect parts of that process. They do not establish a universal success rate.

Use a tool: The AI task brief builder turns your own inputs into a structured brief with checks and boundaries. There is no AI call. You can inspect how it works and what it cannot do. It is not a measured productivity result.

Inspect a recorded case: Follow an AI-assisted website repair, including the defect, the separate checks and their limits. For a smaller instructional contrast, try an independent-checking example. The recorded release is evidence about one defect, not a claim that every AI-assisted change is safe.
