Strongest to weakest

Read down the list. A royal flush is the highest straight flush, not a separate mathematical hand category. These are standard hold'em rankings, not short-deck or lowball rankings.

  1. Royal flush

    The ace-high straight flush, the highest possible hand.

    A♠K♠Q♠J♠10♠

  2. Straight flush

    Five consecutive ranks in one suit.

    9♥8♥7♥6♥5♥

  3. Four of a kind

    Four equal ranks plus one side card.

    Q♣Q♦Q♥Q♠2♦

  4. Full house

    Three of one rank and two of another.

    J♥J♦J♠4♣4♦

  5. Flush

    Five cards of one suit, not consecutive.

    A♣J♣8♣5♣2♣

  6. Straight

    Five consecutive ranks, not all one suit.

    9♠8♦7♥6♣5♠

  7. Three of a kind

    Three equal ranks and two unmatched side cards.

    8♠8♥8♦K♣3♠

  8. Two pair

    Two different pairs and a side card.

    K♠K♥5♣5♦2♠

  9. One pair

    One pair and three unmatched side cards.

    10♠10♥A♣7♦3♣

  10. High card

    None of the combinations above.

    A♠J♦9♣6♥3♠

Compare only the best five cards

Compare categories first. If equal, compare defining ranks, then kickers highest first. A full house compares three of a kind before its pair; two pair compares the higher pair, lower pair, then kicker. A flush compares its five cards from highest to lowest.

Only the best five count. A sixth or seventh card, or a suit, cannot break a tie. Identical five-card ranks share the relevant pot; venue rules resolve indivisible odd chips.

A-2-3-4-5 is a five-high straight; Q-K-A-2-3 is not. Four cards of one suit are a flush draw, not a made flush.

Can you beat a straight on the board? Board: 6♠ 7♥ 8♦ 9♣ 10♠. Player A: J♦ 2♣. Player B: A♣ K♦. Who wins? If B replaces A♣ with J♣, who wins then?

Reveal both best-five answers

First: A wins. A plays 7♥-8♦-9♣-10♠-J♦, a jack-high straight. B plays 6♠-7♥-8♦-9♣-10♠, a ten-high straight. Holding an ace does not extend this board straight. After B receives J♣: tie. Both make jack-high straights, and suits cannot decide the winner. A straight on the board can be improved with a hole card.

Four fictional showdowns

Choose the best five before opening the answer. Every board is complete.

The kicker counts

Player A: A♠Q♠

Player B: Q♥J♥

Board: Q♦7♣2♠9♥3♦

Reveal the winner: The kicker counts

Player A's best five

Q♠Q♦A♠9♥7♣

Player B's best five

Q♥Q♦J♥9♥7♣

A wins: Q-Q-A-9-7 beats Q-Q-J-9-7. The pair is equal; the first different kicker decides.

The board supplies everything

Player A: 7♥6♦

Player B: 5♠4♣

Board: A♥A♦K♣K♦Q♠

Reveal the winner: The board supplies everything

Player A's best five

A♥A♦K♣K♦Q♠

Player B's best five

A♥A♦K♣K♦Q♠

Tie. Both play A-A-K-K-Q from the board. None of their hole cards improves those five, and there is no sixth-card tiebreaker.

Full house versus full house

Player A: A♠2♦

Player B: K♠Q♥

Board: A♥A♦K♣K♦3♠

Reveal the winner: Full house versus full house

Player A's best five

A♠A♥A♦K♣K♦

Player B's best five

K♠K♣K♦A♥A♦

A wins with aces full of kings, A-A-A-K-K. B has kings full of aces, K-K-K-A-A. Compare the three matching cards before comparing the pair.

The ace is low this time

Player A: A♠2♦

Player B: 6♣7♣

Board: 3♠4♥5♦K♦Q♥

Reveal the winner: The ace is low this time

Player A's best five

A♠2♦3♠4♥5♦

Player B's best five

3♠4♥5♦6♣7♣

B wins with a seven-high straight, 3-4-5-6-7. A has the five-high wheel, A-2-3-4-5. The ace is functioning as a one, not a high-card tiebreaker.

Build one different hand yourself.

Before revealing each showdown, name both best five-card hands and the first rank that decides the winner or tie. The physical cards can differ while the hand values match.

Practice choosing the best five cards in ten changed hands from the free course. Use a deck or paper if interactive practice is unavailable.

For action context, try three paused fictional hands. If stuck, revisit kickers and ties and record one correction on the blank private study card.

During an unfinished hand, the leader can change when another board card arrives. A ranking chart answers who wins with the cards available; it does not by itself tell you who should bet or call.
