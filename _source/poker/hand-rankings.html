Strongest to weakest

Read down the list. A royal flush is the highest straight flush, not a separate mathematical hand category. These are standard hold'em rankings, not short-deck or lowball rankings.

  1. Royal flush

    The ace-high straight flush, the highest possible hand.

    A♠K♠Q♠J♠10♠

  2. Straight flush

    Five consecutive ranks in one suit.

    9♥8♥7♥6♥5♥

  3. Four of a kind

    Four equal ranks plus one side card.

    Q♣Q♦Q♥Q♠2♦

  4. Full house

    Three of one rank and two of another.

    J♥J♦J♠4♣4♦

  5. Flush

    Five cards of one suit, not consecutive.

    A♣J♣8♣5♣2♣

  6. Straight

    Five consecutive ranks, not all one suit.

    9♠8♦7♥6♣5♠

  7. Three of a kind

    Three equal ranks and two unmatched side cards.

    8♠8♥8♦K♣3♠

  8. Two pair

    Two different pairs and a side card.

    K♠K♥5♣5♦2♠

  9. One pair

    One pair and three unmatched side cards.

    10♠10♥A♣7♦3♣

  10. High card

    None of the combinations above.

    A♠J♦9♣6♥3♠

Compare only the best five cards

First compare the category. Within the same category compare the defining ranks, then any kickers from highest to lowest. A full house compares its three-of-a-kind rank first. Two pair compares the higher pair, then the lower pair, then the fifth card. A flush compares all five cards in descending order until they differ.

A sixth or seventh card cannot break a tie. Suits do not rank hands: a spade flush does not beat a heart flush merely because it is spades. If both players' best five-card hands have equal ranks, they share the relevant pot; an indivisible odd chip follows the event's procedure.

An ace can sit above a king or below a two in a straight. A-2-3-4-5 is five-high; Q-K-A-2-3 is not a straight. Four matching suits are a draw, not a completed flush.

Four fictional showdowns

Choose the best five before opening the answer. Every board is complete.

The kicker counts

Player A: A♠Q♠

Player B: Q♥J♥

Board: Q♦7♣2♠9♥3♦

Reveal the winner: The kicker counts

A wins: Q-Q-A-9-7 beats Q-Q-J-9-7. The pair is equal; the first different kicker decides.

The board supplies everything

Player A: 7♥6♦

Player B: 5♠4♣

Board: A♥A♦K♣K♦Q♠

Reveal the winner: The board supplies everything

Tie. Both play A-A-K-K-Q from the board. None of their hole cards improves those five, and there is no sixth-card tiebreaker.

Full house versus full house

Player A: A♠2♦

Player B: K♠Q♥

Board: A♥A♦K♣K♦3♠

Reveal the winner: Full house versus full house

A wins with aces full of kings, A-A-A-K-K. B has kings full of aces, K-K-K-A-A. Compare the three matching cards before comparing the pair.

The ace is low this time

Player A: A♠2♦

Player B: 6♣7♣

Board: 3♠4♥5♦K♦Q♥

Reveal the winner: The ace is low this time

B wins with a seven-high straight, 3-4-5-6-7. A has the five-high wheel, A-2-3-4-5. The ace is functioning as a one, not a high-card tiebreaker.

During an unfinished hand, the leader can change when another board card arrives. A ranking chart answers who wins with the cards available; it does not by itself tell you who should bet or call.
