Five ordinary actions

Check adds nothing when no wager needs matching. Bet opens the wagering on a street. Call matches what is outstanding, limited by the caller's available chips. Raise increases the wager. Fold gives up the hand and its claim to the pot; earlier contributions stay in.

Checking and folding are different. A check may lead to another turn to act if someone bets later. A check-raise means checking, facing a bet, and raising when action comes back.

“To” is a total; “by” is an increment

Fictional flop: 1,200 in the pot

A bets 400. B raises to 1,400. B adds 1,400; A now faces another 1,000. Before A responds, the pot is 3,000 = 1,200 + 400 + 1,400. If A calls, it becomes 4,000.

“Raises by 1,400” would instead mean a new total of 1,800 after a 400 bet. Never silently interchange the two descriptions.

Under the standard no-limit full-raise rule, the minimum new increment matches the last full bet or raise. With blinds 100/200, an open to 500 increases the live wager by 300, so the next minimum full raise is to 800. That is a three-bet, not a requirement to multiply 500 by three.

A short all-in is a special case. If A bets 1,000, B calls, and C can move all-in only to 1,500, the extra 500 is less than a full raise. In this simple sequence it does not reopen raising to A or B, who already acted. Multiple short all-ins can change that result. TDA rules 45 and 49 give the full procedure; the tournament staff resolve live ambiguities.

More chips do not automatically win the hand

Table stakes limit play to the chips available for that hand. A player who cannot cover the full wager may call all-in for less and remain eligible for the matched portion. The excess contested by other players belongs in a side pot. An amount nobody can match is returned, not won.

The main pot is 3,000 = 1,000 × 3. The side pot between B and C is 3,000 = (2,500 − 1,000) × 2. C receives the unmatched 1,500 back. Total accounting: 3,000 + 3,000 + 1,500 = 7,500, equal to the amounts put forward.

A could hold the strongest hand and win the main pot while B, with the next strongest hand, wins the side pot. C's refund is separate from either award. A cannot win the side pot regardless of hand strength.

Try the numbers

A has already bet 600 and faces a raise to 2,000. How much more is a call?

1,400. The 600 is already part of A's wager on that street. Do not count it a second time.

If the shortest player wins, do they take all 7,500 in the side-pot example?

No. A can win the 3,000 main pot. The 3,000 side pot is decided between B and C; the unmatched 1,500 is returned to C.

Can a player check when facing an outstanding bet?

Not as a normal way of staying in the hand. The ordinary choices are call, raise when permitted, or fold. A check is available only when nothing needs to be called.

A “pot” number on a graphic may include the latest bet or show the pot before it. Follow the caption and action sequence rather than inferring the convention from the size alone.
