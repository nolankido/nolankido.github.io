Rules notes

Wait for your turn. The comic’s “all-in at any time” is shorthand. You act only on your turn, and an all-in raise is subject to whether the betting has reopened. No-limit does not remove the betting rules.

Blind amounts vary. The big blind is commonly twice the small blind, but use the actual posted structure. With only two players seated, the button posts the small blind and acts first preflop.

The drawings are illustrative. Read the captions and explanation for the sequence. Community cards arrive after preflop betting, even though the final panel already shows cards on the table.

Text version

This accessible text version follows the six panels and includes the rules clarifications above.

  1. Welcome to the table

    A dealer introduces no-limit Texas Hold’em. This first comic covers setting up a hand and reaching the opening betting round.

  2. A standard deck, no jokers

    The game uses 52 cards. At showdown, compare the best five-card hands made from each player’s two hole cards and the five shared community cards. You may use both, one, or neither of your hole cards. You can also win earlier if every opponent folds.

  3. The dealer button and blinds

    The button marks the dealer position and normally moves clockwise between hands. With three or more players, the next two positions clockwise post the small blind and big blind before the deal. These forced bets start the pot. Live games can use a dead-button procedure when seats become empty.

  4. Post the stated amounts

    The small blind and big blind follow the game’s announced structure. A common example is 100/200: the small blind posts 100 chips and the big blind posts 200. Some games also have an ante, which is separate from the blinds.

  5. Two private hole cards

    Each player receives two cards face down. Keep them private while the hand is in progress. This stage, before the community cards, is called preflop.

  6. The first betting round

    With three or more players, preflop action begins with the first player clockwise after the big blind. On your turn, you may fold, call the amount you face using your available chips, or make a legal raise. If everyone only calls, the big blind may check or raise. Going all-in means committing your remaining chips, subject to the action and betting rules.

What comes next?

The flop brings three community cards, followed by the turn and river, with betting rounds in between when players can still bet. Follow a complete counted hand to see the sequence. For raises and all-ins, use the betting and pots guide.
