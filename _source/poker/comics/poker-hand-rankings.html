Before using this as a reference

The hand categories are in the correct order, but some AI-drawn card faces have inconsistent ranks or suits. Use the written definitions below as your reference.

A royal flush is the highest straight flush. Aces can be high or low in a straight: A-K-Q-J-10 or 5-4-3-2-A. They cannot wrap around in Q-K-A-2-3.

At a Hold’em showdown, use your best five cards from the seven available. You can use both, one or neither of your hole cards. Suits never break a tie; equal five-card hands share the pot.

The rankings, strongest to weakest

This text companion explains each category. The list follows standard high-hand poker, as used in full-deck Texas Hold’em.

  1. Royal flush

    A-K-Q-J-10, all in one suit.

  2. Straight flush

    Five consecutive ranks in one suit.

  3. Four of a kind

    Four matching ranks and a fifth card.

  4. Full house

    Three matching ranks plus a pair.

  5. Flush

    Five cards of one suit, without consecutive ranks.

  6. Straight

    Five consecutive ranks, not all one suit.

  7. Three of a kind

    Three matching ranks and two different side cards.

  8. Two pair

    Two distinct pairs and a fifth card.

  9. One pair

    One pair and three different side cards.

  10. High card

    No pair, straight or flush. Compare highest cards first.

The category comes first

A flush beats a straight. Within a category, compare its key ranks before side cards: for example, compare a full house’s three-of-a-kind rank before its pair. Kickers count only within the best five cards.

The poster’s “A Simple Pair can still win!” is a useful reminder: you do not need a spectacular hand to win a pot. You can also win before showdown when every opponent folds.
