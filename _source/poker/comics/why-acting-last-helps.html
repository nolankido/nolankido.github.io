The button acts last after the flop

On the flop, turn and river, action begins with the first player still able to act clockwise from the dealer button. If the button is still in the hand and has chips to act with, that player acts after the others.

Before the flop, the blinds come after the button in the initial action order at a table with three or more players. If the entire game is heads-up, the button posts the small blind and acts first preflop, then last after the flop. A raise can bring the action back around; acting last in position does not mean nobody can respond to your bet.

A check does not prove weakness, and a bet does not prove strength. Players can check strong hands or bet as a bluff. Treat their actions as clues to consider alongside the board, bet size and previous action.

The comic in words

  1. Sam has to go first

    In early position, Sam feels as though he is playing in the dark. He must decide before seeing what the players behind him will do.

  2. A different view from the button

    After the flop, Sam gets to act last on the button. He compares it to being the last person to take a test, after seeing everyone else’s answers.

  3. Clues, not secret cards

    Sam cannot peek at anyone’s hole cards. But he watches one player check and another bet, giving him information he did not have when acting first.

  4. A better-informed decision

    Sam weighs those actions before choosing his own. The closing lesson: position does not guarantee a win, but acting later gives you more information.

What can you do with that information?

Suppose you are on the button and two opponents check to you on the flop. You can check behind to end that betting round without adding chips, or bet. If an opponent bets before your turn, you must instead decide whether to fold, call or raise. You cannot check when facing an outstanding bet.
