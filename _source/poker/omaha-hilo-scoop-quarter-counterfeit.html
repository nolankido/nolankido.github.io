1. High and low are two separate legal constructions

In four-card Omaha hi-lo, your best high uses exactly two private cards plus three board cards. Your best low independently uses exactly two private cards plus three board cards. The chosen private pair and board triple may differ between high and low. A low qualifies only if its five ranks are all different and all eight or lower, counting an ace as one. For low, a straight or flush is not a penalty. Compare low hands from their highest rank downward: 7-5-3-2-A beats 7-5-4-3-A because the third rank, three, is lower than four.

The PokerStars official Omaha hi-lo rules confirm the two-plus-three restriction, separate selections and eight-or-better qualifier. The PokerStars hi-lo rules reference confirms that the wheel A-2-3-4-5 is the best qualifying low. The same five ranks can also make a five-high straight for high, so a low can be both a low winner and a high winner.

Write two answers before moving a chip

First, choose your strongest high-ranking legal five cards, without trying to preserve a particular low. Second, choose your lowest qualifying five different ranks, without assuming that high selection is fixed. Then compare both results with every known opponent. A qualifying low is an eligible hand; it is not automatically the winning low.

Three distinct board ranks of eight or below are necessary for any Omaha/8 low because every player must use three board cards. On 2♣ 2♦ 7♥ K♠ Q♠, there are only two distinct qualifying board ranks, 2 and 7. Nobody can make a qualifying low regardless of private cards. 3. Scoop: the same player wins both halves

Board: A♥ 2♦ 3♣ K♦ Q♠. A: 4♥ 5♥ A♣ A♦. B: 6♣ 7♣ 9♥ 9♦. All cards are unique.

A's best high is the five-high straight, made with 4♥ 5♥ plus A♥ 2♦ 3♣, even though A also holds two aces. A's best low is the same five-card wheel, 5-4-3-2-A. B's best high is a pair of nines and best low is 7-6-3-2-A. A therefore wins high and low: of a 240-chip pot, A receives 240; B receives 0. Do not describe A's high as trips aces here: the straight is stronger.

This is a closed-action showdown, not a recommendation to play four-five in a particular position. The Omaha and mixed-game overview explains why transferring hold'em preflop assumptions into split-pot games can cause mistakes.

4. No qualifying low: the whole pot belongs to high

Board: K♣ K♦ Q♥ J♥ 9♠. A: K♥ K♠ A♠ 2♠. B: A♥ 3♣ 4♣ 5♦. A makes four kings for high. There are zero board ranks eight or lower. Neither player can assemble three qualifying low board cards, whatever their private low cards. Thus there is no low half to divide, and A receives the entire 240-chip pot. A's private A-2 and B's low-looking A-3-4-5 do not create a qualifying low from that board.

Check the board first before declaring a low draw. Omaha high-only examples in the PLO wraps and redraws guide and nut-flush blocker guide have no low half by their game rules, even if the board contains small cards.

5. River counterfeiting: your low can survive but lose

Turn board: 3♣ 5♦ 7♥ K♣. A: A♠ 2♠ Q♦ J♦. B: A♥ 4♥ Q♥ J♠. All 12 known cards are face up for this calculation. A holds a better low on the turn: 7-5-3-2-A versus B's 7-5-4-3-A. A uses A♠ 2♠, and B uses A♥ 4♥, each with 3♣ 5♦ 7♥. This compares only the best low, not an unconditional pot award on the turn.

Now deal 2♥. A's original 7-5-3-2-A low still qualifies. B can instead choose A♥ 4♥ with board 2♥ 3♣ 5♦, making the 5-4-3-2-A wheel. B wins low. For high, B also makes a five-high straight using the same legal five cards, while A's best high is only a pair of twos. B scoops all 240 chips at this complete fictional showdown. A was not disqualified from low; A was overtaken.

The eight hole cards plus four turn cards expose 12 physical cards. Exactly 52 − 12 = 40 river cards remain; no unknown opponent ranges are involved. The downloadable program checks every river against independently constructed low hands.

6. An extra low card can rescue a tie, not guarantee a win

Make a separate fictional deal: replace A's Q♦ with 4♣, so A now holds A♠ 2♠ 4♣ J♦. Keep B, the turn board and the 2♥ river unchanged. The replacement card is not part of the first deal. A may now select A♠ 4♣ with 2♥ 3♣ 5♦ for the same wheel as B. In this particular runout the wheel is also each player's best high: both tie for high and tie for low. From 240 chips, A gets 120 and B gets 120.

This is a precisely limited example of counterfeit protection: an extra low card changes which two private cards can be selected after the river. It does not show that holding more low cards always preserves the best low, always makes a scoop, or makes a preflop hand profitable. 7. An after-play protocol that catches common errors

When a mixed-game rotation moves to stud, rebuild the high and low from five of seven personal cards instead. The separate Stud8 and razz workshop also demonstrates why a nine-low can win in razz but fail Stud8's qualifier.

  1. Name the game: four-card Omaha/8, Omaha high, Stud8 or something else. Qualifiers and hand-construction rules differ. The stud and lowball guide compares these precisely.
  2. Record exact suits: four private cards per revealed player and the complete board. Leave unknown rivals unknown. Do not silently assign a hand from a later outcome.
  3. Score high independently: try all 6 private pairs and up to 10 board triples on a five-card board, then retain the strongest legal five-card high.
  4. Score qualifying low independently: reject any repeated low rank or card above eight, then compare all valid lows from their top ranks.
  5. Assign the pot: if no low qualifies, distribute 100% to high. Otherwise allocate half to high and half to low, splitting ties within each half.
  6. Check exposure and scope: verify no duplicate physical card, pot conservation and, for future cards, how many deck cards remain. Real side pots, rake, house odd-chip rules and betting decisions need separate treatment.

If you have partial information rather than revealed holdings, this program does not calculate an opponent range or a game-theory line. Use it as a private post-session check, then consult official house or tournament rules for actual chip handling. The multiway pot workshop explains why all-in eligibility cannot be assumed when stacks differ.

8. Check your hand reading before looking at an answer

Can A use A♣ A♠ for high but 4♥ 5♥ for low in the quartered showdown?

Yes. The high and low hands are selected independently. Each uses exactly two of the same four private cards and exactly three board cards.

Why does B get only 75 chips from the 300-chip pot despite making a wheel?

B ties A for the 150-chip low half, so each receives 75 from that half. A alone wins the 150-chip high half. B does not receive a share of high.

Does a wheel lose low because it is also a five-high straight?

No. Straights and flushes do not penalize ace-to-five low. The same legal five cards can win high and low.

On K♣ K♦ Q♥ J♥ 9♠, can A-2 in your private hand create a qualifying low?

No. There are no qualifying low ranks on the board, much less three distinct low ranks. High wins the entire pot.

When 2♥ arrives in the first counterfeit deal, is A's low disqualified?

No. A still has 7-5-3-2-A using A♠ 2♠ and the 3♣ 5♦ 7♥ board triple. B's newly available wheel is simply a better low.

Does 3 / 40 mean B wins the entire 240-chip pot on only three rivers?

No. The 3 / 40 count describes which player wins the low half under one fully exposed turn. Determining each river's entire pot allocation also requires evaluating high.

What changes when A replaces Q♦ with 4♣ in the protected variation?

A can now use A♠ 4♣ plus river 2♥, board 3♣ and board 5♦ to make a wheel for low and a five-high straight for high. Both high and low tie in that new deal, yielding 120 chips each.

Can a side pot or odd-chip rule alter these published awards?

Yes. These cases explicitly assume one pot to which everyone is equally eligible, no fees and convenient even totals. Apply the relevant live house or tournament rules separately when stacks, eligibility or indivisible chips differ.
