Fixed KO, progressive KO and the bounty you have not won

You eliminate someone carrying an $80 bounty. How much money did you actually win? It might be $80, it might be $40, or you might have won no bounty at all. The answer depends on the prize rules, who won the pot containing that player's last chips, and whether the elimination happened. This workshop gives each of those questions its own ledger.

After-play study only. Every amount, stack, hypothetical chance and hand in this lesson is fictional. This is a source-backed explanation of common knockout structures with independently checked arithmetic, not a calculation of a real player's expected return, a solver range, an invitation to chase bounties or a tool to use during a tournament. Unless a case expressly says otherwise, 52-card no-limit Texas hold'em is assumed, each pot has no rake, and there are no ties. Actual tournament rules can specify different bounty splits, award stages, payout pools or special elimination procedures.

1. A bounty is a prize rule, not extra tournament chips

The word knockout names an elimination award in addition to the chips won in a pot. It does not tell you whether that award is fixed, grows during play or has an unknown value. A tournament can also attach a regular finishing-position prize pool to the bounty structure. Start by reading the tournament-format comparison and the actual event's prize terms.

These patterns do not define every organizer's events. A PKO may have a different immediate/progressive split; a progressive-total-KO may have no ordinary finishing-position pool; a mystery format may activate only after a stated threshold. An award labeled a ticket, prize credit or noncash entry also needs separate terms. Do not move money from one prize bucket into another merely because both appear on a tournament listing.

For this lesson, received cash means a knockout payment earned and paid under the hypothetical rules. Own bounty means a marker that another player might win from eliminating you later. The latter is not another payment deposited to your account today. It can matter to future tournament value, but that future value requires a model.

2. One $80 knockout, two very different ledgers

Reset the example for each independent row. You cover the opponent, win the pot containing their final chips outright, and eliminate them. The target's displayed bounty is exactly $80. There are no chops, side pots or other players in this first comparison. For the fixed-KO row, every player's bounty is stipulated to be the same fixed $80. For the PKO row, your pre-hand progressive bounty is $40 and the target's current progressive bounty is $80.

In the stipulated PKO, the target's $80 is divided into two exact $40 parts. One is transferred to your cash received tally, and one increases your own bounty from $40 to $80. The total redistributed from the defeated player's bounty is still $80, but it is false to say you received $80 cash or earned $40 cash twice. Your stack of tournament chips from winning the pot is a third, independent quantity.

The bounty on your head can ultimately be claimed by someone who eliminates you. Some specified PKO formats pay it to the tournament winner if that player survives all the way, but that is a future conditional award. You cannot responsibly book the added $40 as an unconditional personal cash receipt. Nor is it necessarily worth zero in a full tournament equity model. It simply belongs in a different column until the conditions for paying it are resolved.

3. Two progressive eliminations do not pay the displayed sum immediately

For a separate example, your own PKO bounty begins at $40. Two eligible opponents have current bounties of $80 and $60. You eliminate the $80 player first and the $60 player later in two distinct, completed hands. Both outcomes are stipulated outright, and each target's last chips are in a pot you alone win. The event again pays 50% immediately and carries 50% to your own bounty.

Both defeated targets carried a combined $140 of bounties. Exactly $70 was paid to you immediately; another $70 increased the bounty on your head from $40 to $110. That $110 on-head marker includes the $40 already attached to you before either knockout. It must not be added to the $70 payout as if the entire $180 were newly won cash.

If someone later eliminated you while your bounty still stood at $110 under these same 50/50 rules, they would receive $55 immediately and add $55 to their own bounty. That hypothetical next event does not retroactively pay you $55. Likewise, winning a regular finishing-position prize would be a separate entry, not proof that your progressive marker was already paid.

4. A side pot can decide which bounty was earned

The biggest pot is not automatically the pot that awards a particular bounty. In knockout rules that use the relevant pot principle, the eligible eliminator is the player who wins the pot containing the defeated player's final chips. A player who has not lost their last chips remains in the event and does not pay their bounty. PokerStars' official elimination-bounty explanation uses main and side pots to make precisely this distinction; check the rules adopted by your actual organizer.

Fully specified three-way no-limit hold'em fixture

The button X starts with 4 BB; you are the small blind with 14 BB; Y is the big blind with 9 BB. Blinds are 0.5/1 BB, with no ante, no previous voluntary bet and no rake. Before posting, the three stacks sum to 27 BB. X shoves to four total. You call to four total, adding 3.5 over your posted small blind. Y makes a legal full raise all-in to nine total, adding eight over the live big blind. You call five more, making your voluntary and blind contributions nine in total. X and Y are all-in; no further betting is possible, and you retain five behind.

Each of the three can contest exactly the first 4 BB each: the 12 BB main pot. Only you and Y contributed another 5 BB each: the 10 BB side pot. The five chips you never wagered remain yours. Conservation is 12 + 10 + 5 = 27 BB. Any bounty depends on who is eliminated in the relevant pot, not merely on seeing a side-pot graphic in a video.

The first row is a common source of bad video narration. You collected the side pot, yet X won the main pot and survived. You did not win X's $80 bounty. You instead eliminated Y, who was all-in for nine and lost the relevant side pot. A side pot can be the relevant pot for one player's elimination even while the shorter player wins the main pot. The second and third rows verify that the result must be recomputed when the hand rankings change.

Real hands may have unequal cards, ties, split pots, side-pot layering beyond one side pot, dead contributions, short blind posts or simultaneous eliminations. A platform can split a bounty among multiple relevant-pot winners under specified rules. This simple three-player example has no such complication. 5. Expected immediate bounty cash is only one component of a call

Imagine another independent, heads-up artificial example. You cover an $80-bounty opponent. The action is closed, no tie is possible and every win you record both wins the relevant pot and knocks them out; every loss earns no bounty. Let e be the entirely hypothetical 40% probability of that eligible elimination. We are calculating only the immediately payable bounty cash component, not a total action EV.

Cash expected from the bounty event alone

Fixed KO with an $80 immediate award: 0.40 × $80 = $32 in expected immediate bounty receipts. 50/50 PKO with an $80 displayed target: 0.40 × $40 = $16 expected immediate bounty cash. In the PKO there is also a 40% chance of adding $40 to your own bounty; the expected numerical addition to your displayed head bounty is $16, but it is not another $16 expected cash receipt. Future realization and payout treatment require a separate model.

Why is e not always the same as the probability of holding the best two cards? You must cover the opponent, win the relevant pot, and actually eliminate them. A split pot might prevent the knockout. A multiway hand can produce a side-pot win without eliminating the intended target. An event can also have a bounty stage that has not started. Thus, a conditional probability of eligible elimination can differ from a bare showdown-equity percentage.

When the probability changes, both immediate cash figures change linearly under this artificial branch. At zero eligible eliminations, immediate bounty expectation is zero. At certain elimination, the fixed $80 or PKO $40 would be paid immediately. No assumption about future prize payouts, target ranges or hand profitability has been smuggled into those figures.

6. Why the total tournament decision is still unsolved

None of these independent ledgers establishes whether calling, shoving or folding is best. A complete tournament equity comparison could require the change in ordinary finishing-position prize equity, immediate bounty receipts, future opportunity to collect other bounties, different payout treatment for your own progressive marker, other players' stack distribution, ties and the actual action tree. Correct accounting is necessary, but it is not itself a completed strategy solution.

HoldemResources' official bounty-model explanation separates bounty awards arising on the modeled hand from modeled estimates of future bounty collections; it expressly warns against combining dollar bounties with raw chip EV as though dollars were literal chips. Its proportional future-bounty estimate is a model assumption, not a guaranteed future cash-out. GTO Wizard's bounty-model notes similarly distinguish immediate from future knockout value. Neither source has solved this lesson's fictional cases.

The ordinary ICM primer values finishing-position prizes without a bounty. The equal-seat satellite example values qualification probability. The short-stack ICM call adds a legal blind-posted action. The same word equity points to different outcomes in each article. To transfer a result to another tournament, you must re-enter its real prize structure, ante, stacks, eligible bounties and action rights.

For mystery bounties, an advertised jackpot is especially poor shorthand. Before estimating an expected cash award, record whether the bounty phase is active, whether every eligible knockout earns a draw, how remaining draws are distributed, and any announced restrictions. Without those inputs, no sound numerical expectation can be given. Do not manufacture a random-prize distribution from the largest promotional number.

7. Eight checks to make the accounting automatic

In a fixed-KO event where every player carries a fixed $80 bounty, you eliminate one opponent outright. How much is paid immediately under these stated rules?

$80 cash. This is the target's whole fixed bounty. Your own fixed bounty remains $80. Chip winnings and ordinary finishing prizes are separate.

Your own PKO bounty is $40. You eliminate a player whose current bounty is $80 in a 50/50 PKO. How much is immediately received, and what is attached to you afterward?

$40 cash now and your displayed bounty becomes $80 ($40 prior + $40 added). The second $40 is not another cash receipt.

Starting with a $40 own bounty, you eliminate $80 and $60 PKO targets in succession. Is $140 immediately withdrawable under a 50/50 split?

No. Immediate receipts are $40 + $30 = $70; your own bounty rises to $110. That on-head marker is not $110 deposited to you.

Three players contribute four, nine and nine big blinds. How large are the main and side pots, and who can win each?

Main: 12 BB, contested by all three. Side: 10 BB, contested only by the two nine-BB contributors. The fourteen-BB player has another five uncommitted BB. Total chips: 12 + 10 + 5 = 27.

X wins the main pot; you beat Y for the side pot; Y is all-in and eliminated. Do you win X's bounty?

No. X survived by winning the main pot. You eliminate Y in the relevant side pot and may receive Y's bounty under this event's rules, not X's.

In the same three-way fixture, you win both main and side pots while X and Y are eliminated. Who receives their $80 and $60 PKO awards?

You eliminate both. Under the stipulated 50/50 PKO, their combined $140 creates $70 immediate cash and a $70 addition to your existing bounty.

An artificial eligible knockout happens 40% of the time. What immediate bounty cash expectation does an $80 fixed KO have versus a 50/50 PKO?

$32 fixed KO versus $16 PKO. The PKO also has a $16 expected addition to the displayed head bounty, not a second guaranteed cash award. Neither number is a full call EV.

A mystery-bounty event advertises a six-figure maximum. Can you use that maximum as the expected value of eliminating someone before confirming the payout stage and draw distribution?

No. Confirm the bounty phase, knockout eligibility, remaining award distribution and draw rules. A possible maximum is not an expectation or a prize earned by every knockout.

8. An after-play worksheet for any knockout hand

  1. Prize contract: record regular payout amounts, the bounty format, individual current bounties, the immediate/progressive split and whether the bounty stage was active.

  2. Starting state: note every relevant pre-posting stack, seats, positions, blinds, antes, who covers whom and any earlier contribution to the pot.

  3. Action and pots: log the actual raise-to amounts, call increments, uncalled refunds and main/side pot eligibility. Do not allocate a pot to a player with no claim to it.

  4. Eliminations: determine who lost their final chip, who won the relevant pot for each eliminated player, and what the tournament rules say about multiple winners or simultaneous busts.

  5. Three separate outputs: record chips after the hand, cash already received from bounties, and changes to any player's on-head bounty. Keep ordinary placement prizes in a fourth column.

  6. Model limits: label all uncertain hands, conditional knockout probabilities and unsolved decisions. Use source evidence and software outside play under the organizer's rules. Invite correction rather than filling a missing amount with a guess.

For a practical hand-review record, continue to the hand capture guide. For a venue or event decision before paying, use the tournament structure guide. These records can make a vlog more credible without presenting an invented bounty amount, outcome or solver-approved decision.
