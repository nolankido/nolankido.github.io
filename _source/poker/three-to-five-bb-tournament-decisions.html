Three to five big blinds: count the tournament decision before shoving

You have four big blinds in the cutoff. Everybody ahead of you folds. Should you move all-in, make a smaller raise or fold? The answer is not contained in the number four. Start with the 2.5 BB already in the pot, count which player could call and how much, then decide which assumptions a push/fold chart has left out.

After-play study only. Every chip amount, hand situation, response probability and potential action in this guide is fictional. All examples use ordinary 52-card no-limit Texas hold'em tournament rules, a nine-handed table, and a specified 0.5/1 BB blind structure with a single 1 BB big-blind ante paid in full. Stacks are stated before the hand's forced bets unless explicitly identified otherwise. Chip arithmetic is not a solved or recommended hand range. Real tournament payout, bounty, house, minimum-chip and assistance rules take priority. No gambling account or purchase is needed.

1. Specify the whole nine-handed state

Fictional tournament table, used unless a branch says otherwise

Nine seats are dealt in. The small blind posts 0.5 BB, the big blind posts 1 BB live and also contributes a 1 BB big-blind ante. All forced payments are made in full, no other player antes, and there are no straddles, fees, side pots or re-entries affecting this hand. The pot before voluntary action is 0.5 + 1 + 1 = 2.5 BB. Five earlier seats fold to the cutoff (CO). Only button (BTN), small blind (SB) and big blind (BB) remain to act. Cutoff has posted nothing and starts with either 3, 4 or 5 BB in the distinct examples below. Unless stated otherwise, each possible caller starts with at least 8 BB, so the cutoff is the effective all-in stack.

That single big-blind ante is collected once per hand, not once for each of nine players. A blind is a live preflop contribution toward a matching wager. The ante is dead money in the pot, not a credit toward a later call. This is why a player facing the same 4 BB shove from a different seat can owe a different amount. Do not confuse starting stack with chips left after posting. In this scene CO has all 4 BB available; a big blind who began with 4 BB would have fewer than 4 behind after posting.

Preflop action begins with the first active seat after the big blind, not the button. After the five earlier folds, CO may fold, call the 1 BB big blind (limp), make a legal raise or move all-in. A conventional minimum full open-raise is to 2 BB total over the 1 BB live big blind. Raising to 4 BB is legal with a 4 BB stack. This is a list of available actions, not a recommendation that any particular holding should take one.

The 10 to 20 BB short-stack guide teaches the broader chart and payout vocabulary. This lesson answers a narrower question with 3 to 5 BB pre-hand stacks and fully counted live calls. The chart-reading checklist remains important: a heads-up, no-ante or pre-posting chart cannot silently be substituted for this nine-handed big-blind-ante state.

2. Three, four and five BB create three different calls

For a controlled comparison, hold everything fixed except CO's starting stack. Everyone before CO folds; CO shoves; button and small blind fold; and the big blind alone considers a call. The big blind has already put 1 BB live in the pot, plus the separate 1 BB ante, and can cover any shove in this table. The big blind needs to match the cutoff's total wager using only live betting chips.

Every percentage is the additional call divided by the final eligible pot in this closed-action, no-tie, no-fee chip model. It is not the big blind's calculated equity against CO's unknown shoving range. Three BB makes the price mathematically lower, but does not tell us how frequently CO shoves or what a particular holding should call. If CO instead folds from the cutoff, CO has paid nothing this hand and retains the starting stack.

If every player behind CO folds to a 4 BB shove, the unmatched 4 BB bet is returned to CO and CO wins the existing 2.5 BB pot. CO's stack goes from 4 to 6.5 BB, a net gain of 2.5 BB. It is an error to label the temporary 6.5 BB pot after CO places the shove as 6.5 BB of profit on a fold. Count returned uncalled chips correctly before comparing gains.

3. The same four-BB shove has three different caller prices

Restore CO to 4 BB unposted. In each separate branch, all previous players fold, CO shoves four, exactly one of BTN, SB or BB calls, and the remaining eligible players fold. Everyone has enough starting chips to cover CO. These are three possible responses to the same initial action, not a three-way pot.

For the button call, both blinds fold. Their 0.5 BB live small blind, 1 BB live big blind and 1 BB big-blind ante all stay as previously contributed dead money in the final pot. For the small-blind call, the big blind folds and leaves both its blind and ante in the pot, while the small blind receives a 0.5 BB credit toward its total four. For the big-blind call, the small blind folds, and the big blind receives a 1 BB live credit but no call credit for the ante.

These differences are pot arithmetic, not opponent calling frequencies. The button, small blind and big blind have different remaining players and ranges when deciding; their best calling distributions do not follow simply from the three price fractions. If more than one player calls, stop using these single-caller rows, reconstruct who can win each pot and consider side pots and refunds where contributions differ.

4. Why fold equity plus showdown equity matters, without inventing a shove chart

Take the 4 BB CO shove and BB-only call branch. We can construct a two-response chip-EV teaching model relative to CO folding right now: with probability f, all remaining players fold and CO gains 2.5 BB; otherwise, BTN and SB fold and BB calls. Conditional on that call, CO wins outright with probability q or loses with probability 1 − q. There are no ties, rake, bounties or intermediate outcomes, and no future betting because CO is all-in.

When BB calls, CO wins a 9.5 BB pot and its net change from the prehand 4 BB stack is +5.5 BB if it wins, versus −4 BB if it loses. The expected incremental chips from shoving rather than folding at this decision are:

Conditional chip-EV identity, not a solver result

EV(shove − fold) = f × 2.5 + (1 − f) × [q × 5.5 − (1 − q) × 4].

If the assumed joint fold rate is 30% and the conditional chance to beat BB's calling hand distribution is 40%, the result is 0.30 × 2.5 + 0.70 × (0.40 × 5.5 − 0.60 × 4) = +0.61 BB. Change only the assumed conditional showdown win chance to 30%, and the result becomes 0.30 × 2.5 + 0.70 × (0.30 × 5.5 − 0.70 × 4) = −0.055 BB. Neither f nor q was measured; these deliberately invented inputs show sensitivity.

A simpler pure zero-showdown-equity model would demand 4 / (4 + 2.5) ≈ 61.54% joint folds to break even on this shove. The same 4 BB risk with no ante would instead be 4 / (4 + 1.5) ≈ 72.73%. These two fractions isolate the presence of the ante; they do not establish that an actual 4 BB shoving hand has no equity when called, or that the opponents fold at either rate. A tournament decision normally requires the real ranges that produce f and q, plus any prize effects.

Do not treat the four-BB shove as an automatic fold-equity guarantee. There are three players behind, each with a decision, and their actions are not interchangeable independent coin flips. The model's conditional BB-only call is a teaching simplification. The combination workshop explains why possible hands and their weights must be kept separate. A public hand-history example would need actual action and source-backed ranges, not just a shoving percentage.

5. A four-BB stack still has non-all-in actions

Return to an unopened CO with 4 BB. The live bet to match is 1 BB. A limp to 1 BB, a minimum full raise to 2 BB total, and an all-in raise to 4 BB are legal actions in the stated ordinary rules; folding is also available. A push/fold chart may model only fold or shove. That does not make the other actions illegal or establish their strategic value.

Consider only CO's raise to 2 BB. It leaves 2 BB in CO's stack and builds a 2.5 + 2 = 4.5 BB pot before opponents respond. Button and small blind fold. If big blind just calls, its already-live 1 BB reduces its additional call to 1 BB. The resulting flop pot is 4.5 + 1 = 5.5 BB, CO retains 2 BB and BB retains its own remaining stack. Neither is necessarily all-in, and another betting round may happen. A 5.5 BB flop pot does not purchase a guaranteed showdown.

Alternatively, big blind can legally re-raise to 4 BB total, contributing 3 additional BB from the posted live blind. The pot when action returns to CO is 4.5 + 3 = 7.5 BB. CO already invested 2 this street and owes 4 − 2 = 2 BB to call all-in. If CO calls, the final pot is 7.5 + 2 = 9.5 BB and CO has zero chips behind. If CO folds instead, its 2 BB open stays lost; the uncalled excess of BB's raise is returned as the pot is awarded. A legal re-raise of 4 is not evidence that BB's hand is strong or weak.

Now the comparison is real: the all-in open and the 2 BB open create different response trees. They may also induce different opponent ranges. A 2 BB open followed by a fold is not mechanically equivalent to committing 4 BB. We have supplied no values for those missing branches, so the guide cannot decide between them. Even at three BB, smaller raises may remain legal while leaving very little behind; “short” does not automatically remove them from the legal action set.

6. A short big blind may contest less than the original shove

This is a new independent setup, not the covering-BB branch used above. CO again has 4 BB and shoves first in after earlier folds. BTN and SB fold. The big blind instead starts with 3 BB total before posting, pays the complete 1 BB ante plus its 1 BB live blind, and has just 1 BB behind. The initial pot is still 2.5 BB because both forced contributions were paid in full. CO covers this BB after the ante.

BB can call for its last 1 BB, bringing its live contribution to 2 BB total. CO's 4 BB shove now exceeds what BB can match by 2 BB. With no other active caller, that unmatched excess returns to CO. The contested pot is CO 2 + BB 2 + SB 0.5 + BB ante 1 = 5.5 BB. This is neither the 9.5 BB pot in the covering-BB branch nor 7.5 BB from incorrectly adding BB's last chip to CO's full unmatched shove.

With no further betting, fees or ties, BB's extra 1 BB call would require 1 / 5.5 ≈ 18.18% average contested-pot share to break even in chips. BB has no chips left after paying. CO receives the unmatched 2 BB back and retains that money outside this contested pot. This example deliberately avoids a partial blind or partial ante: a shorter BB unable to pay both forced obligations in full requires the operator's precise posting priority and eligibility rules before any pot calculation is trusted.

Here, “effective stack is three BB” is dangerously imprecise. BB started with three, spent one on a dead ante and could contest only two live betting chips; CO started with four but could commit only two against this sole caller. The all-in and eligibility facts matter more than either person's original round-number stack label.

7. A five-BB rejam and a three-BB short raise are different legal actions

Full rejam case, new branch: nine-handed, same 2.5 BB starting pot, all earlier positions fold to hijack (HJ). HJ has 8 BB before action and opens to 2 BB total. CO holds 5 BB unposted and announces all-in to 5 BB total. BTN, SB and BB fold; HJ alone decides. After HJ's open the pot is 4.5 BB; after CO's shove it is 2.5 + 2 + 5 = 9.5 BB. HJ has already put in 2 and owes 5 − 2 = 3 BB to call. A call makes 9.5 + 3 = 12.5 BB, with CO all-in and HJ retaining 3 BB behind. The simplified no-tie chip share needed to call is 3 / 12.5 = 24%.

CO's increase from HJ's 2 BB to 5 BB is a full raise: the ordinary minimum re-raise would be to 3 BB total, because HJ's open increased the live bet from 1 to 2 by 1 BB. If HJ folds to CO's shove, the unmatched 3 BB from CO comes back, and the contested pot is 2.5 + 2 + 2 = 6.5 BB. HJ's fold and HJ's call are alternative outcomes. The 24% call price cannot establish HJ's equity against CO's actual rejam range.

Short all-in case, separate branch: reset the pot to the same 2.5 BB. HJ opens to 2.5 BB total; CO now starts with 3 BB and moves all-in to 3 BB total. That is only a 0.5 BB increase over HJ's wager. The ordinary full minimum raise would require a raise to 4 BB total because HJ raised from the 1 BB blind to 2.5, an increase of 1.5 BB. The 3 BB all-in is legal as a short all-in but does not constitute a full raise.

If everyone else folds, the pot facing HJ is 2.5 + 2.5 + 3 = 8 BB. HJ needs 3 − 2.5 = 0.5 BB to call, producing a contested 8.5 BB pot and a no-tie direct call price of 0.5 / 8.5 ≈ 5.88%. Under Poker TDA's 2026 Rule 49, this single less-than-full all-in raise does not reopen betting for HJ, who had already opened, if no intervening full raise occurred. HJ can call the additional half blind or fold in this branch, but cannot re-raise merely because CO put in an extra half blind. A later full raise by another player would require a fresh analysis.

The short all-in price is tiny because the previous opener has already invested 2.5 BB and has to match only another half. The action restriction is about betting rights, not a forecast that the opener should call every two cards. A floor ruling and the event's adopted rules govern actual play. The rules reference explains why an operator's own procedures must be checked even when a TDA recommendation exists.

9. Eight quick checks with answers hidden until you choose

Nine-handed, a 1 BB big-blind ante, 0.5/1 blinds and no other antes. How much is in the pot before voluntary action?

2.5 BB. The SB contributes 0.5, BB contributes 1 live and the one BB pays 1 as an ante. It is not a 9 BB ante total. The full BB ante is dead money, not a live matching wager.

CO has 3 BB and shoves first in; BTN and SB fold, the covering BB calls. What additional amount does BB pay, and how big is the final pot?

2 BB more; 7.5 BB total. The BB's existing 1 BB live blind counts toward the three, but its 1 BB ante does not. The pot starts at 2.5, rises to 5.5 after CO's 3, then to 7.5 after BB's 2. A simplified closed-action no-tie call price is 2 / 7.5 ≈ 26.67%.

CO shoves 4 BB, and only SB calls. Does SB owe 4 BB or 3.5 BB, and what is the resulting pot?

SB owes 3.5 BB and the final pot is 10 BB. SB's posted live half blind counts toward the four. The BB folds and leaves its 1 BB live blind and 1 BB ante in the pot. The final amount is 2.5 + CO 4 + SB's additional 3.5.

Everybody folds to CO's 4 BB all-in. Does CO profit by 6.5 BB because the pot visibly contained 6.5 after the shove?

No. The 4 BB unmatched bet returns to CO. CO wins the original 2.5 BB forced pot, moving from a 4 BB starting stack to 6.5 BB, for a net gain of 2.5.

CO opens to 2 BB from a 4 BB stack and the covering BB three-bets to 4 BB total. What does CO owe to call, and does the call reach showdown?

CO owes 2 BB, then is all-in. BB added 3 BB beyond its 1 BB live blind, so the current pot is 7.5. CO's extra 2 makes 9.5. If only BB remains in the pot, no later betting action is possible against CO, and the remaining community cards are dealt to showdown. A fold would instead lose the 2 BB open.

BB started with 3 BB, posted the full 1 BB ante and 1 BB blind, then called CO's 4 BB shove with its last 1 BB. Is the eligible final pot 9.5 BB?

No: it is 5.5 BB. BB has only 2 BB total live contribution available after the ante, so CO's unmatched 2 BB returns. The contested pot is CO 2 + BB 2 + SB 0.5 + dead ante 1. BB's extra-call price is 1 / 5.5 ≈ 18.18% in this no-tie model.

HJ opens to 2 BB and CO rejams 5 BB, with all others folding. How much more does HJ call, and what is the contested pot?

3 BB more; 12.5 BB after calling. The pot was 2.5, HJ added 2, CO 5, and HJ adds 3 to match. The closed-action no-tie call share is 3 / 12.5 = 24%. CO's shove is a full raise in this branch.

HJ opens to 2.5 BB and CO moves all-in for 3 BB total, everyone else folds. Is HJ's next re-raise available just because CO added 0.5 BB?

No. A full minimum re-raise would be to 4 BB. CO's 3 BB shove is a legal short all-in, but under TDA Rule 49 it does not reopen action for HJ when no other full raise occurs. HJ owes 0.5 to call into an 8.5 BB final pot. This rule does not estimate any hand equity.

10. A six-line record that allows an honest short-stack review

  1. Pre-hand stack: write the actual chip counts and the blind unit; record any already-posted live blind, ante and chips remaining separately.

  2. People and money: note seats dealt in, button, who folded, initial dead money, other covering stacks and any short all-ins with unmatched chips.

  3. Action legality: distinguish a 2 BB total open from adding 2 more, calculate the minimum full raise, and mark any short all-in that does not reopen the betting.

  4. Independent responses: reconstruct exactly one player calling, multiple players calling, everyone folding and any uncalled refunds as different branches.

  5. Model objective: identify whether a chart solves chip EV, ICM, PKO or seat equity, which actions it permits and what ante and stack conventions it uses.

  6. Uncertainties and source: mark opponent ranges, payouts, table assumptions and any missing observation as estimates. Keep the record for review away from play, not as an at-table aid.

The goal is a reproducible decision, not a claim that shoving your last chips always works or that a cheap call is automatically correct. The 10 to 20 BB ICM call shows how a known chip price can conflict with a specified prize objective.
