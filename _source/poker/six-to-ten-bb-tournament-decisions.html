Six to ten big blinds: the decisions between shove and small raise

You have eight big blinds in a nine-handed tournament. Action folds to you in the cutoff. You can move all-in, but a raise to two blinds is also legal and leaves six behind. Those two actions expose you to different calls, re-raises and flops. The first job is to count the money and the branches accurately; a push/fold chart alone cannot compare actions it never modeled.

After-play study only. All stacks, actions, response probabilities and hand situations are fictional teaching cases. This is standard 52-card no-limit Texas hold'em under ordinary tournament rules, not a solved strategy, a hand-specific shove range or live-play assistance. Unless a branch explicitly resets a condition, the table has nine players dealt in, blinds of 0.5/1 BB, and one fully paid 1 BB big-blind ante posted by the big blind alone. Starting stacks are always measured before forced contributions. No fees, bounties, side pots or prize adjustments apply unless separately described. Check actual venue and tournament rules. No gambling account or purchase is needed.

About 10 BB near the money? A shove that can win immediately and a call against an existing shove are different decisions. 1. Begin with the pot, positions and stack convention

Reusable nine-handed tournament fixture

SB contributes 0.5 BB live. BB contributes 1 BB live and 1 BB dead ante. The pot before voluntary action is therefore 0.5 + 1 + 1 = 2.5 BB, regardless of the nine-handed table size. Five earlier positions fold to cutoff (CO); button (BTN), small blind (SB) and big blind (BB) remain to act. CO has posted nothing and begins with 6, 8 or 10 BB as stated in each independent branch. In the first-in comparison, all potential callers begin with at least 13 BB, pay their own forced bets in full and cover CO after posting.

A live blind counts toward a later matching call; the big-blind ante never does. There is one BBA per hand, not nine. An unposted 8 BB cutoff has eight available to wager; a big blind who started with 8 BB has only six behind after paying both the full blind and the full ante. The label “effective 8 BB” is incomplete until you know who posted what and how much of the opponent's live wager is actually matchable.

This guide bridges the 3–5 BB complete pot ledger and the 10–20 BB tournament action and ICM guide. It is intentionally a calculation and action-tree lesson, not a hand chart. For position and stack terms, see position and effective stacks; for what charts leave out, see reading preflop charts.

2. Six, eight and ten BB shoves do not offer the same price

Keep the initial fixture fixed. In three separate examples, action folds to CO; CO shoves the entire 6, 8 or 10 BB pre-hand stack; BTN and SB fold; and a covering BB considers calling alone. CO has no other live contribution. BB's posted 1 BB blind counts toward matching CO, while its separate 1 BB ante remains dead money.

Each percentage is the incremental call divided by the final pot the caller can win if the hand is immediately checked to showdown with no ties. It is a price, not a measured hand equity against CO's range and not a calling recommendation. The larger shove requires a larger additional investment. A different table size, ante arrangement, covering stack or other caller would require a new row.

If all three remaining seats fold to an unposted CO's 8 BB shove, the uncalled 8 BB is returned. CO wins the original 2.5 BB in forced contributions, finishing the hand with 10.5 BB from a starting 8 BB. That is a +2.5 BB net chip gain, not a 10.5 BB profit. The same refund principle applies to the 6 and 10 BB branches.

3. A 2 BB open keeps a flop in the tree

Reset the fixture for each row. CO has 6, 8 or 10 BB unposted. Instead of shoving, CO makes a legal full minimum raise to 2 BB total over the live big blind of 1 BB. BTN and SB fold. The covering BB calls by adding 1 BB beyond its posted blind. The flop pot is identical across these three open-and-call branches: 2.5 + 2 + 1 = 5.5 BB. What changes is CO's stack behind.

SPR means stack-to-pot ratio. Here it uses CO's remaining stack divided by the 5.5 BB pot at the start of the flop. Because the opponent covers CO, that is also the effective remaining stack for betting between these two players. It does not solve a flop decision. Board texture, ranges, position and legal bet sizes still matter. Importantly, a caller after the 2 BB open sees a flop; a caller after an 8 BB shove faces an all-in with no later betting against CO.

A minraise can be folded to a later re-raise, called or shoved over. Its responses are not necessarily drawn from the same opponent distribution as an open-shove. Comparing the 5.5 BB flop pot with a 17.5 BB called all-in pot therefore compares different action histories, not two guaranteed payoffs. The legal actions alone do not show which opener should choose with any particular two cards.

4. Rejamming eight BB over a raise is not opening eight BB

Separate HJ-versus-CO case. Rebuild the original 2.5 BB forced pot. Earlier seats fold to hijack (HJ), who begins with 20 BB unposted and opens to 2.5 BB total. CO begins with 8 BB unposted and moves all-in to 8. BTN, SB and BB fold. HJ alone decides whether to call. This differs from the first-in cutoff case because HJ has already invested 2.5 BB and has represented a raising range.

Rejam pot ledger

Pot before voluntary action: 2.5 BB. After HJ's open: 2.5 + 2.5 = 5 BB. After CO's 8 BB rejam: 5 + 8 = 13 BB. HJ needs 8 − 2.5 = 5.5 BB more to call. If HJ calls, the contested pot is 13 + 5.5 = 18.5 BB, and the direct no-tie chip price is 5.5 / 18.5 ≈ 29.73%. HJ began with 20 BB and retains 12 BB behind after contributing 8; CO is all-in. With only these two players continuing, no further bet against CO is possible.

If HJ folds instead, the unmatched 5.5 BB of CO's shove returns. The pot contested is HJ 2.5 + matched CO 2.5 + forced 2.5 = 7.5 BB. CO finishes with 13 BB, a net gain of 5 BB from the pre-hand eight. Calling and folding are independent outcomes; never add their pots together.

HJ's initial raise increased the 1 BB big blind by 1.5 BB, so a minimum full re-raise is to 4 BB total. CO's 8 BB all-in easily meets that size and is a full raise. It does not follow that CO should shove every hand with eight blinds, nor does HJ's 29.73% chip price prove the equity of HJ's actual holding against an unknown rejam range. For a general raise-response tree, see facing preflop raises.

5. The big blind faces different games after a button shove and a button minraise

Independent BTN-versus-BB fixture. Nine players were dealt in; all seats before BTN fold. BTN starts with 8 BB unposted. SB begins with a covering stack but folds in both branches. BB starts with 12 BB before posting, pays the 1 BB live blind and 1 BB big-blind ante in full, and has 10 BB behind when action reaches BTN. BTN now chooses either a raise to 2 BB total or an all-in to 8 BB total. We study the BB's price if BB elects to call in each separate branch.

Against the BTN shove, BB's direct no-tie chip call price is 7 / 17.5 = 40%. Against the minraise, 1 / 5.5 ≈ 18.18% is only an immediate pot-price fraction, not a guaranteed showdown-equity requirement: further betting, equity realization and possible folds remain. Do not use 18.18% as if a two-blind open automatically buys a five-card showdown. Likewise, a shove/call chart cannot answer the BB's response to a minraise that its tree does not include.

The BB can sometimes raise rather than call the 2 BB open. That adds an entirely different branch with different risk and opponent responses. For the blind's broader action order, see defending the blinds. In either branch the 1 BB ante increased the pot but never reduced the amount BB still owes on a live call.

6. Small blind first in: your own half-blind is not free profit

New blind-versus-blind fixture. All seven seats before SB fold; SB started with 8 BB before posting and has already paid 0.5 BB live, leaving 7.5 behind. BB started with 12 BB and has fully posted 1 BB live plus 1 BB dead ante, leaving ten behind. The pot begins at 2.5 BB. SB raises all-in to 8 BB total, adding 7.5 BB to its posted 0.5. BB needs 8 − 1 = 7 BB more to call. If BB calls, the main pot is 2.5 + 7.5 + 7 = 17 BB; the immediate no-tie chip price is 7 / 17 ≈ 41.18%.

If BB folds instead, SB's unmatched 7 BB above BB's live 1 BB returns. The contested pot is SB 1 + BB 1 + BB ante 1 = 3 BB. SB receives that pot plus the 7 returned, moving from a pre-hand 8 BB to 10 BB. Net pre-hand profit is +2 BB, not +2.5: the other 0.5 of the original 2.5 BB pot was SB's own forced blind. Relative to folding at this decision point, SB is 2.5 BB better off if its shove gets a fold, because folding would have surrendered the posted half blind. These are two different baselines.

The 8 BB cutoff who is not in a blind can win 2.5 BB from three other forced contributions; SB first in cannot treat its own 0.5 BB as an external reward. SB may also complete to 1 BB or make a smaller raise, but those actions give BB different options. This example does not equate a low SPR with a mandatory shove.

7. A short four-bet needs a three-player ledger to explain reopening

Separate three-player case. Reset to the nine-handed 2.5 BB starting pot. CO begins with 8 BB; BTN starts with at least 12; BB starts with at least 12 before paying its full blind and ante. Earlier positions fold. CO opens to 2 BB total; BTN calls 2 BB; SB folds; BB re-raises to 6 BB total, contributing an additional 5 BB beyond its posted live blind. CO moves all-in to 8 BB total, adding 6 BB more. BTN chooses to call the difference from its earlier 2 BB, adding 6 BB. BB now acts again.

Short four-bet: each contribution once

After CO's open, BTN's call and BB's re-raise, the pot is 2.5 + 2 + 2 + 5 = 11.5 BB. CO's final 6 brings it to 17.5 BB. BTN's extra 6 brings it to 23.5 BB. BB has 6 BB live and owes 8 − 6 = 2 BB to call; a call creates a 25.5 BB main pot. The same total can be independently audited as CO 8 + BTN 8 + BB 8 + SB 0.5 + one BB ante 1 = 25.5 BB. CO is all-in; BTN and BB may have chips behind that could form a later side pot between those two if they bet further.

Why is CO's raise to 8 short? BB raised from the prior 2 BB wager to 6, a 4 BB full raise increment. A new full raise would ordinarily need to reach 10 BB total. CO cannot reach ten, so its 8 BB all-in is legal but increases the live wager by only 2 BB. BTN merely called. Under the Poker TDA 2026 Rule 49, that single short all-in does not reopen BB's raising option because BB already acted and is not facing another full raise. In this exact sequence BB can call the additional 2 BB or fold, but not raise again. If BTN had instead made a qualifying full raise, the legal action tree would be different.

This is why action history matters. Both CO and BTN had 8 BB live in the pot at the end, but they reached that amount through different commitments. CO can win only the pot for which its eight is eligible; postflop betting between covering players would make separate eligibility possible. A single-caller price or a two-player push/fold output cannot substitute for this three-way construction. Compare the side-pot lab if future matched amounts differ.

8. A hypothetical shove EV is sensitive to the ranges you have not supplied

Return strictly to the first-in 8 BB CO shove with BB-only call in section 2. For a teaching calculation, invent a joint fold chance f for all players behind CO. When someone calls, assume only BB calls; CO wins outright with conditional probability q and otherwise loses, with no tie, bounty, fee, subsequent betting or other caller. When all fold, CO gains 2.5 BB relative to its unposted starting stack. When BB calls, the pot is 17.5 BB; a CO win gains 17.5 − 8 = 9.5 BB net, and a loss costs 8 BB.

Conditional chip-EV identity, not a solver output

EV(shove − fold) = f × 2.5 + (1 − f) × [q × 9.5 − (1 − q) × 8].

Invent f = 30% and q = 40%: 0.30 × 2.5 + 0.70 × (0.40 × 9.5 − 0.60 × 8) = +0.05 BB. Keep the 30% joint folds but change the conditional win chance to 30%: 0.30 × 2.5 + 0.70 × (0.30 × 9.5 − 0.70 × 8) = −1.175 BB. Neither rate is observed, and no strategy claim is warranted from either invented scenario.

Check where the shove's EV changes sign. At the invented q = 40%, the BB-call branch loses 1 BB on average, so 2.5f − (1 − f) × 1 = 0 requires f = 2 / 7 ≈ 28.57% joint folds to break even. The existing 30%-fold, 40%-win example is only +0.05 BB, while 28% folds with the same conditional win chance gives −0.02 BB. If joint folds are instead fixed at 30%, solving 0.75 + 0.70 × (17.5q − 8) = 0 requires q = 97 / 245 ≈ 39.59%. These narrow margins demonstrate sensitivity, not a reliably profitable shove.

Two players face different thresholds. For this same called pot of 17.5 BB, the BB has already posted a live 1 BB and needs only 7 / 17.5 = 40% equity for its incremental closed-action chip call. By contrast, an unposted CO shoving eight BB and always getting called needs 8 / 17.5 = 16 / 35 ≈ 45.71% conditional showdown wins for the shove to tie CO folding before betting. The 40% call price is not a 40% requirement for CO's shove, and neither figure is a modeled prize-equity threshold.

Do not extend this two-outcome model into a full strategy tree. Here f means the joint probability that BTN, SB and BB all fold; in the other branch BTN and SB fold and BB alone calls. The conditional win chance q concerns only that BB calling branch. Other callers, multiway pots, a CO limp or 2 BB open, later betting and the opponents' actual response ranges were never modeled. A positive shove EV against folding cannot establish that shoving is better than a small open. These are hypothetical sensitivity inputs, not independently measured fold and hand-equity rates. If CO had zero showdown equity when called, an 8 BB shove that wins 2.5 BB on folds needs 8 / (8 + 2.5) ≈ 76.19% joint folds to break even in chips. With the same 0.5/1 BB blinds but no ante, the reward would be only 1.5 BB and the artificial threshold becomes 8 / (8 + 1.5) ≈ 84.21%. Neither is a realistic preflop hand assumption. The purpose is to isolate the ante, not to recommend shoving any cards.

In a real model the fold rate and conditional equity depend on position, allowed action sizes, range weights, calls by different seats and coverage. 9. Chips are not the whole tournament objective

Every quoted direct price or EV above values chips under a precisely constrained branch. None specifies a payout ladder, the remaining field, other short stacks, a satellite's equal seats or a progressive-bounty prize. Those omissions matter even if the action is legal and every chip counted correctly. An ICM or bounty-sensitive answer can disagree with a chip-EV answer. See the independently checked ICM primer and tournament formats before making a prize-equity claim.

For a particularly sharp contrast, compare the chip and seat prices in a fictional two-ticket satellite: a big blind facing a shove can have a profitable chip call that sacrifices a large modeled chance to qualify. Its example has no ante and must not inherit this guide's nine-handed BBA pot.

Also distinguish six-handed from nine-handed charts, live BBA from individual antes, a starting stack from chips behind, an unopened pot from a previous raise, and an action tree that allows minraises from one that allows only push or fold. “Eight blinds” does not preserve those inputs. Ten big blinds near the money: a bubble decision worked from the inputs

Should you shove or call with 10 BB on the bubble? First determine whether you are first in or facing a committed shove. A first-in player may win the uncontested pot when everyone folds; a caller cannot make the existing shove disappear. Then count the live blinds and dead antes, identify who covers whom, and specify every relevant remaining stack and prize. A chip-price percentage alone does not account for losing a chance to cash. The action tree and payout objective are separate model choices.

Reset the earlier fixture completely. This new example has four players remaining, three paid, ordinary cash prizes of $50, $30, $20 and $0, blinds 0.5/1 BB and no ante, and no bounty, deal or deductions. All stacks are measured before posting. The small blind begins with 40 BB, cutoff with 30 BB, button with 20 BB, and you in the big blind with 10 BB. Cutoff and button fold. Small blind shoves 40 BB total, covering your stack. You have already posted 1 BB live and can fold or add 9 BB to call. No ties are assumed in this two-outcome example.

The covering player's 30 BB excess is returned if you call. Only 10 BB from each player is contested, giving a 20 BB final pot. On a closed-action chip-EV comparison, the price of the additional call is 9 / 20 = 45% of that pot. That is a break-even win probability if chip value is the objective, not a measured equity for any two cards.

With no payout on fourth and no tie, a call needs $12.286143 / $21.785714 ≈ 56.40% win probability to match the fold's modeled prize equity. Neither 50% nor 56.40% has been assigned to any real hand or opponent range.

What this example cannot decide: it models a closed-action call, not the small blind's shove strategy, and does not simulate future blind increases, skill, imperfect range estimates, deals, bounties, or a 13-left/10-paid field. A different payout ladder or another player's stack changes the result. A push/fold chart that omits small raises also cannot rank those omitted actions. 10. Nine checks before you reveal the answer

Nine-handed with 0.5/1 blinds and a fully paid 1 BB big-blind ante. Is the starting pot 2.5 or 10.5 BB?

2.5 BB. It is 0.5 SB + 1 live BB + one 1 BB ante. The ante is not multiplied by nine and cannot be credited toward a live call.

CO shoves 6 BB first in and the covering BB calls alone. How much extra does BB pay, and what is the final pot?

5 BB more, 13.5 BB final. The BB's 1 live blind counts toward the six. Starting pot 2.5 + CO 6 + BB extra 5 = 13.5. The direct no-tie call price is 5 / 13.5 ≈ 37.04%.

CO opens to 2 BB from a starting 8 BB; BB calls 1 more. What is the flop pot, CO stack behind and SPR?

5.5 BB in the pot, 6 BB behind, SPR approximately 1.09. The pot is 2.5 + 2 + 1; CO retains 8 − 2 = 6. It is a live flop, not an all-in showdown.

HJ opens to 2.5 BB; CO rejams 8 BB; everyone else folds. What does HJ owe, and what direct call price results?

5.5 BB more into an 18.5 BB final pot, or about 29.73%. The pot before HJ's call is 2.5 forced + 2.5 HJ + 8 CO = 13. HJ calls 5.5. The fraction is a chip price, not HJ's known hand equity.

BTN opens to 2 BB; BB calls 1 BB more, making a 5.5 BB flop pot. Does 1 / 5.5 = 18.18% guarantee a profitable BB call with 19% showdown equity?

No. Further betting, position, equity realization, opponent ranges and future folds can change the EV. The 18.18% is the immediate pot-price fraction, not a closed-action showdown threshold. An all-in call would use a different tree.

SB starts with 8 BB, posts 0.5, shoves to 8, and BB folds. What is SB's net gain from before posting?

2 BB. BB's 1 live blind and 1 dead ante are the external reward; SB's own 0.5 was already its money. SB receives a 7 BB unmatched refund plus a 3 BB contested pot, finishing with 10 from 8.

CO opens 2, BTN calls 2, BB three-bets to 6, CO shoves 8 and BTN calls to 8. Can BB now raise to 12 under the stated TDA rules?

No. BB's full raise increment was 4, from 2 to 6. CO's all-in to 8 is only 2 more and BTN merely calls, so no full raise has reopened BB's option. BB owes 2 to call. If BB calls, the main pot is 25.5 BB.

Why does an 8 BB CO shove called by BB produce a 17.5 BB pot, while the same unposted 8 BB shove over a 2.5 BB HJ open produces 18.5 BB if HJ calls?

They have different prior wagers. BB already posted 1 live and calls 7: 2.5 + 8 + 7 = 17.5. HJ opened 2.5 and calls 5.5: 2.5 + 2.5 + 8 + 5.5 = 18.5. Do not transplant one caller's price into the other's action tree.

Can these calculations determine an ICM shove range near a satellite bubble without prizes and other stacks?

No. They are chip ledgers, legal-action examples and hypothetical responses. Prize or seat equity needs the real payouts, field, coverage, range and model assumptions. Do not treat a price fraction as a solution to an unmodeled tournament.

11. A practical decision record for 6–10 BB study

  1. Before-posting stacks: record all relevant chip amounts, blind level, seats dealt in, positions and exactly who paid the ante.

  2. Current action: state whether you are first in, facing a minraise, facing a shove or responding after your own earlier wager.

  3. Legal options: list fold, call/complete, small raise, full re-raise and all-in only where available; calculate the minimum full raise increment from the actual last full raise.

  4. Separate branches: for each candidate action note the amount added, pot facing the next player, unmatched refunds, who can win which pot and the stack behind if a flop is possible.

  5. Model assumptions: write the chart's ante type, position, effective-stack convention, allowed bet sizes, chip-EV or ICM objective, and any range or fold-frequency estimates.

  6. What remains unknown: write the opponent holdings you did not see, relevant payout/seat/bounty structure, and any hand-history detail you cannot verify. Review away from play; use the hand-recording guide for an honest source trail.

Do not rush from a correct formula to “always jam ten blinds.” Ten is the boundary of this lesson and the opening of the 10–20 BB deeper-stack comparison. The 3–5 BB pot ledgers give the lower-depth contrast. The skill is to keep the decisions comparable without hiding the branch that makes them different.
