Draft / Five learning stages

A course built around doing, not collecting articles.

The working plan is a short sequence of narrated examples, pauses to explain decisions, and changed situations that test whether you can use the idea without seeing the answer first.

  1. 01

    Follow one hand from seat to showdown

    Track the button, blinds, turn order, actions and pot before introducing strategy.

  2. 02

    See the hand you actually have

    Build the best five cards, identify draws and notice when the board changes the story.

  3. 03

    Explain a decision under uncertainty

    Separate chip accounting from strategic reasons. Describe price, possible opposing hands and the limits of your assumptions.

  4. 04

    Rehearse different table situations

    Pause realistic illustrative hands, give a reason, review feedback, then try a changed situation.

  5. 05

    Prepare, reflect and keep perspective

    Understand practical live-game procedures, venue rules, sensible spending limits and how to review hands away from play.

This outline is provisional. The practice material must pass qualified human review and beginner observation before being represented as validated instruction. Learning to explain a hand does not establish profitable play.

Expectations / Boundaries

What this would, and would not, offer.

Who is the proposed course for?

Adults beginning no-limit Texas Hold’em, or people who know the rankings but still find it hard to follow action and explain choices. It is not designed as advanced solver training or a promise to beat a particular game.

When will it launch, and what will it cost?

Neither has been decided. The outline, teaching quality and real demand need validation first. Requesting emails does not create a place in a paid program or an obligation to buy.

Will the free course be locked?

No. Poker Foundations and the existing public guides remain free. Any future paid offer must earn its price through something different.

Does the course teach gambling for profit?

No financial outcome is promised. The goal is to understand hands and make better-informed decisions, with attention to uncertainty, spending limits, and live-room rules. All practice should happen away from active play.
