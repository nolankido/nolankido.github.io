Poker / Resource collections

Watching more poker is not the same as answering a poker question. Choose one thing to learn, find a source that exposes enough information, and leave with a small record you can check.

Choose the format for the job.

A hand breakdown can help reconstruct action. A lecture can develop an argument. An interview may explain a player's experience without supplying a complete strategic proof. All can be worthwhile, but they should not be judged as though they perform the same task.

For a calculation, prefer material with written inputs, readable graphics or a usable transcript. For a conversation about playing recreationally, a clear episode description can help you choose what to hear, but it does not establish what every guest said. Keep the difference between selecting a programme and reviewing its contents visible.

Four starting points, with different review limits.

Combinations: MIT's Lecture 7 with Will Ma is presented as an in-depth hand-analysis session. Its page includes a transcript interface and an authorized offline-video route. The page was inspected; the complete video and transcript were not reviewed for this listing. Use the combinations workshop as an independent exercise rather than assuming the lecture endorses its examples.

Tournament models: MIT's Lecture 6 is described as covering chip values and tournament equity, with a transcript interface and linked slides. It is a 2016 course, not current software documentation. Try writing the model's inputs before opening an ICM tool. The page and available formats were inspected, not the full lecture.

Recreational goals: Thinking Poker episode 466 introduces a conversation with RecPoker participants about recreational players. Audio and show notes are provided; no full transcript was verified. Its topic is a reason to consider listening, not a claim that this site has listened to and validated the whole episode.

A written interview: the James “SplitSuit” Sweeney interview page includes substantial transcript text. This offers a text-first route into a creator conversation. Interview statements remain personal views rather than independently tested performance claims.

Pause before the result changes the story.

For a hand video, pause at one decision. Write the position, stack, pot, action and cards that the player could know then. Keep later-revealed hole cards in a separate line. A broadcast graphic can give the audience information the player did not possess.

Fictional viewing task: the pot is 80, an opponent bets 40, and a call would end the betting. Write the 40 / 160 = 25% threshold before the reveal, then identify the range assumption needed to compare your hand with it. If the video does not supply enough information, the correct note may be “range unknown,” not a confident verdict.

A transcript is useful, but card notation needs checking.

Search within available text for the topic, then read the surrounding explanation. A transcript label does not guarantee that every card, suit or bet amount has been captured accurately. Cross-check technical numbers against the video or slides where available. When that is not possible, label the uncertainty rather than silently correcting it from memory.

Save a link and a timestamp or heading in your private note. Use your own words for the takeaway. Do not redistribute a full transcript, video, paywalled chart or course download merely because you can access it. Link to the publisher's authorized resource instead.

Use one output as the stopping point.

A complete session might end with one reconstructed pot, one hand-ranking correction, or one disagreement whose assumptions you can name. It does not need another hour of viewing. Compare the takeaway with a second suitable source only when that comparison serves the original question.

Check your understanding.

Does a show-notes page prove there is a full transcript?

No. Show notes, a transcript interface, and inspected transcript text are different levels of access and review.

Should you use revealed opponent cards when recording what the player knew?

No. Separate information available at the decision from cards revealed later.

Is a full video download automatically permission to republish it?

No. Use authorized access and inspect the applicable rights. A link and your own short note are different from redistribution.
