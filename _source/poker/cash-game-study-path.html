A useful cash-game study session ends with something you can explain: a game description, a checked pot, a range assumption or a question to investigate. This five-session route starts with free reading and does not require playing for money.

New to live rooms? Start with your first cash-game session. Already have a completed hand? Begin at the first step that reveals something missing from your record. These are self-paced study tasks, not a required course or a promise to improve your results.

Session 1: describe the game before the hand.

Read: the cash-game assumptions and the cost guide. Outside source: an example of an official rake schedule, used to see what needs recording rather than to copy a live room's prices.

Produce: five lines naming the game and stakes, positions, relevant stacks, the charging rule, and who can still act. An invented 1/3 game with stacks of 300 and 180 is 60 BB effective between those two players before any chips are committed. A third player requires another comparison; there is no single effective stack for every possible pot.

Stop when: you can explain why “100 BB cash” is not a complete description. Keep unknowns visible rather than filling them from a convenient chart.

Session 2: price the call without deciding it.

Read: the pot-odds workshop. Outside source: a step-by-step call-price explanation. Use the source for the accounting, then check whether later action or charges invalidate a simple threshold.

Produce: a three-line ledger. In a fictional heads-up river pot of 60 before a 30 bet, the current pot is 90 and the extra call is 30. With no charges or further betting, the final pot is 120 and the threshold is 30 / 120 = 25%. This is not an estimate of the hand's equity.

Now suppose an invented 6 charge comes from the final pot after a call. What changes?

The award becomes 114. Under these closing-action assumptions, the break-even share is 30 / 114 = about 26.32%. Do not apply this change when the 6 was already deducted from the pot figure.

Stop when: you can distinguish the current pot from the final pot and name an assumption needed to turn the price into a decision.

Session 3: write two plausible assumptions, not one hidden certainty.

Read: the combination workshop. Outside source: the beginner combinatorics article. List specific combinations before assigning any weights.

Produce: two explicitly hypothetical opponent ranges for a completed or fictional hand. Explain why changing one assumption might change the conclusion. These ranges remain hypotheses. A shown hand is one observation, not verification of an entire range. As a small card-removal check, holding A♠ K♦ leaves three available aces, so only 3 pocket-ace combinations remain, rather than six.

An equity calculator is optional, not required. Before using one, write the hands, board and range assumptions on paper. An equity percentage alone does not model every future bet. The equity-realization explanation addresses that gap.

Session 4: check the other players and the eligible pots.

Read: the multiway workshop and its linked side-pot case. Outside source: the multiway discussion, especially the distinction between shared defense and a personal heads-up quota.

Produce: an action-order note and a pot-eligibility sketch. Name every player who can still raise and every pot your hand can win. Do not include an uncalled amount in a contested pot. You can complete this with the site's fictional cases instead of collecting a new hand.

Stop when: you can explain why a cheaper-looking call is not necessarily better when another player remains to act. Use the exercise's answer panel only after writing your own explanation.

Session 5: finish one review, then choose one next source.

Read: how to review a hand without starting from its result. Outside source: the expected-value introduction for the distinction between an outcome and an average over specified possibilities.

Produce: a short conclusion that names the facts, the assumed range, the cost model and the remaining uncertainty. Change the ending from a win to a loss: which parts of your explanation should remain the same? A one-session result cannot verify a strategy or a long-run win rate.

Select further material only after naming what is unresolved. Use the task-based tool shortlists for range analysis or model setup. Reading public documentation can be enough; buying access is not the required final step.
