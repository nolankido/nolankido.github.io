A colored square is the last step in reading a preflop chart, not the first. The important information is the game, seats, stacks and actions that produced it.

Read the header before the hand.

Identify the format, number of players, positions, starting stacks, blinds and antes. In cash games, check the rake model. In tournaments, distinguish a chip-EV reference from one modeling payouts or bounties. “20 BB” does not tell you whether the table is heads-up or nine-handed, or whether the hand begins before or after forced bets.

Next, reproduce the action sequence. An unopened button chart does not answer a button decision after a raise and a caller. A push/fold solution answers a restricted action problem; it does not establish that a small raise or limp is inferior in a game where those actions are available.

GTO Wizard's Study Mode documentation shows selection by positions and actions. Use a provider's setup information to identify the spot rather than treating a screenshot as a complete model.

A 169-cell matrix is not 169 equally likely deals.

In standard hold'em, the 13 by 13 matrix groups the 1,326 unordered two-card combinations into 169 hand classes. Before any known-card removal, a pocket pair has 6 suit combinations, a suited nonpair has 4, and an offsuit nonpair has 12.

Thirteen plus 78 plus 78 gives 169 classes, while 78 plus 312 plus 936 gives 1,326 combinations. Selecting one offsuit class can therefore represent three times as many combinations as selecting one suited class. Read the matrix's actual labels and legend rather than assuming every product places suits or colors identically. SplitSuit's range-reading guide connects matrices, written ranges and percentages.

A mixed color describes a conditional frequency.

A fictional frequency, not a solved recommendation

Suppose a teaching chart assigns AKs a 50% raise and 50% call frequency, uniformly across its four unblocked suit combinations. That contributes 2 weighted combinations to each action. It does not mean that a player must raise exactly twice in the next four times they receive AKs, or that half of every holding in their entire range should raise.

When a range is conditioned on earlier actions or known cards, both weights and available combinations can change. Do not average cell percentages without considering their weights. Use the combinations workshop for blocker and weighted-range examples.

Check what “effective” left out.

Fictional mismatch: you recorded 20,000 chips before posting a 1,000 big blind and a 1,000 big blind ante. The record is 20 BB before posting, but 18,000 chips remain behind. A source asking for a pre-posting stack and a source asking for chips behind need different inputs. Do not subtract the forced bets twice.

With several opponents, a single “effective stack” can conceal different caps on different pots. Preserve the relevant players' stacks, positions and contributions. A two-player chart does not become a multiway answer by entering the smallest number at the table. The short-stack guide supplies the input checklist.

Use the output to ask a better study question.

Once the spot matches, compare the available actions and their expected values. A colored preference without EVs does not reveal whether alternatives are nearly tied or substantially different. A small displayed difference may also be smaller than the uncertainty created by your assumed ranges or approximate model.

Make one controlled comparison: same positions and objective, but a different opening size or clearly documented range assumption. Label it as a model comparison, not a measurement of how opponents actually play. An exploitative adjustment needs a defensible read; a result alone is not that read.

Save the source, version or access date, full inputs, and the question answered. Do not redistribute a provider's paid charts. This page teaches chart literacy; it does not publish validated opening or push/fold ranges.

Three useful checks.

Does one AKs square carry the same number of combinations as one AKo square?

No. Before blockers, AKs has four combinations and AKo has twelve. Equal-looking cells do not have equal probability mass.

A chart says “button, 20 BB.” Can it settle a decision after someone has already raised?

Not without the action history and other settings. An unopened-pot reference answers a different question. Mark the mismatch rather than borrowing the chart's precision.

Can you use this guide or its linked charts during a live hand?

Use this site's study material away from play. Some online policies also restrict tools while the playing client is open. Check the rules and fair-play guide and the operator's own policy.
