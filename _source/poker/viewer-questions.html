Read the player, the action, then the amount.

Start with three questions: Who is deciding? What has happened so far? How much more would a call cost? Read the board and pot at that moment, not the cards or result revealed later.

Questions that can interrupt the story

These are anticipated viewer questions, not a record of audience submissions.

Why can we see hole cards the opponents could not see?

An editor may add cards later, or a broadcast may use a separate card-reading system. Viewer information is not automatically player information. Judge a decision using what that player knew then, not what the finished video reveals.

Does a hand graphic prove the exact action was recorded?

No. It can summarize live footage, notes, memory, or an illustrative example. This site labels fictional examples and supports reconstructed-hand labels. Uncertain amounts and suits should not become exact simply because the graphic looks precise.

Why does a player fold without showing?

A player can relinquish a hand without voluntarily revealing the cards in ordinary play. Showdown and all-in tabling requirements are different and depend on the applicable rules. A missing reveal is not proof of a bluff.

Why does a vlog skip so many folds?

An edited story selects moments. It does not show the complete distribution of decisions or results unless it explicitly documents them. A sequence of exciting hands is not evidence that every hand was exciting or profitable.

Is a million-chip pot a million dollars?

Not in an ordinary tournament. Read the event structure and actual payout, not the chip denomination. The tournament guide also shows why cashing can still leave an attempt or trip at a net loss.

Why did a strong starting hand lose?

A starting hand is not a completed five-card hand. Shared cards change the comparison. Even a genuine favorite has a chance to lose; an unfavorable result alone does not settle whether the earlier decision made sense.

Does later commentary tell us exactly what the player thought?

Not unless that thinking was captured at the time. Recollection and later analysis are separate from the recorded action. A useful review says which is being presented.

May viewers or friends help during a live hand?

Do not offer tactical help or reveal folded cards during an ongoing hand. Follow one-player-to-a-hand and venue rules. Save analysis for afterward. Filming and device use also require compliance with the event, not just permission from a person in the video.

Where should a rules dispute be decided?

By the event staff using the applicable house rules and regulations, not by a caption or a general internet guide. The references here explain common procedures but cannot decide an unseen live situation.
