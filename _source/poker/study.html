01

One decision

The hand-review sheet

Use it for: a particular decision you want to reconstruct and revisit. Record the situation, action, what you thought then, and your later interpretation separately.

Smallest useful version: positions, relevant stacks, action up to the decision, and one unanswered question. Mark unknown details instead of guessing.

Worked example: the button faces a raise to 7 BB after betting 2 BB in the fictional hand-review guide. Start by checking the extra 5 BB to call before debating the strategy.

02

One session

The session-debrief sheet

Use it for: preserving the shape of a session without turning every hand into a project. Keep a few factual anchors, one moment worth reviewing, and a realistic next action.

Smallest useful version: what is recorded, which decision needs a closer look, and what information is missing. Keep results separate from the quality of the decisions.

Read a short fictional example

Record: A fictional player saved two hand notes after a tournament. One includes exact raise sizes; the other has an uncertain starting stack.

Review question: Can the pot in the first hand be reconstructed from the written action?

Next step: Recalculate that pot before adding a strategic explanation. Leave the second stack marked unknown. Do not invent a result to complete the account.

Keep it proportionate

A worksheet is not the verdict.

These records do not validate a strategy or promise better results. They may be unnecessary for a small question that a conversation can resolve. Take a break rather than turning a frustrating session into more compulsory work.

Keep completed records private. Before sharing a hand, remove names and other identifying details, label recollections, and decide which details are actually appropriate to publish.
