Try a fresh situation before moving on

After the stage you are studying, try its matching checkpoint situation. After all six, choose a pair of unfamiliar situations and explain them with the lesson closed. The aim is to use an idea in a changed setting, rather than recognize a paragraph you just read.

Try the beginner checkpoint to see which explanation to revisit. It offers six situations, explanations of common mistakes and six changed situations to retry. Choose two, give your own reason first, then use the exact repair link that fits the difficulty. A familiar-looking answer is more useful when you can explain a new example too.

Keep the blank checkpoint workbook (TXT) in your own notes. Record your first reason, one thing you corrected and what happened on the retry. The website does not collect those answers. If wording or navigation still gets in the way, use the optional reader-feedback prompts; they lead to the existing contact form and explain what it asks for.

If you are comfortable with those ideas, you can choose a specific live-play preparation guide or an optional study task below. There is no requirement to start playing for money.

Return later and explain a changed situation

At your next study session, close the lesson and try two checkpoint questions before reopening the explanation. Pick different situations on a later day. A possible routine is a short recall check at the next session and another later in the week; this is a practical suggestion, not a tested optimal schedule for poker.

  1. Recall: say the rule or question in your own words without looking.
  2. Change the example: use the checkpoint’s changed situation, not just the same answer letter.
  3. Repair one gap: follow the precise lesson link, then explain the difference.
  4. Keep one note: record the skill, the mistake and the link to return to. Pause when the task is done.

Use the six-stage study card for your own notes. It includes the route, guided-hand prompts and a later-review plan. Answers and progress are not sent to the website.

Why the course uses examples, explanations and return visits

The Institute of Education Sciences recommends combining worked examples with problem solving, asking for explanations, using retrieval practice and spacing study. Those general findings informed this design. They do not validate this poker course or establish a learning rate for its readers. Read the IES practice guide.
