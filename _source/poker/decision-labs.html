Pause / Predict / Reveal

Put yourself at the decision, not just the result.

Three visual, fictional cases. Work out one thing before opening the next panel: the price, the cards that change the winner, or which player can win which chips.

For after-play study and poker viewers. No account, answer submission or live-play assistance. The panels work without JavaScript.

01 / Read the range

One river. Two different stories.

A♠Q♣

A river call can win or lose without changing what was known beforehand. Price the call, change the bluff weighting, then compare two endings.

Leave with: a conditional decision explanation that survives either result.

02 / Read the runout

The flush arrives. What can still change?

A♥J♥

Follow a face-up hand across the streets. Count the possible rivers and check what changes when the opponent holds a different hand.

Leave with: an exact remaining-card count with its information set stated.

03 / Read the pots

Three all-ins. Two pots.

A♥A♦

Separate a three-way all-in into a main pot, side pot and refund. Then explain why the strongest hand does not receive every chip.

Leave with: a complete chip ledger and a clear explanation of the result.

Choose your route

Start with the question you care about.

New viewer: start with the runout case, then the pot case. Use hand rankings whenever the name of the winning hand is unclear.

Tournament student: begin with pot eligibility, then try the range case. A correct price is only useful when it refers to the right pot and the right assumptions.

Vlog creator: work through any case and describe it in three sentences before reading its closing narration. Keep the action, your interpretation and the eventual result distinct.

A small practice loop

Read the setup. Make one prediction in your own private notes. Reveal the explanation. Name the exact mistake or uncertainty. Finally, change one input and try again. The changed examples check understanding rather than memory of the first answer.

No score is assigned and nothing records your answers. A missed example is a study question, not a verdict on your ability. Ordinary pageviews follow the existing privacy notice.
