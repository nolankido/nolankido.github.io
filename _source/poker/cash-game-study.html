The same ace-queen hand can sit inside very different problems. A cash-game hand has a cash ledger and a house-charge structure. A tournament hand may also involve changing blinds and a payout model. Start by naming the actual game instead of borrowing a chart from the other format.

For callers, action order and separate pots, use the multiway-pot workshop.

Write the game above the hand.

Record the stakes, game, number of players, positions, effective stacks, antes or straddles and the exact action. A fictional 300-chip stack with a 3-chip big blind is 100 BB, but that alone does not tell you which ranges, bet sizes or fees a reference assumed.

In an ordinary cash game, chips have a fixed redemption value. That is not a guarantee of profit, and it does not make every pot a simple heads-up calculation. The position and stack guide explains the units; the tournament-equity guide shows why prize value is a different objective.

A small house charge can change a close calculation.

Read the actual room or operator's fee schedule. PokerStars' official rake page below illustrates how an operator documents rates and caps; it does not supply the rates for a live cardroom. Percentage rake, fixed collection, promotional drops and time charges should not be silently treated as the same cost.

Fictional river example, not a recommended wager

The pot is 80 before an opponent bets 40. You face a 40 call, with no later betting. Before charges, the final pot would be 160. Suppose this invented game charges 5% of that pot, capped at 6, with no other deductions. The charge is 6, leaving 154 to award.

The break-even equity is 40 / 154 = about 25.97%, not 40 / 160 = 25%. At exactly 25% equity, the call's expected value relative to folding is 0.25 × 154 - 40 = -1.50. With no charge it would be zero. These are conditional calculations, not an estimate of your actual equity.

Count a charge only once. If a displayed pot already excludes a collection, do not subtract it again. If a fee has already been paid regardless of the current decision, separate that sunk cost from additional costs caused by calling. The example assumes the stated charge is taken from the final pot after a call.

A multiway pot needs everyone's decision.

Before applying a heads-up lesson to a multiway hand, write who can still act after you. A call may not close the betting. Another player may raise, a short stack may have a separate main pot, and not everyone is eligible for every chip. The side-pot lab lets you check that bookkeeping without guessing strategy.

Do not turn a convenient simplification into an observed fact. “Assume the third player folds” is a scenario to test, not a description of a hand in which that player has not acted. A chart with two participants cannot establish the effects of a third participant's decisions.

Implied odds are conditional, not money already in the pot.

The implied-odds article below introduces future payments after a draw completes. When reviewing its logic, ask whether the opponent would actually continue, whether the draw can make a second-best hand, and how much is available to win. A large opposing stack does not mean that stack will be paid to you.

Start with the amount required now, then write separate future branches. In one branch the draw misses; in another it completes and receives no further payment; in another it gets action. Naming these branches is more useful than adding an optimistic future amount to every outcome.

Use a library as a set of testable explanations.

SplitSuit and The Poker Bank provide different entry points into cash-game teaching. The Crush Live Poker video catalogue adds live-hand material with its own access conditions. Choose a question such as bet sizing or effective stacks, then compare the assumptions instead of trying to finish every archive.

Record actual buy-ins, top-ups and cash-outs separately from your hand-review conclusions. An attractive hourly result over a short sample is not a demonstrated long-run win rate. Choose personal time and spending limits without relying on predicted winnings. Study does not require paying for another playing session.

Is the example's 6-chip cap the fee I should use in my own review?

No. It is an invented input that makes the example checkable. Use the applicable fee schedule and record whether the pot figure is before or after deductions. A reference from a different game or jurisdiction is not enough.
