Poker / Tournament guides

The entry buys a seat. The trip also buys travel, meals, a place to sleep and time away. Three transparent budgets show how a $400 tournament becomes a $451.50 local outing, a $734.25 overnight trip or a $1,417.50 two-event weekend.

Illustrative budgets, not price quotes or accounts of Nolan’s trips. Event information checked October 15, 2026. The October 2026 WSOP Circuit at Turning Stone is a dated worked example; use the method with your own event and current booking totals. All amounts are US dollars.

Before reading every line, choose the closest plan: local, one entry; one entry and a hotel; or two events over a weekend. Then copy the worksheet. If you are still comparing events, start with the structure-sheet guide.

Read these three numbers separately

A useful tournament budget answers three questions. What do I intend to spend? What extra money have I set aside for defined possibilities? What did I actually spend when I got home?

Overnight, one initial entry $734.25 $649.75 $1,384.00
Weekend, two planned entries $1,417.50 $649.75 $2,067.25

On a narrow screen, scroll the comparison sideways. Each model below gives the full breakdown.

The reserves in the last two rows consist of $400 for at most one optional re-entry plus $249.75 for an extra night, meals and incidentals. The local reserve is $50 for a possible return visit and food. Unspent reserves are not expenses or losses. The funds column excludes any hotel authorization hold and costs outside the stated scenarios.

These figures describe three invented travelers. They are not an average price, a recommendation to spend this amount or a forecast of winnings. The model works only if a zero-payout trip fits the traveler’s discretionary budget. Necessary household money and the means to get home remain protected.

  1. Planned spending = planned entries + planned direct trip expenses.
  2. Funds available = planned spending + optional-entry reserve + defined contingencies.
  3. Actual spending = entries and trip expenses actually paid, including reserves used.

Money held temporarily on a card is a separate liquidity issue. It can reduce spending capacity before checkout without becoming a final charge. Time and foregone income also deserve their own record; they are not hidden additions to these cash totals.

What the $400 entry includes

The official October 2026 Turning Stone structure packet lists the Mini Main, Event #2, as a $400 total entry: $330 toward the prize pool and $70 administrative fee. Its fee includes staffing/dealer fees and rake. Events #12 and #14 show the same breakdown. [1]

The fee is inside this $400 entry
Part of this entry Amount
Prize-pool contribution $330.00
Included administrative fee $70.00
Total payment for one entry $400.00

The disclosed fee is 17.5% of the total payment: $70 ÷ $400. The prize-pool component is 82.5%. Do not add $70 again and call this a $470 entry. These percentages describe these particular events, not every $400 tournament. A separate payment-method charge, if applicable, would need its own line. A voluntary tip is not assumed to be mandatory.

A re-entry is another paid attempt after elimination during the permitted window. Here that means another $400, not another $70. A starting flight is a scheduled starting session of the same tournament. A separately numbered event is a different tournament. Read registration and re-entry for the distinctions.

Check: how much for two entries?

The entry is listed as $330 + $70. You use one initial entry and one permitted re-entry. Is the entry bill $470, $730 or $800?

Show the calculation

$800: 2 × ($330 + $70). Each $400 entry already includes its fee. Travel and other purchases remain separate.

Budget one: local, one entry

Profile: the player lives 30 road miles away and attends Event #12. One $400 entry is the entire poker allocation. No hotel or re-entry is planned.

The driving inputs are assumptions: 60 miles round trip, 28 miles per gallon and $3.50 per gallon. Fuel is 60 ÷ 28 × $3.50 = $7.50. The model assumes a route without tolls and uses the venue’s advertised free self-parking. Meals and incidentals are personal spending caps, including applicable taxes and tips, not claims about menu prices. [4]

The $50 gives a possible added tournament day somewhere to live in the plan. The published one-day format can be extended. It is a modest modeled buffer, not a guarantee that every disruption fits. [1]

If the player receives no payout, spends the base plan and never touches the buffer, the pre-tax trip result is −$451.50. It is not −$501.50. The $50 remains theirs.

The entry is about 88.6% of planned spending. Buying another $400 stack would change this outing much more than a small snack purchase. This player’s precommitted limit is one entry even though the event permits more.

Protect the afternoon, evening and a possible later day. Include registration and a safe journey home after a late finish. The budget cannot predict when the player will be eliminated.

Budget two: one entry and an overnight stay

Profile: the player drives 150 miles each way to the Saturday morning Mini Main flight and books Saturday night. If they advance, Sunday’s noon restart does not require a late 150-mile drive followed by an immediate return. The base plan has one entry; one additional entry is optional.

Driving assumptions are 300 miles round trip, 28 mpg and $3.50 per gallon, producing $37.50 of fuel. The $12 toll allowance is an assumption, not a quoted Thruway fare. Use the official calculator for the actual route, vehicle and payment account. New York’s Thruway uses cashless tolling; payment method affects the amount. [5]

Room assumption: a nearby taxable offsite hotel costs $180 before tax for one person for one night, with no separate mandatory fee in this model. Neither that rate nor availability has been quoted for the event dates. The illustrative tax load is 13.75%, using an 8.75% sales-tax component and 5% county occupancy-tax component. State and county references support those components; they do not establish the checkout total at a particular property or the tax treatment of every resort room. [6] [7]

Modeled room: $180 × (1 + 0.0875 + 0.05) = $204.75. Replace the whole line with the selected booking’s verified total, including every applicable mandatory charge. Do not add tax a second time to an already tax-inclusive quote.

The extra-night reserve is $204.75 room + $35 meals + $10 incidentals = $249.75. It assumes another room is available at the same modeled price. A real extension may cost more, so establish a workable backup before entering. Sunday play can end late or extend to another day. Advancing does not make lodging free, and the trip should work before a payout is known.

If the player busts Saturday, uses the already-booked room and returns Sunday, the unused $649.75 reserve is not a loss. If they use the optional entry, spending becomes $1,134.25 before an extra night. If they use both defined reserves, spending reaches $1,384.00 under these assumptions. A vehicle problem or a higher last-minute room price can exceed that modeled amount.

The room is about 27.9% of the base plan. It is now a substantial part of the decision. Check cancellation terms as well as the nightly rate: an early exit does not necessarily make a booked night refundable.

Check: was the unused reserve lost?

You made $1,384 available, used one entry and the base trip, received no payout, and left the reserves untouched. What did the outing cost?

Show the calculation

$734.25. That is $400 in entries plus $334.25 in trip expenses. The unused $400 entry reserve and $249.75 contingency, totaling $649.75, were available but never spent.

Budget three: a two-event weekend

Profile: the player drives 240 miles each way, books Friday and Saturday nights, and plans to enter Event #12 on Friday and Event #14 on Saturday. There is one optional re-entry across the whole weekend, not one per event.

The second event is conditional on being free to play it. If the first tournament extends and conflicts with the second, the player skips the second. Planning for two entries does not require paying both in advance or abandoning a surviving stack. Check the day’s transitions and continuation arrangements before committing.

Driving assumptions produce 480 ÷ 28 × $3.50 = $60 in fuel, plus an assumed $18 in tolls. Two nights at the same modeled all-in room price cost 2 × $204.75 = $409.50.

Entries account for about 56.4% of the base plan and lodging for about 28.9%. The advertised $400 entry is real; it is simply a small description of a much larger purchase.

Sharing the room changes only the costs actually shared. If the same $409.50 room total covers two people without an extra charge, each pays $204.75 toward lodging. Holding every other input fixed, each person’s base plan falls by $204.75 to $1,212.75. This comparison deliberately leaves the driving cost unchanged. If they also share a car, allocate the actual vehicle bill separately rather than halving everything. Each person still pays for their own entries.

After an early bust, there is no requirement to replace the event with another tournament or cash game. Rest, a non-poker activity or going home when reservations permit can all fit the plan. A reserve is a ceiling, not an assignment to fill every free hour with play.

Small adjustments are easier to judge when the baseline is visible.

None of these is a recommendation to enter. The point is to change an input and recalculate, rather than stretch the spending limit after a loss.

Flying is a replacement transport plan. Suppose airfare is an assumed $300 return and checked bags an assumed $70 total. Turning Stone advertises private Syracuse airport transportation at $125 each way, including tax and gratuity, subject to availability and requested at least 48 hours ahead. Confirm that the service is available for your chosen hotel and dates; an offsite stay may require additional transport. [8]

If those transfers fit the itinerary, the illustrative package is $300 + $70 + $250 = $620. Replacing the weekend’s $78 fuel-and-toll line increases the base by $542, to $1,959.50. This is not a fare quote or a general comparison of flying and driving. It assumes a free drop-off at the origin airport, no airport parking bill and no other ground transfers. Replace those assumptions for an actual itinerary. Do not leave both complete drive and flight packages in the same total unless both really occur.

The drive models count fuel and toll cash expenses, not depreciation or maintenance. You may track vehicle wear separately. A tax mileage allowance is not literal gasoline spending.

A cash is not necessarily a profitable trip

For this guide, the accounting is:

Pre-tax trip result = gross tournament payouts − actual entry spending − actual direct trip spending.

“Gross payouts” means the tournament prizes before personal tax consequences. It does not mean profit. Count every entry and all direct trip spending once, including any reserve actually used. Exclude reserves left untouched. Keep another event’s cash-game result separate if you want to understand this tournament trip.

Consider a hypothetical $600 gross payout on the overnight base plan:

$600 − $400 entry − $334.25 trip expenses = −$134.25 pre-tax.

The player cashed and still spent more than they received. Pre-tax break-even gross payouts are $451.50 for the local base, $734.25 for the overnight base and $1,417.50 for the solo weekend base. Using the overnight re-entry raises that threshold to $1,134.25, assuming all other spending stays fixed.

These are accounting thresholds, not estimates of the chance of reaching them. The examples supply no expected return, cashing probability or evidence of an edge. A prize-pool guarantee does not establish any of those things. The results guide explains why an individual cash and a record of profitability answer different questions.

Personal income-tax treatment is outside these models and depends on the applicable rules and the player’s situation. The arithmetic here is consistently pre-tax. This trip limit also does not prescribe the size of an overall poker bankroll.

Check: what if the player re-entered?

The overnight traveler receives the same hypothetical $600 payout but paid for two $400 entries and $334.25 of trip expenses. What is the pre-tax result?

−$534.25: $600 − $800 − $334.25. Counting only the last entry would overstate the result by $400. Any extra-night spending would reduce it further.

Keep time and opportunity cost visible

Protect a calendar window for travel, registration, play, rest and a possible continuation. For these profiles that means a local day with possible return, a Saturday–Sunday overnight trip with a possible extension, and a Friday–Sunday weekend with a possible extension. They are planning windows, not predictions of playing time or elimination.

Then ask what the time displaces. If eight hours away genuinely cost $200 of wages, record $200 in foregone income, separately from receipts. Paid vacation does not automatically mean losing a day’s salary. It uses limited time that you may value differently. A leisure valuation is also possible, but label it as a personal judgment rather than a cash payment.

Avoid counting the same hours twice as both lost wages and another full hourly opportunity cost. Likewise, a cash budget can count the full casino meal bill while a separate incremental-cost comparison subtracts spending genuinely avoided at home. Use a documented baseline; do not invent a food offset to make the trip look cheaper.

The useful leisure question is: If payouts are zero, is this experience worth the money and time I have chosen? Enjoying the experience and demonstrating a profitable poker edge are separate judgments.

Before leaving, resolve these points:

  1. Entry cap: the total number and price of planned and optional entries, and when a new entry would no longer fit the day.
  2. Booking and return plan: checkout total, cancellation terms, card holds, safe transport home and a possible later day.
  3. Available funds: the base plus defined reserves, without depending on a payout. This does not require carrying the whole amount in physical cash; verify the accepted payment methods.
  4. Stop rule: losses do not authorize more entries. An unused reserve can stay unused.

Questions before you commit

How much money should I bring?

Enough accessible funds for your plan and chosen reserves, plus any separately disclosed authorization hold. The three examples require $501.50, $1,384 or $2,067.25 under their stated assumptions. They are not universal amounts to carry in cash. Check payment instructions and protect return transport independently of poker winnings.

What happens if I reach another day?

The event continues according to its rules. Reaching it can trigger another room, more food, changed transport or displaced commitments. Here an extra night plus meals and incidentals is modeled at $249.75. Verify actual availability and price rather than assuming the sample reserve guarantees a room. Do not count a future payout as the funding source.

Can I budget for no re-entry?

Yes. The local plan does exactly that. A permitted re-entry is an option under event rules, not an obligation. Leaving after one attempt can be a complete plan.

What if the trip only works if I cash?

The budget is relying on an uncertain return. Reduce the outing, stay local, remove optional entries, wait or skip it. A cash game after elimination is not a dependable way to recover the hotel bill. For gambling that is becoming difficult to control, NCPG’s help and treatment resources are separate from poker coaching. The site’s player-protection guide explains how to find official support routes.

The advertised buy-in tells you the price of one entry. A complete budget lets you decide whether the whole outing fits before the first hand.
