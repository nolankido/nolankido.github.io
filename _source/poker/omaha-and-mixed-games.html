Exactly two is the rule worth checking first.

In a fictional Omaha hand, you hold ace of spades, king of diamonds, queen of clubs and jack of hearts. The board is two of spades, seven of spades, nine of spades, jack of spades and three of diamonds. You do not have a flush: using four board spades and one hole spade is illegal in Omaha. Only one of your hole cards is a spade.

Five-card Omaha adds a hole card, not permission to use three. Do not use a hold'em hand evaluator, or a four-card range table, without checking which game it supports. A visually impressive screenshot is not enough to establish a legal hand.

Winning low does not always mean winning half.

A fictional Omaha eight-or-better showdown

Board: three of clubs, four of diamonds, eight of hearts, king of spades, king of diamonds.

Player A: ace of spades, two of spades, king of clubs, queen of hearts. Player B: ace of hearts, two of hearts, queen of clubs, jack of diamonds.

For high, A uses king of clubs and queen of hearts with the two board kings and eight: three kings. B cannot beat that. For low, both use ace-two with the board's three-four-eight: 8-4-3-2-A.

With a 200-chip pot to distribute after any applicable charges, A wins the 100-chip high half and 50 of the tied low half, receiving 150. B receives 50. B has been quartered. These are awards, not each player's net profit; their earlier contributions are not specified.

A low needs five different qualifying ranks. Having ace-two does not guarantee a qualifying low on every board, and two players can hold equally good lows. When no one qualifies in this format, the high winner receives the whole pot. Those rules do not mean that all lowball games have a qualifier.

“Low” is not one ranking system.

In ace-to-five lowball, aces are low and straights and flushes do not spoil the low. In deuce-to-seven, aces are high and straights and flushes count against it. Thus an unsuited 7-5-4-3-2 is an excellent deuce-to-seven hand, while A-2-3-4-5 is not the lowball winner it would be under ace-to-five rules.

Badugi adds another distinction: rank and suit duplication can reduce the number of cards that count. Read its four-card ranking rule before comparing the numerical height of two hands.

Check the current game, not just the previous hand.

In mixed games, confirm the rotation and betting structure before applying an action rule. A fixed-limit round and a pot-limit round do not offer the same raise sizes. In short-deck hold'em, even the familiar high-hand hierarchy can change: the linked PokerStars six-plus rules rank a flush over a full house. Other organizers' short-deck rules need their own check.

Both players make the best low. Does each get half the entire pot?

Not in the example. They split only the low half, so each receives a quarter of the whole pot from low. A also wins the entire high half and receives three quarters overall.
