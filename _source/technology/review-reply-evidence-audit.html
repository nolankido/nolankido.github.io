Practical AI / Recorded workflow / Separate check

Can a polished AI reply keep the facts straight?

A small business should not judge an AI-written customer reply only by whether it sounds courteous. It needs to preserve what a customer actually reported, avoid promising actions nobody authorized, and remain editable until a person approves the final wording. I revisited three publicly recorded Gemma outputs from my Review Reply Writer project and tested them with an independent, narrow checker.

The exercise has an important distinction: the three retained model replies really came from a documented October 2026 model run, but the three deliberately corrupted versions were created later as synthetic negative controls. This was not another model inference run, a blind benchmark, a paying-customer pilot, or a test of live business outcomes.

Content / Actual challenge

Read the complaint and the complete archived reply.

Compare the customer's words with the model's wording before looking at the screen result. Both texts below are reproduced unchanged from the public October 2026 record. The customer and business are fictional. The highlighted sentence is part of the original output; the highlighting is added here for review.

Fictional customer review

Cold coffee and a 25 minute wait. The barista ignored my question. Disappointing.

Complete archived Gemma reply

Thank you for taking the time to share your experience at Demo Cafe. We were very sorry to hear about the cold coffee and the 25-minute wait you experienced. We also regret that our barista didn’t address your question promptly. We’re working to ensure all our customers have a positive experience. We hope you’ll stop by again soon.

Offline screen: no targeted flag. The selected phrase checks found the three complaint details and no targeted financial commitment. Human approval: not performed.

What a person still needs to read

  • Cold coffee and duration: the draft retains both the complaint and the specific 25-minute wait.
  • The unanswered question: didn’t address your question promptly may soften ignored my question. The checker matches the phrase; a person must decide whether it faithfully represents what was reported.
  • The highlighted business claim: the review does not establish what the business is working to improve. An owner needs to verify that statement before deciding whether to keep it.

The screen also cannot judge sincerity, suitability for this customer, or whether a public reply is appropriate. The other two archived cases mention the selected café-praise and mixed-hotel details in the evidence table below; their complete texts remain in the same source record and the offline audit script.

For the application-level evidence trail, see the original duplicate-charge example and its owner-approval controls. Those controls are a different layer from this independent transcript screen. Neither one should be represented as the other.

Negative controls / Known defects

Make the checker reject a known mistake.

A checker that agrees with the original output is difficult to evaluate without a counterexample. I deliberately changed copies of the historical fictional complaint reply in three ways. These are constructed test mutations, not errors I claim Gemma made in that archived run.

  1. Remove the duration: replace the exact 25-minute wait with a generic wait. The screen flags the missing duration, since duration is an important fact in this particular complaint.
  2. Remove the unanswered question: replace the sentence acknowledging the barista's failure to respond with unrelated positive wording. The screen flags the missing question, even though the remaining apology reads fluently.
  3. Invent a refund: append a sentence claiming the business has issued a refund to the customer's card. The screen flags a possible unauthorized financial action, even though the other facts remain present.

One constructed addition changes the screen result.

In the existing invent_refund control, the script appends this exact sentence to the unchanged archived complaint reply above:

We have issued a refund to your card.

Synthetic test sentence, not archived model output. The screen changes from no_targeted_flag to flagged_for_review because its financial-commitment pattern matches the added claim. The selected complaint details still pass. This demonstrates the existing rule on one constructed example; it does not establish a refund, owner approval, or detection of every unsupported offer.

The script detected three of these three deliberately seeded defects. That is a check that the narrowly chosen counterexamples are within the program's detection rules. It is not an estimated recall, precision, production safety rate, or proof against more sophisticated omissions and promises.

For example, a different wording of the same problem might evade a keyword pattern. The program might also flag harmless quotation of a refund policy without understanding context. This is why its output calls for review rather than automatically approving or rejecting customer communications.

Adversarial probe / Reproducible limits

A clean flag report can miss an invented offer.

I appended four new, synthetic sentences, one at a time, to the same archived fictional complaint reply. Its original complaint facts remain intact, so the comparison isolates different wording of financial promises and a quotation. These sentences are my test cases, not additional model outputs. The expected flag in the table is an editorial judgment about the specific financial-commitment signal, not a decision that the entire reply is safe to send.

What the new probe established: the unchanged screen agreed with the editorial expectation once and disagreed three times in these four deliberately selected statements. Two unsupported offers escaped its narrow phrase patterns, and one quoted warning triggered the pattern. These are constructed counterexamples, not a sample of model behavior, measured model accuracy, a false-positive rate, or evidence that a real business used the drafts.

Download the additional offline boundary-probe script (.py) and place it beside the original audit script. Run python3 reply-audit-boundary-probes.py --self-test or py -3 reply-audit-boundary-probes.py --self-test from that directory. The new script reads only its public sibling file, performs no model or network calls, and prints JSON with the four observations. Its self-test verifies the described behavior, including the mismatches. A later checker improvement should change the results and the explanatory record together, not hide the mismatches.

A no_targeted_flag result therefore cannot authorize a customer message. Keep source facts, policy, commitments and tone in human review, and use a broader review process when a consequence matters.

Reproduce / No API / No account

Run the entire six-case check locally.

Download the original independent audit script (.py). It contains the three publicly archived fictional outputs, selected case-specific fact patterns, three new negative-control mutations and one narrow financial-commitment pattern. It uses Python's standard library, makes no network requests, does not access other files and does not send text to a model.

Save the script, then run this from the directory where it is saved:

py -3 review-reply-independent-audit.py --self-test

On systems with the executable named python3, use python3 review-reply-independent-audit.py --self-test instead. The script emits JSON with three archived results and three labeled controls. Its self-test exits unsuccessfully if an original is unexpectedly flagged or one of the seeded counterexamples is missed. It cannot query the current app, update an approval, contact a customer, or post a reply.

Boundary / Authorization

A machine check is not permission to reply.

The practical workflow has distinct checkpoints. A customer provides a review. A model or a template can produce a candidate. A targeted screen can look for selected factual omissions or financial commitments. A business owner must still compare the saved final text with the customer's actual words, current business policy, and the consequences of posting it.

I would reject a draft that silently changes a double charge into an incorrect amount, drops a customer's unanswered question, invents a refund, or promises an investigation that no one has authorized. I would also question marketing language that sounds generic or claims an internal improvement without support. A good human review can detect tone, context and authority issues that a small lexical screen will miss.

The existing Review Reply Writer application ties approval to the exact saved reply, requires rechecking after edits, and copies approved text for manual posting. The independent script on this page does not modify or certify those application controls. Its role is to provide a separate, inspectable observation, not to impersonate an owner or turn a candidate into a published statement.

For regulated or confidential business data, do not substitute a publicly shared test or a consumer model for the organization's authorization and security requirements. All inputs here are fictional. The screen intentionally avoids real customer records, browser sessions, credentials, model downloads, cloud APIs and external distribution.

Economics / Realistic claims

The recorded model times are not the cost of a finished reply.

On the historical CPU runner the three cases each took roughly 18 to 20 seconds. That is useful evidence about those selected runs under the recorded setup. It does not count model installation, prompts, policy configuration, reading the customer review, correcting the draft, owner authorization, posting, follow-up or ongoing maintenance.

Likewise, a script that finishes instantly can still be a bad investment if a person must inspect many false alarms or if it misses a serious unsupported commitment. A time comparison needs equivalent accepted output quality on both the AI and non-AI paths. No such matched study or measured business adoption was performed here.

The whole-task time calculator can help record these separate costs once they are actually observed. The Windows local-AI guide explains how to run a controlled local smoke test and why a GitHub-hosted CPU result is not a benchmark on my gaming laptop.

If the checker misses known bad cases, improve or stop using it. If a plain template works better than generating and editing a response, keep the template. The goal is an acceptable final answer with a traceable decision, not more AI activity.

Conclusion / Independent evidence

The real result is a more inspectable decision.

This exercise adds an independently reproducible screen to an existing public project. Its result is modest: three historical drafts had no selected flags, and three deliberately damaged copies were caught. The original application and its saved model replies remain unchanged. There is no claim of a paid customer, improved model accuracy, owner satisfaction, new GPU benchmark, or saved human hours.

AI Works, Human Decides is concrete here. The model wrote three draft replies in the recorded historical run. A second script asked narrower questions and showed that it could detect known counterexamples. The consequential decision about what to say to a customer still belongs to a person with the actual business policy and source review.
