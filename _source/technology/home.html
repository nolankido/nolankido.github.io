Nolan Kido / A practical AI method

AI Works Human Decides

Give AI a defined job. Keep authority and accountability with the people affected. I use three rules:

  • AI Works. Set the permitted inputs, output and stopping point before delegating a draft, organization task or code change.
  • Human Decides. People choose the purpose, review facts and consequences, authorize commitments, and can reject the result.
  • Proof Matters. Check claims independently. Count setup, review, corrections and maintenance when deciding whether AI helped.

Follow the four checkpoints: Decide, Brief, Check, Measure. The browser tools make no AI calls.

See the method in a website repair that passed 347 tests but still contained an HTML defect and the site-visibility release record. Each documents a specific result and its limits.

This working standard cannot replace professional review when consequences are high. About Nolan Kido.

Small business / One accountable pilot

How can a small business start using AI on a real workflow?

Choose one repeatable, reversible task that produces a reviewable draft, such as a customer reply or a summary of approved public notes. Keep sending, promises, financial decisions, private records and customer-account changes outside this first pilot.

  1. Decide. Name the result, simpler alternative and permitted inputs. Pause if permissions are unclear. Use the task-choice guide.
  2. Brief. Specify required facts, prohibited claims, format, reviewer and stop condition. Build a task brief before trying a model.
  3. Establish a baseline. Record the manual process at acceptable quality. Use fictional, public or explicitly approved examples. Keep inputs, prompts, outputs, corrections and rejected drafts together privately.
  4. Check. Compare facts and promises with authorized sources. Try a missing complaint and an invented offer. Require a person's approval. Inspect the offline reply audit for a limited example.
  5. Measure. Count preparation, model use, review, corrections, setup, maintenance and failed attempts at the same acceptable quality. Keep the manual fallback. Use the full-workflow time calculator with observations or labeled estimates.

A concrete approval boundary: a fictional review reply

A fictional customer reports a duplicate charge and an unanswered question. AI may draft an acknowledgment of both. It may not invent a refund policy, promise compensation, retrieve private records or send the reply. A person checks the facts, authorizes any promise and decides whether to publish.

The Review Reply Writer case and separate evidence audit demonstrate that boundary using historical fictional examples. They do not establish client deployment, savings, compliance or a general model reliability rate.

When to stop: unauthorized input, an unsupported promise, a failed acceptance check, or total effort that does not justify the result. For broader risk-management background, see the NIST AI Risk Management Framework. This educational pilot outline is not a NIST assessment or a substitute for legal, security or professional review.

Recorded AI-assisted work / Identity evidence

How an AI-assisted audit corrected a public identity claim.

On November 4, 2026, an AI-assisted review found role and credential claims in this site's public JSON-LD identity file that the reviewed evidence did not support. The visible About page did not make those claims. Pull request #180 removed them while preserving the Person identifier, scholarly article and existing profile links.

The revised source and published JSON-LD matched on readback. That verifies the correction, not search-engine use, better rankings, AI citations or visitor trust.

How the work was checked

  1. Read the records. Compare the public before file and after file.
  2. Separate support from inference. Coauthorship does not establish an institutional appointment; public code does not establish a business outcome.
  3. Limit the edit. Remove unsupported claims and retain documented work, identifiers and citations. The separate historical COO description remained historical.
  4. Verify publication. Parse and compare source and published data, then inspect the committed diff. This checks the change, not every remaining biographical claim.

Reproduce the bounded comparison

Download the two complete public JSON-LD files linked above as before.jsonld and after.jsonld. Save the offline Python identity-evidence gate in the same folder. With Python 3.10+ on Windows:

py -3 identity-evidence-gate.py --self-test
py -3 identity-evidence-gate.py --before before.jsonld --after after.jsonld

Five synthetic self-tests check expected pass and fail scenarios. The comparison checks structural invariants, not biography: exit code 0 means those checks passed, 2 reports findings, and 1 reports an input or execution error. It runs offline without an account or AI request. Download the case record and reusable evidence worksheet.

What remains a human decision

I authorized the improvement process; the assistant made the scoped edit. This was not independent human verification of every credential, employer or account-ownership claim. Restoring a title or degree requires a suitable source, precise dates and a disclosure decision. My About page links the available evidence.

The lesson: keep an evidence trail and a correction path. A structural checker is not a truth checker. This single-site case does not measure AI editing quality or business returns.

The missing outcome measurement

Not established: increased impressions, clicks, rankings, indexing, reader satisfaction, YouTube subscriptions or AI-answer inclusion. Those outcomes need baseline and follow-up measurements with consistent definitions. Publication checks alone do not establish them.

A no-cost monthly check: An offline Search Console CSV summarizer turns owner-exported page, query and optional daily chart reports into a private aggregate JSON summary. The export and interpretation instructions explain the missing-data rules. With a complete daily chart export, it checks calendar coverage and uses chart rows for property clicks and impressions, never a sum of truncated page rows. It makes no network calls, does not expose query text in the output, and cannot confirm the original Search Console filters, freshness, or the date ranges of separate table exports.

How to check search visibility without paying for another dashboard

A website can pass technical checks and still have no measured search improvement. A useful follow-up compares the same reporting period and filters over time, without treating page publication as proof of more clicks. Here is a free procedure that keeps the raw data on your computer.

  1. Export one complete month. In your verified Google Search Console property, open Search results, select Web and the last complete calendar month, then export the daily chart (sometimes Dates.csv) and Pages table. Record the property, date range, filters and export date privately. Queries are optional.
  2. Run the local checker. Save the Python script linked above beside the CSV files. In that folder, this example handles a month-first chart dated like 10/1/2026: 
    py -3 .\gsc-monthly-local-audit.py --month 2026-10 --pages .\Pages.csv --chart .\Dates.csv --chart-date-format mdy
  3. Read the two kinds of totals separately. Property clicks and impressions appear only when the daily chart has every date in the selected month. The Technology, Poker and other section totals are sums of the exported Pages rows, which may be limited. A visible nonbrand Technology click lower bound requires a combined Query + Page export, never a join of separate files. For ISO chart dates, omit the format option; use dmy for explicitly day-first dates.
  4. Repeat without overclaiming. Use the same property, Search type and filters for a later complete month. Check the figures against Search Console before keeping a private scorecard. A month-over-month difference does not establish causation, indexing, AI-generated citations or reader satisfaction.

Try it before exporting private data. Download the synthetic Pages CSV and synthetic Dates CSV. Both contain invented counts, with an extra Synthetic column marked YES. Save the two files beside the Python checker, then run this Windows command:

py -3 .\gsc-monthly-local-audit.py --month 2026-10 --pages .\gsc-synthetic-pages.csv --chart .\gsc-synthetic-dates.csv --chart-date-format mdy

Expected fictional result: 31 October dates, 62 property clicks and 3,100 property impressions from the sample chart, versus 22 clicks across the three sample Pages rows (12 Technology, 7 Poker, 3 Other). The gap is intentional: page-row totals cannot stand in for property totals. Optional query figures remain unavailable because no query file is supplied. These files are not genuine Google exports, Nolan Kido traffic measurements or evidence of SEO growth. This release of the checker recognizes nolankido.com URLs and its section names are specific to this site; using it for another website requires adapting those rules. The checker still prints its standard privacy warning because real exports must stay private.

Google explains the property-versus-page counting distinction and export row limits. The open-source tool's initial release, daily-chart validation and explicit date formats are inspectable changes, not observed search gains. The script prints aggregate JSON locally, writes no file on its own and never sends your CSV data away.

A method you can reuse: name a reader's question, identify the best existing page, make a useful and accurately linked revision, preserve its canonical URL, verify the published result, and measure the outcome separately. Google's link guidance explains crawlable descriptive links; its sitemap guidance explains when a changed date is meaningful. Neither promises a ranking increase.

Worked example / Offline QA

Seven constructed edit conflicts. Seven targeted flags.

To make the truth-to-screen release gate testable, I wrote a small standard-library Python checker for structured edit claims from the public Portland Meadows Episode 1 record. This is a deterministic audit of text fields, not an AI watching my footage. I constructed four fictional, incorrect scene records and paired each with a corrected record. The checker found the seven expected targeted conflicts in the constructed records and none in the corrected examples.
