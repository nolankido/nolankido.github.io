Nolan Kido / A practical AI method

AI Works Human Decides

My approach to implementing AI in real life is simple: give it bounded work, then keep authority and accountability with the people affected. A convincing answer is not yet a useful result. This is a working standard, not a claim that every project succeeds.

  • AI Works. Use it for a defined part of the job, such as a draft, an organization task, or code with a testable outcome. Set permitted inputs, boundaries, and a stopping point first.
  • Human Decides. People choose the purpose, review the facts and consequences, authorize commitments, and can reject the output. Delegating production does not delegate responsibility.
  • Proof Matters. Check important claims independently and include setup, review, corrections, and maintenance when deciding whether AI actually helped.

Follow the four checkpoints: Decide, Brief, Check, Measure. The free worksheets and tools below can help with each stage. No AI runs in the browser tools themselves.

Inspect a recorded example: a website repair that passed 347 tests but still contained an HTML defect shows why independent checks matter. It documents one public repair and its limitations, not a claimed business outcome or proof of broad AI expertise. A later public site-visibility release record shows exactly which discoverability changes were published and which outcomes remain unmeasured.

The method applies to personal workflows and small teams, but it cannot replace domain-specific safeguards or professional review when consequences are high. About Nolan Kido explains the broader interests behind it.

Small business / One accountable pilot

How can a small business start using AI on a real workflow?

Begin with one repeatable, reversible task. A useful first pilot produces something a person can inspect, such as a draft customer response or a summary of approved public notes. Keep sending, promises, financial decisions, access to private records, and changes to customer accounts outside the pilot. The aim is to decide whether this one workflow is worth keeping, not to announce an organization-wide transformation.

  1. Decide whether the task belongs in AI. Name the desired result, the simpler alternative, who may supply the input, and what must never leave an approved system. If permissions or confidentiality rules are unclear, pause rather than uploading the material. Use the task-choice guide.
  2. Brief a narrow, checkable output. Define the permitted input, required facts, prohibited claims, expected format, reviewer and stop condition. For a customer reply, a polished tone is not an acceptance test. Build a task brief before trying a model.
  3. Compare against an agreed baseline. Use fictional, public or otherwise explicitly approved examples. Record the manual process and its acceptable quality before comparing a proposed AI-assisted process. Keep the inputs, output, prompt version, corrections and rejected drafts together, without publishing sensitive records.
  4. Check independently and require human approval. Compare every factual statement and commitment with the authorized source. Try deliberate counterexamples, including a missing complaint and an invented offer. The reviewer must be able to reject a draft. Inspect a bounded offline reply audit, which is not a production error-rate study.
  5. Measure the whole job and decide whether to continue. Count setup, preparation, model use, human review, correction, maintenance and failed attempts. Compare work at the same acceptable quality. Keep the manual fallback if the evidence is insufficient. Use the full-workflow time calculator with your own observations or clearly labeled estimates.

A concrete approval boundary: a fictional review reply

Example input: A fictional customer reports a duplicate charge and an unanswered question. AI may: draft an acknowledgment that preserves both issues and leaves unknown details open. AI may not: invent a refund policy, promise compensation, retrieve private account records, or send the message. A person checks: both complaints are represented, every promised action is authorized, and the final reply is appropriate before deciding whether to publish it.

The Review Reply Writer case documents an application built around approval and its historical fictional demonstration. The separate evidence audit shows what a limited checker flags and misses. Neither is evidence of client deployment, measured savings, regulatory compliance or a general model reliability rate.

When to stop: unauthorized input, an unsupported promise, a failed acceptance check, or total effort that does not justify the result. For broader risk-management background, see the NIST AI Risk Management Framework. This educational pilot outline is not a NIST assessment or a substitute for legal, security or professional review.

How an AI-assisted audit corrected a public identity claim.

The real task: I wanted this site to explain who Nolan Kido is and what evidence supports the work, without inflating professional credentials. A separate, already public JSON-LD identity file claimed an AI startup and angel-investor title, two degrees, and an MIT Research Affiliate role. The visible About page did not make those claims. In a November 4, 2026 improvement pass, an AI assistant compared the data with the public evidence trail and narrowed the file rather than inventing more impressive claims.

The outcome: Pull request #180 was merged. The revised source and published JSON-LD were read back and confirmed identical, while the stable Person identifier, scholarly article and existing external links were retained. This is a recorded content correction. It does not establish that a search engine used the standalone asset or that search rankings, AI citations or visitor trust improved.

How the work was checked

  1. Read the actual published data. Compare the before record with the after record, not a reconstructed story about them.
  2. Separate support from inference. A research paper establishes coauthorship, not an institutional job title. Public code establishes that an artifact exists, not a business outcome. An attractive biography is not a credential verification.
  3. Review a narrow edit. Remove the three categories of unsupported prominent claims, rewrite the description around work visitors can inspect, and preserve unrelated identifiers and the existing scholarly citation. The separate historical COO description stayed historical, not a current position.
  4. Check the result independently of the narrative. Re-read the JSON source and generated asset, parse the graph, compare immutable nodes and stable links, and inspect the actual committed diff. A code diff can prove changed bytes; it cannot prove the truth of every remaining claim.

Reproduce the bounded comparison

Download the two complete public JSON-LD files linked above as before.jsonld and after.jsonld. Save the offline Python identity-evidence gate in the same folder. With Python 3.10+ on Windows:

py -3 identity-evidence-gate.py --self-test
py -3 identity-evidence-gate.py --before before.jsonld --after after.jsonld

The five synthetic self-tests check expected pass and fail scenarios. The second command checks this case's structural invariants, not the accuracy of free-text biography. Exit code 0 means only those invariants passed; code 2 reports structural findings; code 1 is an input or execution error. The script uses no network, account, AI request, visitor data, or paid API. Download the case record and reusable evidence worksheet for the sources and a review method to adapt to another public profile.

What remains a human decision

The owner authorized a broad improvement process; the assistant made the scoped edits and opened the pull request. This release did not include independent human verification of every academic, employer or account-ownership claim. Restoring a professional title or degree requires reviewing a suitable source and choosing precise wording, dates and public disclosure. External account control, search indexing and any improvement in discovery remain unmeasured. My About page links evidence people can inspect, without treating an AI-generated description as a credential.

The lesson: when AI helps publish a professional identity, require an evidence trail and a correction path. A structural checker is not a truth checker. This case concerns one public website, not a benchmark of AI editing quality, search performance, or small-business return on investment.

A public release trail / What the evidence supports

What did an AI-assisted website visibility sprint actually change?

This website is a small example of applying AI to a real publishing problem. I wanted Nolan Kido's practical AI work, technology projects and tournament poker records to be easier to find and understand. The public record shows changes to pages, links and their supporting evidence. It does not yet show that search rankings, visitor numbers or AI-assistant citations improved.

  1. Make evidence easier to reach. The homepage and About page gained descriptive links to an original local-AI laptop guide and an independent reply-audit case. Home also gained a link to my source-linked tournament player record. Inspect the reviewed link changes.
  2. Keep freshness signals truthful. Four key landing pages had substantive updates while their sitemap dates still described older content. Their lastmod dates were brought into line with those reviewed revisions, without treating a rebuild as a new publication. Inspect the sitemap correction.
  3. Give readers a useful answer. The Technology page gained a small-business AI pilot guide that sets task boundaries, review ownership, independent checks and stop conditions. It is instructional material, not a claimed client deployment. Inspect the source and published-page diff.

How I checked the releases

Each reviewed change compared the editable source with the public HTML, checked that its destination links and canonical routes existed, and looked for claims the record could not support. The published pages were then checked directly after deployment. Those checks demonstrate that the intended content was released at the sampled time. They are not a replacement for a complete browser, accessibility or security review, and some later full test suites could not run in the available environment.

The missing outcome measurement

Established: the specific public pages, links, sitemap dates and guide text changed as documented in the linked pull requests. Not established: increased Google impressions, clicks, rankings, indexing, referral traffic, reader satisfaction or appearances in AI-generated answers. Those outcomes require a baseline and follow-up data, with query and page definitions held consistent; no such result is asserted here.

A method you can reuse: name a reader's question, identify the best existing page, make a useful and accurately linked revision, preserve its canonical URL, verify the published result, and measure the outcome separately. Google's link guidance explains crawlable descriptive links; its sitemap guidance explains when a changed date is meaningful. Neither promises a ranking increase.

AI judgment in software, small-business workflows, and poker

  • Software: A website repair that passed 347 tests still had an HTML defect. Test results are evidence within their scope, not proof that every browser experience works.

Creative production / A human evidence gate

How I check an AI-assisted poker vlog edit against the hand record.

AI can help arrange a story, draft an overlay or organize a revision list. It cannot establish an unrecorded card suit, reconstruct a missing board, or turn my later analysis into something I knew at the table. My public Portland Meadows Episode 1 companion supplies two documented hands for a practical truth-to-screen check. This is a proposed repeatable release standard, not proof that every earlier upload passed it.

  1. Lock the source before editing. Give each hand a fact record, a public evidence link, and an explicit unknowns field. Treat private footage notes and later recollections as different evidence categories.
  2. Reconcile voiceover with every on-screen assertion. Check positions, stack units, raise-to amounts, pot arithmetic, stated hand, board progression and outcome at the moment the viewer sees them. If a suit is unknown, either omit it or clearly mark a purely illustrative reconstruction.
  3. Separate in-the-moment thought from hindsight. A retrospective range or solver-style question must not be presented as a contemporaneous calculation or a verified solver result.
  4. Review the actual release cut. Earlier version 7 chapter offsets in the companion were not checked against the current public upload. Never carry old timestamps into a chapter list merely because the story is similar.
  5. Give a person the final decision. Accept, revise or hold each sequence; check music, footage, venue rules and participant privacy separately. An AI edit suggestion does not supply those permissions.
