Can AI find continuity errors in a book series?

Yes, but not reliably enough to skip an editor. Preflight, my book-series continuity project built on the open-source canonkeeper engine, found Watson's changing war-wound description in six Sherlock Holmes volumes. In a separate, imperfect benchmark with 20 planted changes, it found 8, missed 12 and reported 2 false positives after the same output was rescored. Those are results from one recorded test, not an accuracy forecast for your manuscript.

The useful role of AI is to extract chapter facts with source quotations, compare possible conflicts, and show an author exactly which passages need attention. A report can be wrong about a disguise, a name change or the meaning of a sentence. The author still decides whether an apparent inconsistency needs a revision.

Recorded audit / Public-domain books

Watson's wound moves from shoulder to leg.

In A Study in Scarlet, Watson describes a bullet striking his shoulder; another passage refers to his injured left arm. In The Sign of the Four, he nurses a wounded leg. The saved report puts the passages together and suggests changing the later book's wound reference.

The important design choice is the evidence beside the finding. An author can read both passages and decide whether the change is an error, a deliberate development, or a question requiring more context. A confident label without those passages would be harder to challenge.

The accompanying series bible makes extracted facts browsable by character and book. In the Watson wound view, the reader can follow the changing descriptions and open their source passages.

An editor's decision / Free review log

What should an author do when an AI checker flags a contradiction?

Short answer: keep both exact passages, check that they refer to the same person and point in the story, look for an explanation, then record an editorial decision. Treat the AI finding as a question to investigate, not an automatic instruction to rewrite the manuscript.

  1. Capture two sources. Record book, chapter and the exact sentence for each claimed value. If the checker cannot point to both passages, mark the finding as unsupported rather than inventing the missing source.
  2. Check context before calling it an error. A disguise can change an appearance; a maiden name is not necessarily a surname conflict; injuries can occur at different times. In the recorded Holmes audit, the system incorrectly flagged a disguised eye description and a maiden-name example.
  3. Decide and document. Classify the item as a supported error, explained change, unresolved question or unsupported flag. Note whether you will revise a passage, keep it intentionally, or seek more context. Recheck the exact revised sentence against the rest of the series.
  4. Look beyond the flagged list. A checker can miss facts during extraction before comparison even begins. In the recorded rescored test, 8 of 12 missed plants were lost during extraction and 4 during judgment. An empty findings list is not a clean bill of health.

From chapters to reviewable findings

Extract, compare, then ask what the text supports.

  1. Extract sourced facts. A model reads chapters into individual claims. The engine checks whether the accompanying quotation exists in the text before retaining the claim.
  2. Find possible disagreements. Local rules collect candidates such as conflicting descriptions, names, or appearances. This stage deliberately raises questions for later review.
  3. Judge with context. A model examines relevant material and can identify a contradiction, clear a change using an explanatory passage, or leave a question for the author.
  4. Return to the books. The report and bible let a person inspect the passages and accept or reject the finding. Suggested fixes remain editorial choices.

The engine documentation explains the stages and available providers. Checking that a quotation exists establishes traceability; it does not establish that the model assigned it to the right character or interpreted it correctly. Findings / Mistakes / Misses

Measure the errors the report fails to find.

The October 27 project record describes 3,963 extracted facts from six volumes. Alongside Watson's wound and the Hudson/Turner landlady discrepancy, it records two wrong contradiction calls: Holmes's eye description while disguised, and a maiden name mistaken for a conflicting surname. The stored corrections preserve those reasons.

Of the 12 misses in the revised record, eight were lost during extraction and four during judgment. That points to a practical lesson: improving the final explanation cannot recover a fact that never entered the comparison. AI Works, Human Decides here means using the report to direct attention while retaining the author's decision about the text.
