Human decision lab: should this AI draft be published?

Fictional source record, Tuesday at 2:00 p.m.: A free community workshop begins Friday at 6:00 p.m. Registration is required by Thursday at noon; there is no approved walk-in policy. The room has 24 places, with 18 registrations confirmed at the recorded time. Accessibility arrangements have not been confirmed. A designated organizer must approve public announcements, and the private attendee list is out of scope for any AI input.

Constructed AI draft, not an actual model output: “Come Friday at 7:00 p.m.! All 24 spots are still open. Just walk in, and wheelchair accessibility is guaranteed. We can publish this automatically.”

Try it first: Identify each contradicted fact, calculate the number of unreserved places at the snapshot, flag missing evidence and decide whether you may edit, send or automatically publish. A convincing sentence is not a checked source or permission to act.

Reveal the source check and human decision
  1. Time: The draft says 7:00 p.m.; the source says 6:00 p.m. Revise.
  2. Capacity: 24 minus 18 equals 6 unreserved places at Tuesday 2:00 p.m., not 24. This is a snapshot, not a guarantee that six will still be available later. Recheck current availability or omit the count.
  3. Registration: Walk-ins were not authorized. Registration is required by Thursday at noon. Replace the false invitation.
  4. Accessibility: No confirmation was supplied. Omit any guarantee and direct questions to the organizer, without implying that access is either confirmed or unavailable.
  5. Authority and privacy: The organizer must approve the exact public text. An AI draft cannot authorize its own publication. Do not feed a private attendee list into an unapproved tool.

Possible corrected private draft: “A free community workshop is planned for Friday at 6:00 p.m. Registration is required by Thursday at noon. Contact the organizer for current availability and accessibility details.”

Decision: REVISE AND RECHECK the draft against the source. HOLD PUBLICATION until the designated organizer reviews and approves the exact version. STOP USING AI FOR THIS TASK if an unapproved system would require confidential attendee details or unauthorized publishing. This exercise does not establish that any draft was actually sent.
