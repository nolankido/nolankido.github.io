About the book

What is One Workflow at a Time about?

One Workflow at a Time: How Small Business Owners Can Test AI Before Spending More Time or Money starts with one useful question: does an AI-assisted process actually improve a real task once all the work is counted? A convincing demonstration is not the same as a reliable system.

The approach is to choose one bounded task, name a human owner, define a reference result, test what the system gets right and wrong, and include preparation, corrections, approval and maintenance in the time calculation. The conclusion may be to adopt the workflow, narrow its scope, or stop.

This is an author description of the book's subject, not a guarantee of saved time or a claim that a particular AI system meets business compliance requirements.

Intended reader

Who is this practical AI book for?

Owners and operators of small businesses, and independent creators testing repeatable tasks, who want an evidence-based way to decide whether AI is worth using. The book's approach emphasizes permissions, a documented baseline, explicit quality checks and human accountability. Sensitive records require appropriate authorization and safeguards.

Worked example: when the AI draft costs more time

A fictional weekly business update takes 12 minutes manually. An AI-assisted version takes 4 minutes to prepare its sources, 3 minutes to draft, 7 minutes to fact-check, and 1 minute to fix an error. The total is 15 minutes, three minutes longer than the manual version. Fast drafting does not establish overall savings. These are invented teaching numbers, not business performance data.

Try a second, original site-only example: A fictional report takes 12 minutes manually per run. An AI approach takes 8 minutes per run after 30 minutes of initial setup. Over 10 runs, which is faster, before accounting for any unmeasured quality differences?

Reveal the full-task calculation and decision limit

Manual: 10 × 12 = 120 minutes. AI-assisted: 30 + (10 × 8) = 110 minutes. That is a hypothetical 10-minute advantage across ten runs, not a measured saving. Another 11 minutes of review or repair would reverse it. The comparison only matters if both methods reach the same acceptable quality and an authorized human checks the final work.
