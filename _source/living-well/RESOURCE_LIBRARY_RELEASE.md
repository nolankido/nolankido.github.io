# Living Well resource library and public-message review

Editorial date: October 9, 2026.

## Reader-facing scope

The owner requested a larger, thoughtfully organized resource collection and a positive, credible public presentation. The library grows from 18 to 66 selected entry points, adding 48 original annotations. Twelve collections now have their own pages, adding twelve useful subsections rather than one undifferentiated link list. Living Well has 53 pages, retaining 23 published readings, three explicitly proposed experiments, and all five unchanged worksheet text files.

Collections cover meaning and spiritual curiosity; attention and self-kindness; AI literacy; nature, science and wonder; relationships and memory; philosophy and decisions; religious literacy and particular traditions; free learning; books, art and creativity; digital privacy; practical tools; and community contribution. The twelve collections are grouped into four directions for initial browsing.

The main resource page offers a few immediate starting points, collection choices, and a compact complete index. Every earlier resource ID and shelf anchor remains usable. Full annotations live on the matching collection page, with requirements visible before the optional context panel. Original destinations remain directly accessible from the complete index. No JavaScript or account is needed on this site.

Each new collection has an original introduction, a suggested first selection, navigation within the collection, access conditions, perspective labels, contextual limits, and two related Living Well readings. Collection routes participate in the existing manifest, sitemap, metadata, internal-link checks and exact-byte live verifier. They are CollectionPage records, not falsely dated personal articles, and do not inflate the editorial RSS feed.

## Public message and personal boundaries

The public introductions now connect Technology, Living Well, Poker and Creative Work consistently. About introduces meaning, attention and everyday life; the short and long biographies describe the actual site and approved broad interests. Technology and Creative introductions replace outdated construction-language with a clear description of existing writing and resources. Creative Work links to the new books and art collection.

The intended impression is curious, practical, considerate and intellectually honest. This is an editorial aim, not a guarantee about every reader's response. No title, credential, employer, client, affiliation, achievement, spiritual identity, practice history or personal experience has been invented. Resource inclusion is not presented as personal use or endorsement of every view. Different spiritual and academic perspectives are named rather than treated as one universal doctrine.

The website's public page descriptions and main-content text were reviewed for positioning and unsupported personal claims. The review also inspected the public introductions, Living Well copy, and keyword matches in their actual context. Matches such as "expert" in an explanation example and "not a guarantee of profit" in Poker were not misleading personal claims; useful qualifications remain. This is a public-message review, not a fresh scientific or mathematical audit of every existing article or every external resource.

The shared Living Well disclosure is shorter and constructive while retaining AI assistance, hypothetical-example labels and separation from Nolan's lived experiences. The field-notes page still explicitly states that no completed personal field notes have been published. The contact, privacy and responsible-use boundaries remain intact. Honest qualifications were not removed to make the site look more impressive.

## Source selection and limitations

The resource registry identifies creators, original destinations, suggested starting points, free-access conditions, perspective and source-review scope. It includes public institutions, libraries, open educational resources, particular spiritual traditions, accessibility resources and established open-source projects. Optional paid features, equipment and library eligibility are identified where relevant. Free-to-read does not imply permission to republish.

Provider material and official source information were reviewed during this expansion. Access descriptions do not claim installed-app, logged-in, playback, security or clinical testing. Direct retrieval restrictions for the Smithsonian Transcription Center and Poetry Foundation are recorded in their review notes; official indexed descriptions support those selections. A former learning-resource domain was found parked and not selected: the current LearnFree destination is used. Retired Smithsonian and NASA paths were replaced with current identified entry points.

`python _scripts/check_living_well_links.py --output /tmp/living-well-links.json --report-only` performs bounded, read-only GET diagnostics. It reports transport failures, restrictions and host changes separately. It never rewrites the catalog or equates HTTP 200 with confirmed free access or editorial suitability. It is not added to the deterministic build or mandatory third-party-dependent CI gates.
