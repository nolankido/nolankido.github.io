# Living Well: reading expansion

Editorial date: October 9, 2026. Continues the approved free-content plan without waiting for personal stories, endorsements, or experiment results.

## Published content in this release

Five new pages:

- `/living-well/meditation-without-buying-a-lifestyle/`: a free practice path through UCLA web recordings, Greater Good in Action's outward-looking exercise, and Plum Village's explicitly Buddhist introductory collection. Access, context, and evidence limits are separated. No treatment promise or personal practice is claimed.
- `/living-well/consciousness-which-question/`: an introductory essay separating experience, explanation, philosophical disagreement, spiritual interpretation, and AI-consciousness assessment. Primary author material and a public research abstract are identified with their limits. No current commercial AI system is assessed.
- `/living-well/a-tool-you-can-put-down/`: an editorial argument about completion, learning, maintainability, and agency, with an explicit exception for continued accessibility and other useful support. It is not falsely presented as an empirical finding or a firsthand experiment.
- `/living-well/learning-with-ai-without-skipping-understanding/`: an optional, tool-neutral learning workflow using an original fictional workshop notice, illustrative answer, and independently checkable questions. The answer is not presented as an executed model test or an actual event.
- `/living-well/read-a-poem-without-a-lesson/`: a short reading note pointing to Amanda Gunn's Ordinary Sugar and the public Poetry Unbound transcript. No verse is reproduced and no personal favorite or listening experience is invented.

The existing `two-good-lives` essay receives a bounded hypothetical comparison between poker decisions and ordinary-life choices, including the limits of that comparison. The existing `quiet-morning` guide receives links to the free practice path and the poetry note. Their original publication dates and old section anchors are preserved, with explicit revision notes.

There are 41 Living Well pages, including 23 published readings and three separately labeled proposed experiments. The 18-resource collection remains selective; no additional resource cards or commercial services are introduced. Existing entries gain closer internal companion links.

## Reader organization

The Essays page separates reading paths, short reads, and longer essays. The Guides page introduces a free practice path before ordinary practical guides. The homepage features a short reading and the meditation path, with direct links to consciousness and the AI-learning example. New pages are also reachable through their subject pages, related reading, sitemap, and RSS. Existing URLs, primary navigation choices, and important anchors remain available.

New source-note blocks make the exact external source and its role visible beside each research-based piece. The content loader validates source structure, nonempty fields, dates, HTTPS URLs, and allowed formats. Rendered source text is escaped. Sources enter the article outline and reading-time estimate. The source shelf is not inflated into a second resource directory.

## Research and authorship limits

Public source pages, transcripts, metadata, and access descriptions were reviewed on October 9, 2026. The AI-consciousness discussion uses only the method stated in the public abstract of Butlin and colleagues' review, with its online-first and issue dates distinguished. The Stanford reference is identified as background, not a current product survey. Chalmers and Frankish are presented as authors of contrasting arguments, not interchangeable scientific evidence.

No app was installed and no media playback, course completion, clinical benefit, or third-party security audit is claimed. All examples remain fictional, and all personal field notes remain pending genuine source material. The user's private records, communications, family material, and project details were not used.
