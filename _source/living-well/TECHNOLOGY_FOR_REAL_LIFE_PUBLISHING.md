# Technology for Real Life: future article and video method

Prepared October 10, 2026. Internal editorial guide, not a published account of Nolan's experiences.

## Purpose

Show what a tool contributes to a real human activity. Start with a situation and finish with a result a reader can inspect or use. The device or model is part of the explanation, not automatically the hero of the story. A worthwhile result might be less repetitive administration, a plan everyone can understand, easier participation, a piece of creative expression, or a more considered choice.

Spiritual inquiry, attention, care, and ordinary enjoyment remain purposes in their own right. Do not claim that saved minutes measure the value of a relationship or a spiritual practice. Ongoing accessibility support can be valuable without ever making itself unnecessary.

## Four distinct evidence labels

**Worked guide:** An editorial method with a plainly labeled fictional input and illustrative output. It does not establish that the process was executed, that a model followed a prompt, or that the author benefited.

**Recorded demonstration:** An actual execution using synthetic or approved input. Record the date, tool/version, settings that matter, unedited starting material, observed output and corrections. Do not generalize from one successful attempt.

**Firsthand field note:** Nolan's actual supplied account of a situation and what happened. Confirm his words and the boundaries of any other person's participation before publishing. Document inconvenience, costs, failed attempts and uncertainty as readily as useful results.

**Research explanation:** A carefully scoped account of a source, with author, date, method and limitations. Provider documentation can establish what a feature is described as doing, not that using it improves a reader's life.

Changing a label requires the corresponding new evidence. A generated draft never becomes a firsthand report through a change in pronouns.

## Reusable article structure

1. **The situation.** One ordinary difficulty and why addressing it matters. No theatrical crisis or invented personal opening.
2. **The finish line.** A concrete result: one retrievable document, one confirmed plan, or one draft ready for a specified audience.
3. **The simpler alternative.** What works with paper, existing tools, or another person. Record the previous approach before replacing it.
4. **The starting material.** Show a small approved input. Describe omissions and redactions rather than implying the example is complete when it is not.
5. **The tool's contribution.** Identify exactly which step receives help. Distinguish dictation, search, display adjustments and automation from generative inference.
6. **What needed checking.** Dates, names, conditions, source fidelity, access permissions, document versions and changes of meaning. Include corrections.
7. **The result in use.** Can the intended person open it, understand it and act on it? A tidy screenshot alone is not proof that the task is complete.
8. **The full cost.** Setup, input preparation, checking, transferring, maintaining, cash cost and work created for other people. Separate measured numbers from illustrative arithmetic.
9. **The human consequence.** What became possible outside the tool, as actually observed. A clearer plan or easier reading may matter without a time-saving claim.
10. **The limited conclusion.** Keep, simplify, stop, or still unsure. State who may need a different approach and what this one example cannot establish.

An optional closing question is enough. Do not require every reader to submit a journal or begin a tracking routine.

## Reusable short-video structure

Open by naming the task and showing the intended result. Show the original material, a simpler option, the tool-assisted step, at least one real verification step, and the finished result. End with the tradeoff rather than a sweeping product verdict. A short screen demonstration or narrated sequence can accompany the article, but neither is required before the guide is useful.

Keep a written transcript and a text-first companion page with the exact instructions and source links. Provide captions and descriptive narration where the visual content matters. Do not publish fake screen recordings, invented conversations, or synthetic imagery as documentation of an actual event. Any conceptual illustration must be identified as such.

## Capture and permission checklist

Use synthetic or explicitly approved material. Before recording, close unrelated tabs, hide notifications and remove passwords, tokens, private file names, addresses and identifiable details. Check the exported media again; an overlooked frame can reveal information that was absent from the script. Redact before capture wherever possible rather than relying only on later editing.

Agree separately on permission to record, send content to a third-party service, edit, quote and publish. Confirm the actual version and audience. A person's willingness to discuss a subject does not authorize impersonation, voice cloning, broad publication or unrelated reuse.

Do not open private inboxes, calendars, journals or family files for the sake of content production without express permission for the exact purpose. Do not treat publicly accessible third-party material as permission to republish it in full.

## Source packet before publication

Keep the actual account or demonstration notes, dates and software versions, approved input/output, sources read, original and corrected result, permissions, costs, unanswered questions and final approval. Only claim checks that were really performed. Record whether the conclusion is observation, arithmetic, opinion or research.

The seven published templates support planning and documentation; they are not proof of outcomes. The existing three proposed experiments retain their status until someone actually completes them and supplies a defensible account.

## Starting sequence, not a promised schedule

The most straightforward future demonstrations are notes into a next action, a shared plan, a retrievable document and a small creative draft. Reflection and reading-access pieces deserve particular care about privacy and individual needs. There is no promised posting frequency and no need to manufacture a result to fill a content slot.

Use the existing source catalog and review workflow. Preserve stable public URLs, mark actual revisions, recheck volatile product instructions against official sources, build and test, then verify deployment. Do not add visitor uploads, intimate-answer forms or extra tracking merely to make the collection interactive.
