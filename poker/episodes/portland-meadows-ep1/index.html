Portland Meadows: From 6.5 Big Blinds to a Trophy

Published November 2, 2026 · Revised November 4, 2026 · Nolan Kido · Public video companion

From 6.5 big blinds to a trophy.

In this Portland Meadows High Noon Deepstack episode, I return from the second break with 65,000 chips, only 6.5 big blinds, and 26 players still in. The video follows the short-stack recovery, two consequential all-ins, and an unexpected official first-place trophy after a four-way deal.

A quick evidence ledger for the two decisive hands.

6-4 offsuit near the bubble
As I recalled it: 13 left, 10 paid; small blind shove with 6-4 offsuit, called by A-K suited. At a reported 148,000 stack and 15,000 big blind, the conditional pre-hand calculation is about 9.87 BB. Individual suits, complete board, exact stack timing and independent opponent ranges remain unknown.
Pocket sevens four-handed
As I recalled it: big blind with 7-7, button opens to 75,000 at a 30,000 big blind, I reshove, button calls with A-T offsuit, and the sevens hold. A reported 585,000 stack is 19.5 BB, and the button's raise-to amount is 2.5 BB. Those are arithmetic checks, not a solver or ICM verdict; full board and effective stacks remain unresolved.
Advertised prize versus received payment
The original advertised first prize was $5,100; I reported receiving $2,900 after the four-way deal. That is a gross reported payout, not a claim of net profit or an independently audited settlement.

The tournament's turning points.

This is a topic map from the reviewed version 7 narration, not a set of chapter timestamps for the newer public upload. Use the current public episode to watch; exact offsets in that cut are not verified here.

  1. 6.5 big blinds and 26 players left. At the second break, about 4:15 PM, the reported stack was 65,000 with a 10,000 big blind and a 10,000 big blind ante.
  2. Uncalled shoves and a short-stack recovery. My earlier narration describes moving from 65,000 to roughly 148,000 before the bubble without a called all-in.
  3. 6-4 offsuit against A-K suited. At 13 remaining with 10 paid, I shoved from the small blind, was called, and won after a four appeared. The opponent's calling range is not independently known.
  4. Pocket sevens against A-T offsuit. Four-handed, I reshoved over the button's raise and won when called.
  5. A four-way deal and the trophy. I reported receiving $2,900 while the advertised top prize was $5,100. Those are distinct amounts.
  6. Recap and a following event. The earlier version teased a Survivor tournament. This list does not establish a current video's exact chapter order or subsequent publication.

Thinking reported in the video

I describe trying to pressure an opponent who might call tighter near the money bubble. I characterize the big blind as tight-aggressive and say I believed his calling range was tighter than a generic baseline. This is my post-tournament description of the read, not an independently timestamped thought recorded during the hand. The opponent's actual A-K suited does not establish his complete calling range.

What later analysis can establish

I was called by a strong hand and caught a four. The favorable result does not prove the shove was good, just as losing would not prove it was bad. At a reported 9.87 BB, blind pressure matters, but so do the big blind's effective stack, calling frequencies, payout structure, and risk of elimination. My statement that I would make this shove again is a personal judgment, not a verified solver result or a universal any-two-card recommendation.

Unresolved inputs: exact effective stack, complete board, precise pot and ante contributions, all payouts, independent opponent range evidence and a reproducible solver configuration. Those gaps prevent a credible numerical chip-EV or ICM verdict. The stack-to-blind division is arithmetic, not a profitability estimate.

Hand 2: pocket sevens four-handed.

Big blind with pocket sevens; no suit details are required or reported
Action
Cutoff folds; button raises to 75,000 total, or 2.5 BB; small blind folds; I shove; button calls with A-T offsuit
Outcome
My sevens hold. I report doubling and taking the chip lead, without a complete board in the account

Alternatives described in the narration

I describe the button as aggressive and consider calling, making a smaller raise or moving all-in. I say I disliked calling out of position because overcards often make later streets difficult for a medium pair. That account was narrated afterward; no contemporaneous written decision record is available.

Review without knowing the winner

The 75,000 open is 2.5 BB, and my stated 585,000 stack is approximately 19.5 BB. A reshove can succeed when the button folds and can retain showdown equity when called. Which action has the greatest tournament value depends on the button's actual opening and calling ranges, other players' stacks, the short small-blind stack and the payout ladder. Holding against A-T offsuit demonstrates the actual outcome, not that my shove was theoretically best.

Unresolved inputs: exact remaining stacks, full payouts and prior chip contributions, board cards, button frequencies and a verified ICM or solver solution. At the last break I later report about 1.4 million chips with a 60,000 big blind, approximately 23.3 BB. A four-way deal follows, with my reported $2,900 payment; the advertised $5,100 first prize was not the amount I report receiving.

Follow-up study: How does a 19.5-BB reshove change if the button opens widely but calls tightly, with another player much shorter? Review this through the tournament short-stack guide and private hand-review worksheet, with assumptions recorded explicitly.
