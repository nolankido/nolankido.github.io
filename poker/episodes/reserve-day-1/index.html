Seven Big Blinds and a Three-Way A-K Pot at the Reserve

Published November 18, 2026 · Nolan Kido · Archived-source NolanPoker video companion

Seven big blinds near the money, then an A-K three-way pot.

At the 2026 WSOP Circuit stop at The Reserve Poker Club in Toledo, I reported having about seven big blinds with 94 players left and 80 paid. I later made the money and built to roughly 28 big blinds, then reshoved A-K into an existing all-in. The big blind came along short, I lost both pots, and about ten uncalled big blinds came back to me. The final result was 63rd place and an $800 gross cash. This companion separates that narrated sequence, independently listed event results and the calculations a reader can actually check.

At seven big blinds, the money bubble changed the question.

In the archived narration, I said I had about 7 BB with 94 left and 80 paid. That is 14 eliminations away from the money, not fourteen players from the field size. My later account recalls about 5 BB in the big blind with two players remaining before the cash line. Those are two different points in the episode, not a single reconstructed hand.

A-K after the bubble: what did the action actually say?

By the main A-K hand I reported already being in the money with about 28 BB on the button. The lowjack opened to 2 BB, the hijack called, and the cutoff moved all-in for approximately 18 BB. I held A♣K♥ according to the archived hand graphic. I reshoved for roughly 28 BB. The big blind then entered all-in for less, approximately 7 BB; the lowjack folded and the hijack asked for a count before folding. Three players remained all-in: me on the button, the cutoff and the big blind.

  1. Before my action: lowjack 2 BB open, hijack call, cutoff all-in around 18 BB, me on the button holding archived-graphic A♣K♥ and roughly 28 BB.

  2. My choice: call around 18 BB or reshove to around 28 BB. I chose the reshove. My stated reason was that I wanted certain hands behind, such as 8-8 or J-T suited, to fold. That is my contemporaneous reasoning about ranges, not evidence those exact hands were present or would fold.

  3. The action changed: the big blind entered for less, around 7 BB; lowjack and hijack folded. This made a three-way main pot and a separate side pot between the two larger stacks.

  4. Hands described in the archived account: my A♣K♥, cutoff Q-J offsuit and big blind pocket nines. The cutoff and big-blind suit combinations were not recorded in the v8 hand graphic; no specific suits are claimed for them.

My narration says I preferred isolating the original cutoff shove, but the later big-blind all-in meant isolation did not occur. It does not follow that the reshove was theoretically optimal. Exact effective stacks, the real action ranges, the full blind and ante ledger and any remaining payout jumps are needed for a meaningful chip-EV or ICM comparison.

Why did I get about 10 BB back after losing A-K?

Short answer: my approximate 28 BB reshove covered the cutoff's approximate 18 BB all-in. The extra 28 − 18 = 10 BB was not called by anyone and was returned to me. Losing the contested main pot and side pot did not put the uncalled chips at risk. The reported fall from around 28 BB to around 10 BB reflects that refund, not a ten-blind consolation prize or a pot I won.

The last call: 9♦10♦ and roughly four and a half blinds.

After the A-K pot I said I was card-dead for a couple of orbits. In the final recalled hand, I held 9♦10♦ in the big blind with about 4.5 BB. The button made a large open to around 4 BB. I called all-in, described pairing up while the opponent made a queen, and was eliminated. My account does not give the opponent's exact hole cards, the complete board, the posted-ante stack convention or a full betting transcript.

I wanted a deep run and described the call as appealing at the table. That motivation and a single observed result cannot establish the best response to a button open. Three questions to study after watching.

Was the 9-7 offsuit big-blind fold automatically correct because the money bubble was close?

No verified verdict. The reported approximately 5 BB, button raise to 3 BB and proximity to the money frame the choice. Full stacks, posting conventions, payout jumps, button frequencies and actual hand equity are absent. A tournament bubble can change risk premiums, but one fact does not supply a solved calling range.

Did my A-K reshove guarantee that I would be heads-up against the cutoff?

No. The big blind could still act and entered all-in for less. The lowjack and hijack folded, so three players contested the main pot. I chose a reshove partly to discourage calls behind; the actual action tree included another caller.

If my 28 BB covers a cutoff's 18 BB and the third player has 7 BB, how much of my shove can be returned?

About 10 BB. No one matches my contribution above 18. At the level of rounded input stacks, the first 7 BB per player form a three-way layer; the next 11 BB per larger stack form a side layer; my unmatched ten returns. It is not an award from winning either pot. Folded chips and exact ante and blind accounting remain separate.
