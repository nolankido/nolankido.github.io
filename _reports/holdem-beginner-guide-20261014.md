# Beginner Hold’em guide: editorial and integration review

The supplied research essay is published as a comprehensive Poker guide at `/poker/no-limit-holdem-guide/`. Its broad scope complements the shorter viewer explanation and the four foundation lessons. Those lessons, the range and betting lessons, and the six-skill checkpoint remain available.

## Editorial findings and changes

- Corrected the review example that described a $90 pot before a $30 bet but treated the final pot as $120. The revised example uses $60 before the bet, $90 after it, and $120 after calling. It separately explains that $90 before the same bet gives a $150 final pot and a 20% price.
- Retained the complete fictional hand, reconciled pots of $19, $39, $87 and $167, and separated the $167 receipt from the hero’s $87 net gain after an $80 contribution. Calling and bet sizes are illustrated actions, not validated optimal choices.
- Specified legal suits for the paired-board comparison. Independently checked the supplied showdowns and the three new practice questions with the repository’s separate five-card evaluator.
- Distinguished an expected pot share from an outright win probability, including a hypothetical tie example. Distinguished completing a nine-out flush draw from winning. Explained the unknown-card model, possible dirty outs, and the cost of future streets.
- Recalculated the draw, call-price, EV, bluff and SPR examples. The $3.48 EV remains explicitly conditional on exactly nine winning cards, no ties, no fees and no further betting.
- Explained short all-ins player by player, retained the last full raise increment, and added the treatment of unmatched excess chips.
- Replaced inaccessible research citation tokens and unrendered diagram/math syntax with native HTML, tables, simple text formulas and eleven verified primary or operator source links. Checked source pages October 14, 2026, including TDA 2026 version 1.1 dated September 21.
- Scoped DeepStack to heads-up no-limit Hold’em and AIVAT to agent evaluation. Neither paper establishes the effectiveness of these lessons for beginners.
- Connected three native, initially closed checks to the existing lesson repair anchors. Readers can use them without JavaScript, an account, a wager or submission of answers.
- Used the shared Poker glossary rather than publishing a second competing glossary. The guide has thirteen major chapters and sixty-two named subsections, with a native outline and direct links to the price examples.
- Preserved disclosure that the supplied research draft and its revision used AI assistance. The upload remains unchanged.

## Validation and limits

The release gate includes 591 unit/content tests and 45 browser suites. The new browser suite checks 320, 390, 768 and 1,440 pixel viewports, keyboard reading and answers, seven incoming routes, a search-to-section arrival, a repair link, text-spacing reflow, accessibility, print and no-JavaScript use. Candidate browser previews and the exact tested commit are recorded in the pull request receipt before publication.

Build, HTML5 parsing, metadata, internal links and fragments, independent card comparisons, arithmetic, exact predecessor restoration, and the lossless search decoder are checked. The full predecessor fixture pins all 179 prior pages, 206 generated outputs and 414 source/script/asset/download files. Its inverse accepts only complete reviewed digests; unreviewed edits remain visible to historical checks. Older golden digests are unchanged.

The completed site has 180 public pages, 99 Article records, 175 sitemap URLs, 55 Poker guides and 420 live byte-verification targets. Publication is complete only after the tested tree is merged, Pages deployment succeeds and the current-main live checker confirms the exact published bytes. Actual results and identifiers belong in the pull request receipt.

No real beginner was recruited, observed or assessed during this edit. Browser automation checks the reading interface, not comprehension. The earlier reader-observation protocol and optional checkpoint feedback still provide the route for learning where actual people get stuck. More content alone is not evidence that the lessons work better.
