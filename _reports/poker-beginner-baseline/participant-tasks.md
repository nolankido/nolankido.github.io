# Follow-up problems: participant view

Draft pending qualified human review. Show only the assigned section.
All situations are fictional. Counters have no monetary value. No wager is required.
Explain your reasoning; it is fine to say what you do not know.

## T1

On the flop, the other remaining player bets 9 counters. You paid 8 before the flop and nothing on this flop. There are no all-ins or side pots.

Your private cards: ace of spades (As), jack of clubs (Jc).

Shared board: ace of diamonds (Ad), eight of spades (8s), four of clubs (4c).

Name your current best five cards. Can you check now? How many more counters would calling require, and why?

## T2

Six seats in clockwise order are first, next, cutoff, button, small blind, big blind. You are the big blind. Blinds are 1 and 2. First and next fold; cutoff raises to 8 total; button calls 8; small blind folds. You posted 2 and have not yet taken your initial turn. Assume enough counters, no all-ins or side pots.

Your private cards: queen of clubs (Qc), jack of clubs (Jc).

Shared board: not dealt yet.

Does posting the big blind count as your initial decision? What do you add to call 8? After you call and these three players see the flop, who acts first? Explain.

In a different hand with the same private cards, seats and blinds, cutoff raises to 12, button calls 12 and small blind folds. You have posted 2 as the big blind. What would you add to call then, and why?

## T3

On the flop, the other remaining player bets 6 counters. You have paid nothing on this flop. There are no all-ins or side pots.

Your private cards: nine of spades (9s), eight of spades (8s).

Shared board: nine of diamonds (9d), six of spades (6s), two of spades (2s).

Describe your current best five and any spade draw. What might another spade change, and what would it still not establish? Can you check in response to this bet?

## T4

On the river, the pot held 40 counters before the other remaining player bet 20. You have paid nothing on the river. There are no fees, all-ins, side pots, further bets or prize effects.

Your private cards: king of spades (Ks), jack of spades (Js).

Shared board: king of hearts (Kh), ten of spades (Ts), four of spades (4s), three of diamonds (3d), eight of clubs (8c).

Name your final best five. What does calling cost now, and what would the pot contain after your call? Calculate the break-even share of that final pot. Does that price establish whether calling is profitable? Explain what remains unknown.

## T5

On the river the other remaining player checks. Consider the three candidate holdings below; no probabilities or response frequencies are supplied.

Your private cards: ace of diamonds (Ad), queen of clubs (Qc).

Shared board: queen of diamonds (Qd), eight of spades (8s), five of clubs (5c), three of hearts (3h), two of diamonds (2d).

Candidate 1: ace of diamonds (Ad), jack of hearts (Jh).

Candidate 2: king of hearts (Kh), king of diamonds (Kd).

Candidate 3: jack of clubs (Jc), ten of clubs (Tc).

Which candidates are physically possible, and which possible ones beat your current hand? Does the check exclude the stronger candidate? If one weaker candidate is revealed once, what does that tell you about how often weaker hands occur next time?

## T6

On the river the other remaining player checks. For this fictional question only, assume both candidate holdings below would call a proposed bet of 6. Their relative frequencies and all other responses are unknown.

Your private cards: ten of diamonds (Td), nine of diamonds (9d).

Shared board: ten of clubs (Tc), eight of clubs (8c), five of spades (5s), three of hearts (3h), queen of hearts (Qh).

Candidate 1: nine of clubs (9c), nine of hearts (9h).

Candidate 2: queen of spades (Qs), jack of hearts (Jh).

Which candidate helps the value-bet argument, and why? Does knowing it would call establish that betting is better than checking? What else is needed? If the stronger candidate calls and wins this time, does that result alone settle whether the bet was well reasoned?
