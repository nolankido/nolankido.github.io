## T1 / S1 / first-decisions

1. Pair of aces with jack, eight and four; exactly five available cards.
2. Checking is unavailable while facing this unmatched bet; a legal response includes folding or calling.
3. Calling adds 9, not 1 or 17; the 8 paid before the flop is from a different betting round.

Checked rank: one pair; numeric tie-break vector [14, 11, 8, 4] (2 to 14, ace high).

Checked call: 9 minus 0 = 9 additional.

## T2 / S2 / starting-hand-decisions

1. Posting is not the initial decision; you still have that turn after the small blind folds.
2. Call adds 6 for a total of 8; the alternate raise requires 10 more. The same private cards do not fix the price.
3. You, the big blind, act first postflop among big blind, cutoff and button, clockwise after the button; this was a six-player deal.

Checked call: 8 minus 2 = 6 additional.

Checked alternate_call: 12 minus 2 = 10 additional.

## T3 / S3 / first-flop-decisions

1. Pair of nines with eight, six and two, with four available spades; not a completed flush.
2. Another spade would make a flush available, but improvement does not guarantee winning against every possible opposing hand or later board.
3. Cannot check past an unmatched bet; the observed bet changes the available response, without proving the opponent's cards.

Checked rank: one pair; numeric tie-break vector [9, 8, 6, 2] (2 to 14, ace high).

## T4 / S4 / turn-and-river-decisions

1. Pair of kings with jack, ten and eight; four spades do not complete a flush.
2. Call costs 20; current pot 60 becomes 80; 20/80 = 25% break-even pot share under the stated assumptions.
3. Price is not estimated equity. Opposing holdings/frequencies and ties affect expected pot share; without them there is no automatic profitable-call conclusion.

Checked rank: one pair; numeric tie-break vector [13, 11, 10, 8] (2 to 14, ace high).

Checked call: 20 minus 0 = 20 additional.

Checked river: 40 + 20 = 60 before calling; 80 after calling; price 1/4 = 25%.

## T5 / S5 / ranges-for-beginners

1. Candidate 1 is impossible because you hold the ace of diamonds; candidate 2's kings beat your queens; candidate 3's queen-high loses.
2. A check alone does not logically exclude kings or establish a holding or its probability.
3. One compatible weaker reveal does not establish future weaker-hand frequencies or justify assuming all checks are weak.

Checked rank: one pair; numeric tie-break vector [12, 14, 8, 5] (2 to 14, ace high).

Checked candidate order: impossible, stronger, weaker.

## T6 / S6 / betting-with-a-purpose

1. The nines are worse than your tens and would call by the stated assumption, so they are a relevant value target; the queens are stronger.
2. A possible worse caller is not enough: need frequencies, other calls/folds/raises, and comparison with checking; no unique optimal action follows.
3. A loss to the stronger candidate does not alone grade the prior reasoning; distinguish known assumptions from unknown frequencies and one outcome.

Checked rank: one pair; numeric tie-break vector [10, 12, 9, 8] (2 to 14, ace high).

Checked candidate order: weaker, stronger.
