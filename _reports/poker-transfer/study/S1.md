### S1-A: Flop wager after a preflop payment

In a fictional heads-up flop, you paid 5 counters preflop. On the flop you have paid zero. The other player bets 7. Both players have at least 30 counters behind at this decision, with no all-ins or side pots.

Private cards: Kc, 7h. Shared cards: Ks, 9d, 3c.

Private card names: king of clubs; seven of hearts. Shared card names: king of spades; nine of diamonds; three of clubs.

**Try without opening an answer:** Name your best five now. Which actions are available in response to this bet? How many new counters would a call require? Explain how, if at all, the earlier payment affects that amount.

**Private note:** First explanation: ______. Confidence: sure / partly sure / guessing. Prompt or help received: ______. Unclear step: ______.

<details>
<summary>Compare your explanation</summary>

1. Your five cards form one pair of kings, with nine, seven and three as kickers.

2. You cannot check while facing an unmatched bet; folding, calling or a legal raise are the available action families.

3. The call costs 7 on this street. The five paid preflop is already in the pot, not credit for the flop.

**Likely misconception:** Treating earlier-street chips as credit on this street.

### S1-B: Big blind facing a raise

Six players begin a new hand with 1/3 blinds. You posted the big blind of 3 and no other chips. A player makes a legal raise to 11 total. You still have enough counters and no other bets or all-ins intervene.

Private cards: 6h, 6c. Shared cards: not dealt.

Private card names: six of hearts; six of clubs.

**Try without opening an answer:** How many extra counters would you put in to call? Is checking an option at this point? Explain the difference between the total wager and what you must add. Would different private cards change that call price?

**Private note:** First explanation: ______. Confidence: sure / partly sure / guessing. Prompt or help received: ______. Unclear step: ______.

<details>
<summary>Compare your explanation</summary>

1. The big blind's 3 already counts on this preflop round, so calling adds 8 and reaches 11 total.

2. Checking is not an option against a raise that exceeds your current contribution.

3. The hand cards do not determine the price. The current wager and what you paid on this round do.

**Likely misconception:** Adding the full raise total twice or checking against an unmatched wager.

### S1-C: An ante and a raised pot

In a fictional six-player hand, blinds are 2/4 and each player also posts a separate ante of 1. You are the small blind and have posted those two required payments. An earlier player raises to 9 total. It is now your turn, with no intervening raise. Everyone has at least 60 counters behind; there are no all-ins or side pots.

Private cards: Jd, 8s. Shared cards: not dealt.

Private card names: jack of diamonds; eight of spades.

**Try without opening an answer:** How many new counters would calling add? Explain which of your earlier payments counts toward matching 9. Can you check here, and what are the available response families?

**Private note:** First explanation: ______. Confidence: sure / partly sure / guessing. Prompt or help received: ______. Unclear step: ______.

<details>
<summary>Compare your explanation</summary>

1. Calling adds 7 counters: the current wager of 9 minus your small blind of 2.

2. The separate ante is already in the pot but does not count toward the preflop wager; it cannot reduce the call to 6.

3. You face an unmatched wager, so checking is unavailable; you may fold, call or make a legal raise.

**Likely misconception:** Subtracting an ante from the additional call as though it were part of the live blind.

**Starting repair:** https://nolankido.com/poker/first-decisions/#chips-owed

### S1-D: A preflop turn in the big blind

In a fictional six-player hand with 1/3 blinds and no ante, you posted the big blind of 3. The first player calls 3, the next two players fold, the button calls 3 and the small blind folds. Every remaining player has at least 80 counters behind; no raise or all-in has occurred.

Private cards: Qs, 8d. Shared cards: not dealt.

Private card names: queen of spades; eight of diamonds.

**Try without opening an answer:** How many extra counters are needed to match the current wager? Name the actions that keep your hand live, including what an increase above 3 would be called. Did posting your blind already use up this voluntary decision?

**Private note:** First explanation: ______. Confidence: sure / partly sure / guessing. Prompt or help received: ______. Unclear step: ______.

<details>
<summary>Compare your explanation</summary>

1. You owe zero extra counters because your posted 3 already matches the live preflop wager.

2. You may check or make a legal raise; increasing the live big-blind wager is a raise, even though nobody raised earlier.

3. Posting the forced blind did not use up your opportunity to act, so the dealer must wait for your decision.

**Likely misconception:** Treating a matched blind as a completed voluntary turn, or calling its increase a bet.

### S1-E: A response to your flop bet

Only you and one opponent remain in this fictional hand with 1/2 blinds. On the flop you act first and bet 5. The opponent raises to 16 total. Both players have at least 80 counters behind at your next decision; the raise is a full legal raise, with no all-ins or side pots.

Private cards: 7s, 7d. Shared cards: Ac, 9h, 2d.

Private card names: seven of spades; seven of diamonds. Shared card names: ace of clubs; nine of hearts; two of diamonds.

**Try without opening an answer:** How many new counters would a call add, and what would your total flop contribution become? Does your original bet count toward that amount? Name your available responses, including whether checking is possible.

**Private note:** First explanation: ______. Confidence: sure / partly sure / guessing. Prompt or help received: ______. Unclear step: ______.

<details>
<summary>Compare your explanation</summary>

1. Calling adds 11 counters, bringing your total contribution on this flop from 5 to 16.

2. Your original 5 counts because it was paid on this same betting round; the announced 16 is a total, not 16 more.

3. The raise creates a new unmatched wager for you, so you may fold, call or legally reraise, but cannot check.

**Likely misconception:** Adding the entire raise total again or assuming that an earlier bet removes the need for another decision.

### S1-F: The action returns on the flop

Two players remain in this fictional hand with 1/2 blinds. On the flop you act first and check. Your opponent then bets 9. Both players have at least 70 counters behind when the action returns to you; there are no all-ins or side pots.

Private cards: Kd, Qh. Shared cards: Ks, 6c, 2h.

Private card names: king of diamonds; queen of hearts. Shared card names: king of spades; six of clubs; two of hearts.

**Try without opening an answer:** What responses are now available? Did your earlier check surrender the ability to raise this bet? How many new counters would calling require, and did checking contribute anything toward that price?

**Private note:** First explanation: ______. Confidence: sure / partly sure / guessing. Prompt or help received: ______. Unclear step: ______.

<details>
<summary>Compare your explanation</summary>

1. Facing the bet, your response families are fold, call or legal raise; another check cannot match it.

2. An earlier check keeps the hand live and does not surrender the right to make a legal raise when a later bet returns the action.

3. Calling adds 9 counters because you contributed zero on this flop; a check was not a payment.

**Likely misconception:** Treating a check as either a contribution or a promise never to raise on that street.

### S1-G: A river with a paired board

In this fictional hand with 1/2 blinds, two players reach the river. The opponent acts first and checks. You have not acted on the river. Both players have at least 50 counters behind; no river wager, all-in or side pot exists.

Private cards: Ac, 4h. Shared cards: As, Kd, Kh, 9s, 2c.

Private card names: ace of clubs; four of hearts. Shared card names: ace of spades; king of diamonds; king of hearts; nine of spades; two of clubs.

**Try without opening an answer:** List your best five cards and say how many private cards are used. Must your other private card be included? How many counters are owed now, and which actions can keep your hand live?

**Private note:** First explanation: ______. Confidence: sure / partly sure / guessing. Prompt or help received: ______. Unclear step: ______.

<details>
<summary>Compare your explanation</summary>

1. Your best five are two aces, two kings and the nine: two pair, aces and kings, with a nine kicker.

2. Only your private ace is used; the shared nine is a better fifth card than your four, and hold'em does not require both private cards.

3. You owe zero counters and may check or make a legal bet; keeping your hand live does not require betting.

**Likely misconception:** Forcing both private cards into the hand or believing a player must bet to keep a checked-to hand live.

### S1-H: Three players on the flop

This fictional hand with 1/2 blinds has three players left: you in the small blind, the big blind and the button. Each began the flop with 200 counters. You check, the big blind bets 4, the button raises to 12, and you call 12. The big blind then makes a full legal raise to 30, and the button calls 30. It is your turn again. There are no all-ins or side pots.

Private cards: Ah, Jh. Shared cards: Ac, 8d, 3s.

Private card names: ace of hearts; jack of hearts. Shared card names: ace of clubs; eight of diamonds; three of spades.

**Try without opening an answer:** How many extra counters would calling now add? Explain why your earlier call did or did not finish your decisions on this round. Can you check now, and what responses remain available?

**Private note:** First explanation: ______. Confidence: sure / partly sure / guessing. Prompt or help received: ______. Unclear step: ______.

<details>
<summary>Compare your explanation</summary>

1. You have paid 12 on the flop and face a total of 30, so a call adds 18 counters.

2. The later full raise returned action to you; having already called once did not finish the betting round.

3. You may fold, call or make a legal reraise, but cannot check while 18 remains unmatched.

**Likely misconception:** Paying only the first call or overlooking a new decision created by a later full raise.

### S1-I: A preflop raise to 10

A fictional six-player hand uses 1/3 blinds and no ante. The first player raises to 10 total; the next two players fold. You are on the button, have paid nothing preflop, and now have the turn. Both blinds remain. All players still in the hand have at least 70 counters behind, and no short all-in or other raise has occurred.

Private cards: Kd, Ts. Shared cards: not dealt.

Private card names: king of diamonds; ten of spades.

**Try without opening an answer:** What would calling cost? Name the available response families and say whether checking is one of them. If you choose a full reraise, what is the smallest legal new total and how do you calculate it?

**Private note:** First explanation: ______. Confidence: sure / partly sure / guessing. Prompt or help received: ______. Unclear step: ______.

<details>
<summary>Compare your explanation</summary>

1. Calling costs 10 new counters because you have paid nothing on this preflop round.

2. Facing the raise, you may fold, call or make a legal reraise; checking cannot match the wager.

3. The raise from 3 to 10 increased the wager by 7, so a full reraise must reach at least 17 total, not merely add another big blind.

**Likely misconception:** Using the big blind as the minimum reraise increment after a larger full raise.

### S1-J: Five shared cards and a river bet

Only you and one opponent remain on the river in this fictional hand with 1/2 blinds. The opponent acts first and bets 8. You have paid nothing on the river. Both players have at least 60 counters behind; there are no all-ins or side pots.

Private cards: 4c, 2d. Shared cards: Js, Tc, 9d, 8h, 7s.

Private card names: four of clubs; two of diamonds. Shared card names: jack of spades; ten of clubs; nine of diamonds; eight of hearts; seven of spades.

**Try without opening an answer:** List your best five cards and the number of private cards they use. Can an unused private card break a tie between identical best-five hands? Which responses are available to this bet, and how much would a call add?

**Private note:** First explanation: ______. Confidence: sure / partly sure / guessing. Prompt or help received: ______. Unclear step: ______.

<details>
<summary>Compare your explanation</summary>

1. Your best five are the shared jack, ten, nine, eight and seven, a jack-high straight using zero private cards.

2. An unused private card cannot break a tie between identical best-five hands; there is no sixth-card tiebreaker.

3. Playing the board does not remove the wager: calling adds 8, and the response families are fold, call or legal raise, not check.

**Likely misconception:** Adding a private-card tiebreaker or treating a shared straight as an exemption from responding to a bet.

### S1-L: A flop bet and a caller

This fictional hand with 1/2 blinds has three players left: the big blind, the cutoff and you on the button. Each began the flop with 100 counters. The big blind bets 6 and the cutoff calls 6. You have contributed nothing on the flop, and it is your turn. There are no all-ins or side pots.

Private cards: 9s, 6d. Shared cards: Qc, 9c, 4h.

Private card names: nine of spades; six of diamonds. Shared card names: queen of clubs; nine of clubs; four of hearts.

**Try without opening an answer:** How much does calling add, and should the other caller's payment be added to your price? What responses are available? If you only call, does that finish this flop round, and does it pay for all later cards?

**Private note:** First explanation: ______. Confidence: sure / partly sure / guessing. Prompt or help received: ______. Unclear step: ______.

<details>
<summary>Compare your explanation</summary>

1. Calling adds 6 counters, not 12; each player matches the current wager rather than the sum of other players' contributions.

2. Your response families are fold, call or legal raise; checking cannot match the bet.

3. A call here completes the flop round, but later streets can create new wagers and new costs before showdown.

**Likely misconception:** Adding every opponent payment to the call price or treating a completed flop call as a ticket to every remaining card.
