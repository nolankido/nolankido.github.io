### S6-A: A checked river and two possible callers

On the river, your opponent checks. In this fictional comparison only, assume both example holdings below would call a proposed bet of 5.

Private cards: Qs, Qh. Shared cards: Kd, 9c, 5h, 3s, 2c.

Private card names: queen of spades; queen of hearts. Shared card names: king of diamonds; nine of clubs; five of hearts; three of spades; two of clubs.

Candidate 1: Jd, Jh.

Candidate 1 card names: jack of diamonds; jack of hearts.

Candidate 2: Ks, 6d.

Candidate 2 card names: king of spades; six of diamonds.

**Try without opening an answer:** Which candidate supports a value-bet explanation? Which would be ahead of you? Does the existence of one weaker caller prove the bet earns more than checking? Explain what else you would need to compare betting with checking.

**Private note:** First explanation: ______. Confidence: sure / partly sure / guessing. Prompt or help received: ______. Unclear step: ______.

<details>
<summary>Compare your explanation</summary>

1. Jd-Jh is a worse pair of jacks and is assumed to call, so it is a possible value target.

2. Ks-6d makes a pair of kings, ahead of your queens, and is also assumed to call.

3. Without frequencies and other possible responses, the comparison of betting with checking is underdetermined; no profitable-bet claim follows.

**Likely misconception:** Concluding that one worse caller guarantees a good value bet.

Candidate 1: Kh, Kd.

Candidate 1 card names: king of hearts; king of diamonds.

Candidate 2: 9c, 9s.

Candidate 2 card names: nine of clubs; nine of spades.

**Try without opening an answer:** Which candidate is weaker than you, and which is stronger? Does the described fold prove the bet was a successful bluff or a good value bet? What comparison is missing?

**Private note:** First explanation: ______. Confidence: sure / partly sure / guessing. Prompt or help received: ______. Unclear step: ______.

<details>
<summary>Compare your explanation</summary>

1. Kh-Kd makes one pair of kings, weaker than your pair of aces. 9c-9s makes three nines, stronger than your aces.

2. Folding the worse holding is not evidence of forcing a better hand to fold, and gives up that holding's possible call value.

3. Compare the outcomes of betting and checking across possible holdings and responses, with frequencies; one fold does not establish the better action.

**Likely misconception:** Confusing a fold of a worse hand with profitable bluffing.

### S6-C: Three responses to a bet of six

In this fictional heads-up river exercise, the pot is 24 counters and both players have 60 behind. The opponent checks to you last to act. Checking is legal and would end betting. You are considering a bet of 6. No money is wagered; no fees, side pots or tournament prizes are modeled. For this comparison only, assume Candidate 1 folds, Candidate 2 calls and Candidate 3 calls. These stipulated responses exclude raises.

Private cards: 9c, 9d. Shared cards: Jh, 7s, 4c, 3h, 2d.

Private card names: nine of clubs; nine of diamonds. Shared card names: jack of hearts; seven of spades; four of clubs; three of hearts; two of diamonds.

Candidate 1: Ts, Th.

Candidate 1 card names: ten of spades; ten of hearts.

Candidate 2: 8c, 8d.

Candidate 2 card names: eight of clubs; eight of diamonds.

Candidate 3: Qs, Qd.

Candidate 3 card names: queen of spades; queen of diamonds.

**Try without opening an answer:** Compare all three candidates with your final hand. Which response supplies a value target, which supplies a bluff target, and which call costs you the new bet? Does having both types of target establish that betting is better than checking?

**Private note:** First explanation: ______. Confidence: sure / partly sure / guessing. Prompt or help received: ______. Unclear step: ______.

<details>
<summary>Compare your explanation</summary>

1. Your best five are 9-9-J-7-4. Candidate 2 has 8-8-J-7-4 and is assumed to call, supplying a possible value target.

2. Candidate 1 has T-T-J-7-4 and beats you, so its assumed fold supplies a bluff target. Candidate 3 has Q-Q-J-7-4 and also beats you; its assumed call loses your new six counters.

3. These targets describe useful responses in different alternatives. Their existence alone does not compare betting with checking: the possible holdings and their frequencies at this decision are still needed.

**Likely misconception:** Assuming that naming both a value target and a bluff target proves a bet beats checking.

In this fictional heads-up river exercise, the pot is 40 counters and both players have 100 behind. The opponent checks to you last to act. Checking is legal and would end betting. You are considering a bet of 5. No money is wagered; no fees, side pots or tournament prizes are modeled. For this comparison only, assume each of the three candidates calls the proposed bet. These stipulated responses exclude raises.

Private cards: Qc, 8d. Shared cards: Qs, Qh, Jh, Jc, 2s.

Private card names: queen of clubs; eight of diamonds. Shared card names: queen of spades; queen of hearts; jack of hearts; jack of clubs; two of spades.

Candidate 1: Qd, 9s.

Candidate 1 card names: queen of diamonds; nine of spades.

Candidate 2: Js, Jd.

Candidate 2 card names: jack of spades; jack of diamonds.

Candidate 3: As, Kd.

Candidate 3 card names: ace of spades; king of diamonds.

**Try without opening an answer:** Compare the best five for all three candidates with yours. Which call supplies value, which loses the new bet, and what does the tied call change compared with checking? Do three listed callers by themselves determine the average benefit of betting?

**Private note:** First explanation: ______. Confidence: sure / partly sure / guessing. Prompt or help received: ______. Unclear step: ______.

<details>
<summary>Compare your explanation</summary>

1. Your Q-Q-Q-J-J ties Candidate 1's Q-Q-Q-J-J. Candidate 2 has J-J-J-J-Q and beats you. Candidate 3 has Q-Q-J-J-A and loses.

2. Candidate 3 supplies a weaker call and Candidate 2 a losing call. Candidate 1 ties, so its call is not value from a worse hand: with no fees, equal five-counter contributions are split back and your share of the existing pot is unchanged.

3. The number of listed callers does not give their frequencies. A complete comparison with checking needs the possible holdings and how often each response occurs, including any alternatives not represented here.

Candidate 3 card names: ace of hearts; four of clubs.

<details>
<summary>Compare your explanation</summary>

1. Your best five are the board's ten-high straight. Candidate 1 makes a queen-high straight and beats you. Candidates 2 and 3 both play the ten-high board straight and tie you.

2. Candidate 2's fold wins the existing pot instead of splitting it, but does not make a stronger hand fold. Candidate 3's tied call supplies no value from a worse hand; equal new contributions are split back under these no-fee assumptions.

3. Candidate 1's call loses the ten-counter bet. The comparison must weigh that cost against the benefit of tied hands folding and any other responses. These example rows do not establish the needed frequencies.

### S6-F: Three holdings and a proposed bet

In this fictional heads-up river exercise, the pot is 32 counters and both players have 80 behind. The opponent checks to you last to act. Checking is legal and would end betting. You are considering a bet of 8. No money is wagered; no fees, side pots or tournament prizes are modeled. Consider the three candidate holdings. Their responses to the proposed bet have not been assigned.

Private cards: As, 9h. Shared cards: Ac, Td, 7s, 4h, 2c.

Private card names: ace of spades; nine of hearts. Shared card names: ace of clubs; ten of diamonds; seven of spades; four of hearts; two of clubs.

Candidate 1: Ah, 8d.

Candidate 1 card names: ace of hearts; eight of diamonds.

Candidate 2: Tc, Th.

Candidate 2 card names: ten of clubs; ten of hearts.

Candidate 3: Qc, Jc.

Candidate 3 card names: queen of clubs; jack of clubs.

**Try without opening an answer:** Compare all three candidates with your best five. Which could support a value-bet explanation if they called, and what must you learn about their responses? Does beating two listed holdings establish that betting earns more than checking?

**Private note:** First explanation: ______. Confidence: sure / partly sure / guessing. Prompt or help received: ______. Unclear step: ______.

<details>
<summary>Compare your explanation</summary>

1. Your A-A-T-9-7 beats Candidate 1's A-A-T-8-7 and Candidate 3's A-Q-J-T-7. It loses to Candidate 2's T-T-T-A-7.

2. Candidates 1 and 3 could be value targets if they call the proposed amount. Being weaker does not supply that call assumption; their responses, including folds or raises where allowed, must be modeled or supported.

3. Beating two listed hands does not establish a better bet. Checking already wins against those hands, and the comparison needs their frequencies and their responses to this amount, as well as the cost of responses from hands that beat you.

**Likely misconception:** Inferring that a physically weaker holding will call even though no response assumption was supplied.

In this fictional heads-up river exercise, the pot is 36 counters and both players have 90 behind. The opponent checks to you last to act. Checking is legal and would end betting. You are considering a bet of 9. No money is wagered; no fees, side pots or tournament prizes are modeled. For this comparison only, assume Candidate 1 folds, Candidate 2 calls and Candidate 3 folds. These stipulated responses exclude raises.

Private cards: 8c, 7c. Shared cards: 8h, 7d, Ks, Kh, 2d.

Private card names: eight of clubs; seven of clubs. Shared card names: eight of hearts; seven of diamonds; king of spades; king of hearts; two of diamonds.

Candidate 1: Ac, Qd.

Candidate 1 card names: ace of clubs; queen of diamonds.

Candidate 2: Kd, 3s.

Candidate 2 card names: king of diamonds; three of spades.

Candidate 3: 8d, Ah.

Candidate 3 card names: eight of diamonds; ace of hearts.

**Try without opening an answer:** Choose your best five and compare each candidate, explaining the kicker where it matters. Which response supports a bluff explanation even though you can name two pairs? Which other outcomes must you include before comparing betting with checking?

**Private note:** First explanation: ______. Confidence: sure / partly sure / guessing. Prompt or help received: ______. Unclear step: ______.

<details>
<summary>Compare your explanation</summary>

1. Your best five are K-K-8-8-7. Candidate 1 has K-K-A-Q-8 and loses. Candidate 2 has K-K-K-8-7 and wins. Candidate 3 has K-K-8-8-A and wins on the ace kicker; your second private rank does not add a third pair to the best five.

2. Candidate 3's assumed fold is a fold by a better two-pair hand, so it can support a bluff explanation. Calling your own hand two pair does not determine the bet's purpose.

3. Candidate 1's fold adds no new opposing counters and checking would already beat it. Candidate 2's call loses nine new counters. Compare those possibilities and the stronger fold using a full response model and frequencies before claiming betting improves on checking.

**Likely misconception:** Using the two-pair label to skip kicker comparisons or to label every bet as value.
