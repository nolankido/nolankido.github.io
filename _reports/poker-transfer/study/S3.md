### S3-A: Private and shared cards on the flop

The flop has been dealt. Your opponent bets 4 and you have not yet put counters into this betting round. Both players have at least 30 counters behind when you face this bet, with no all-ins or side pots.

Private cards: Qc, Jc. Shared cards: Qd, 8c, 2c.

Private card names: queen of clubs; jack of clubs. Shared card names: queen of diamonds; eight of clubs; two of clubs.

**Try without opening an answer:** Describe the hand you already have and the possible club improvement separately. Is a club flush complete? Which of check, fold, call and legal raise can you choose against the bet?

**Private note:** First explanation: ______. Confidence: sure / partly sure / guessing. Prompt or help received: ______. Unclear step: ______.

<details>
<summary>Compare your explanation</summary>

1. You currently have a pair of queens with jack, eight and two kickers.

2. Two clubs in your hand and two on the board make four clubs, not a completed five-card flush.

3. Checking is unavailable against a live bet; calling, folding and a legal raise are possible action families.

**Likely misconception:** Calling four cards a flush or checking through a bet.

<details>
<summary>Compare your explanation</summary>

2. You can use four hearts now, two private and two shared. Another heart could make a flush, but not guarantee winning.

### S3-C: A flop after a check

In this fictional hand, the flop is dealt and the other player checks. No bet has been made on this flop.

Private cards: 4h, 2h. Shared cards: Jh, 9h, 7h.

Private card names: four of hearts; two of hearts. Shared card names: jack of hearts; nine of hearts; seven of hearts.

**Try without opening an answer:** Name your best five-card hand now. How many hearts can you use, and does your hand need another heart to qualify as a flush? Does this hand description establish that you are ahead of every possible opponent? Explain.

**Private note:** First explanation: ______. Confidence: sure / partly sure / guessing. Prompt or help received: ______. Unclear step: ______.

<details>
<summary>Compare your explanation</summary>

1. Your best five are jack, nine, seven, four and two of hearts, a jack-high flush.

2. All five available cards are hearts. The flush is already complete; another heart is not needed to make the category.

3. A completed flush is not proof that you are ahead. An opponent can hold higher hearts that make a stronger flush on this same board.

**Likely misconception:** Treating every suited hand as unfinished, or treating a completed flush as a guaranteed winner.

### S3-D: Jack and ten on a new flop

Only your private cards and these three shared flop cards are known in this fictional hand. All other card locations are unknown.

Private cards: Jd, Th. Shared cards: 9c, 8s, 2d.

Private card names: jack of diamonds; ten of hearts. Shared card names: nine of clubs; eight of spades; two of diamonds.

**Try without opening an answer:** Name your best five now. Which next-card ranks would complete a straight, and how many physical cards of those ranks are not among the known cards? Would drawing one of those cards prove that you will win the hand? Explain.

**Private note:** First explanation: ______. Confidence: sure / partly sure / guessing. Prompt or help received: ______. Unclear step: ______.

<details>
<summary>Compare your explanation</summary>

1. Your made hand is jack-high: jack, ten, nine, eight and two. Four consecutive ranks do not yet make a five-card straight.

2. A seven or a queen completes a straight on the next card. None is known, so the four sevens and four queens give eight physical completion cards.

3. Completing a straight is an improvement, not a guaranteed win. Opposing cards and any later shared card still affect the showdown.

**Likely misconception:** Calling four consecutive ranks a straight or treating eight completion cards as eight guaranteed winning cards.

### S3-F: Ace and two with three shared cards

The flop is dealt in a fictional hand. Use the five displayed cards to describe the hand already present.

Private cards: As, 2d. Shared cards: 3c, 4h, 5s.

Private card names: ace of spades; two of diamonds. Shared card names: three of clubs; four of hearts; five of spades.

**Try without opening an answer:** Name your strongest current five-card hand. Explain how the ace is used when naming that hand's strength. Compare your hand with a six-high straight: which hand is stronger, and why?

**Private note:** First explanation: ______. Confidence: sure / partly sure / guessing. Prompt or help received: ______. Unclear step: ______.

<details>
<summary>Compare your explanation</summary>

1. Ace, two, three, four and five already make a straight; no further card is needed to complete it.

2. The ace works below the two in this sequence, so this is a five-high straight, also called a wheel.

3. A six-high straight wins the comparison. The ace does not act as a high kicker or raise the height of this five-high straight.

**Likely misconception:** Treating an ace-low straight as ace-high or adding a kicker to compare straights.

### S3-G: Ace and eight after the flop is checked

In a fictional heads-up hand, the opponent checks this flop before you act. Both players have ample counters, and neither is all-in.

Private cards: Ah, 8h. Shared cards: Kh, 7c, 2d.

Private card names: ace of hearts; eight of hearts. Shared card names: king of hearts; seven of clubs; two of diamonds.

**Try without opening an answer:** Name your current five-card hand. Count the hearts and explain how many later hearts would be needed for a flush, including whether a single heart on the turn would finish it. If you check now, have you secured both later shared cards without any possible further payment? Explain.

**Private note:** First explanation: ______. Confidence: sure / partly sure / guessing. Prompt or help received: ______. Unclear step: ______.

<details>
<summary>Compare your explanation</summary>

1. Your current hand is ace-high: ace, king, eight, seven and two, with no pair or flush.

2. Three hearts are available. Both the turn and river would have to be hearts to make five; one turn heart would leave only four.

3. Checking behind ends this flop betting round, but a turn bet can create another payment decision. It does not secure both later cards without any further bet.

**Likely misconception:** Treating three suited cards as a one-card flush draw or a flop check as payment for all future cards.

### S3-H: Pocket sixes and a queen on the flop

This fictional hand has reached the flop. Your two private cards and the three shared cards are the only cards shown.

Private cards: 6d, 6c. Shared cards: Qh, 6s, 2h.

Private card names: six of diamonds; six of clubs. Shared card names: queen of hearts; six of spades; two of hearts.

**Try without opening an answer:** Name your best five-card hand. Count the hearts available to you: if both remaining shared cards were hearts, how many hearts could you then use, and would that make a flush? Does your current made hand prove that no possible opponent is ahead? Explain.

**Private note:** First explanation: ______. Confidence: sure / partly sure / guessing. Prompt or help received: ______. Unclear step: ______.

<details>
<summary>Compare your explanation</summary>

1. Your two private sixes and the shared six make three of a kind, with queen and two as the other cards.

2. You have two hearts available, both shared. Even two more hearts would give only four, so these private cards cannot make a heart flush on this board.

3. Three sixes are not a guaranteed lead. An opponent with two queens can have three queens, which outrank your three sixes.

**Likely misconception:** Overlooking the third matching rank or assuming two hearts on the board let every holding complete a heart flush.

### S3-I: Ace and jack on the final board

The river is dealt in this fictional open-card comparison. Consider each candidate holding separately against your hand.

Private cards: Ac, Jd. Shared cards: 8c, 8d, 8h, Ks, 2c.

Private card names: ace of clubs; jack of diamonds. Shared card names: eight of clubs; eight of diamonds; eight of hearts; king of spades; two of clubs.

Candidate 1: Ah, Qd.

Candidate 1 card names: ace of hearts; queen of diamonds.

Candidate 2: Qh, Td.

Candidate 2 card names: queen of hearts; ten of diamonds.

**Try without opening an answer:** Name your best five, including both side cards. Does your private jack belong in that best five, and why? Compare your final hand with each candidate: do the three shared eights force everyone to tie?

**Private note:** First explanation: ______. Confidence: sure / partly sure / guessing. Prompt or help received: ______. Unclear step: ______.

<details>
<summary>Compare your explanation</summary>

1. Your best five are three eights, ace and king: three of a kind with ace and king kickers.

2. The shared king is higher than your private jack, so the jack is omitted. You use only the private ace alongside four shared cards.

3. Candidate 1 also has eights with ace and king and ties you. Candidate 2 has eights with king and queen and loses; shared three of a kind does not force equal kickers.

### S3-J: Pocket kings with three shared cards

Use your private cards with this fictional flop. For the last question, compare the five-card categories as stated.

Private cards: Kh, Kd. Shared cards: Ks, 8c, 8d.

Private card names: king of hearts; king of diamonds. Shared card names: king of spades; eight of clubs; eight of diamonds.

**Try without opening an answer:** Name your best five-card hand. Are the two eights unused kickers, or do they form part of the hand category? Compare your hand with a full house containing three eights and two kings: which matching rank is compared first, and which hand is stronger?

**Private note:** First explanation: ______. Confidence: sure / partly sure / guessing. Prompt or help received: ______. Unclear step: ______.

<details>
<summary>Compare your explanation</summary>

1. Your five cards are king, king, king, eight and eight: kings full of eights, a full house.

2. The two eights are the pair needed alongside the three kings to make a full house; they are not unused kickers.

3. Full houses compare the rank of the three matching cards first. Three kings outrank three eights, so kings full of eights beats eights full of kings.

**Likely misconception:** Describing a full house as only three of a kind or comparing its pair before its three matching cards.

Candidate 2: 6c, 5d.

Candidate 2 card names: six of clubs; five of diamonds.

3. Candidate 1 uses a queen kicker with the same aces and kings and beats you. Candidate 2 uses a six kicker with those pairs and loses to your seven.

### S3-L: Ten and five on the final board

This fictional hand has reached the river. Examine each opposing candidate separately; the displayed holdings are examples for comparing final hands.

Private cards: Th, 5c. Shared cards: Ah, Qh, 7h, Qs, 2h.

Private card names: ten of hearts; five of clubs. Shared card names: ace of hearts; queen of hearts; seven of hearts; queen of spades; two of hearts.

Candidate 1: Qd, 7s.

Candidate 1 card names: queen of diamonds; seven of spades.

Candidate 2: 9h, 8c.

Candidate 2 card names: nine of hearts; eight of clubs.

**Try without opening an answer:** Name your best five-card hand. What hand does Candidate 1 make from its private cards and the shared board, and which hand wins? Compare Candidate 2 with your hand, then explain whether your final hand alone establishes that you are ahead.

**Private note:** First explanation: ______. Confidence: sure / partly sure / guessing. Prompt or help received: ______. Unclear step: ______.

<details>
<summary>Compare your explanation</summary>

1. Your best five are ace, queen, ten, seven and two of hearts, an ace-high flush. The private ten joins four shared hearts; the private five is unused.

2. Candidate 1 joins its queen to the two shared queens and its seven to the shared seven, making queens full of sevens. That full house beats your flush.

3. Candidate 2 makes a lower flush, ace, queen, nine, seven and two of hearts. Your flush wins at the first different rank, ten versus nine. The completed flush therefore does not establish the result against every possible holding.

**Likely misconception:** Treating the completion of a flush as the same event as winning against any opponent.
