### S5-A: Three candidate river holdings

On a fictional river, the other player checks. The three candidate opposing holdings below are just examples.

Private cards: Ah, Kd. Shared cards: Ks, 9c, 6d, 4h, 2c.

Private card names: ace of hearts; king of diamonds. Shared card names: king of spades; nine of clubs; six of diamonds; four of hearts; two of clubs.

Candidate 1: Ah, Qs.

Candidate 1 card names: ace of hearts; queen of spades.

Candidate 2: Qc, Qd.

Candidate 2 card names: queen of clubs; queen of diamonds.

Candidate 3: Kh, 2d.

Candidate 3 card names: king of hearts; two of diamonds.

**Try without opening an answer:** Identify the impossible holding. Of the possible holdings, which beats your final hand? Does a check rule that holding out, or tell you how often it appears?

**Private note:** First explanation: ______. Confidence: sure / partly sure / guessing. Prompt or help received: ______. Unclear step: ______.

<details>
<summary>Compare your explanation</summary>

1. Ah-Qs is impossible because you hold Ah; Qc-Qd is a weaker pair of queens; Kh-2d makes two pair, kings and twos, beating your one pair of kings.

2. A check alone does not reveal or exclude the stronger holding.

3. No candidate frequency was supplied, so this list cannot be treated as a probability distribution.

**Likely misconception:** Allowing known cards in an opposing holding or treating a check as proof of weakness.

### S5-B: A checked river and one later reveal

You reach the river. The other player checks. Consider the three example holdings below.

Private cards: Jc, 9d. Shared cards: Jd, 8c, 3h, 2s, 6c.

Private card names: jack of clubs; nine of diamonds. Shared card names: jack of diamonds; eight of clubs; three of hearts; two of spades; six of clubs.

Candidate 1: 9d, As.

Candidate 1 card names: nine of diamonds; ace of spades.

Candidate 2: Ks, Kc.

Candidate 2 card names: king of spades; king of clubs.

Candidate 3: Ad, 7c.

Candidate 3 card names: ace of diamonds; seven of clubs.

**Try without opening an answer:** Which holding cannot exist alongside your cards? Which possible hand is ahead of yours? What does the check tell you about which holding is present? Suppose the weaker possible holding appears at showdown once: does that prove all future checks are weak? Explain.

**Private note:** First explanation: ______. Confidence: sure / partly sure / guessing. Prompt or help received: ______. Unclear step: ______.

<details>
<summary>Compare your explanation</summary>

1. 9d-As is blocked by your own 9d; Ks-Kc makes a pair of kings stronger than your jacks; Ad-7c makes only ace-high and is weaker.

2. A check can be consistent with more than one holding, including a stronger one.

3. One revealed hand is one observation, not an established distribution for later opponents or decisions.

**Likely misconception:** Generalizing a single showdown or ignoring physical card removal.

### S5-C: Four river alternatives with aces

In this fictional heads-up river exercise, the opponent checks to you last to act. Checking back is legal and would end betting. No money is wagered. Consider the four candidate holdings separately.

Private cards: Ac, Jh. Shared cards: Ad, Tc, 6s, 3h, 2d.

Private card names: ace of clubs; jack of hearts. Shared card names: ace of diamonds; ten of clubs; six of spades; three of hearts; two of diamonds.

Candidate 1: Ac, Ks.

Candidate 1 card names: ace of clubs; king of spades.

Candidate 2: As, Kh.

Candidate 2 card names: ace of spades; king of hearts.

Candidate 3: Qc, Qd.

Candidate 3 card names: queen of clubs; queen of diamonds.

Candidate 4: Ah, Jd.

Candidate 4 card names: ace of hearts; jack of diamonds.

**Try without opening an answer:** Which candidate is physically impossible, and which exact known card excludes it? Compare each legal candidate with your best five, including any tie. Does holding one ace exclude every opposing ace or allow you to infer an exact holding from the check?

**Private note:** First explanation: ______. Confidence: sure / partly sure / guessing. Prompt or help received: ______. Unclear step: ______.

<details>
<summary>Compare your explanation</summary>

1. Candidate 1 is impossible because it repeats your ace of clubs. The other aces are different cards, so Candidates 2 and 4 are physically possible.

2. Your best five are A-A-J-T-6. Candidate 2 has A-A-K-T-6 and wins on the king kicker. Candidate 3 has Q-Q-A-T-6 and loses. Candidate 4 also has A-A-J-T-6 and ties.

3. An exact ace removes holdings containing that card, not all ace holdings. The check does not identify which legal holding is present or prove the opponent has no ace.

**Likely misconception:** Treating one known suit of a rank as if every card of that rank were unavailable.

### S5-D: A river with two shared eights

In this fictional heads-up river exercise, the opponent checks to you last to act. Checking back is legal and would end betting. No money is wagered. The four rows are alternative holdings for the same opponent.

Private cards: 8c, 7h. Shared cards: 8d, 8s, Qh, 4c, 2d.

Private card names: eight of clubs; seven of hearts. Shared card names: eight of diamonds; eight of spades; queen of hearts; four of clubs; two of diamonds.

Candidate 1: 8d, Ac.

Candidate 1 card names: eight of diamonds; ace of clubs.

Candidate 2: 8h, As.

Candidate 2 card names: eight of hearts; ace of spades.

Candidate 3: Qc, Jd.

Candidate 3 card names: queen of clubs; jack of diamonds.

Candidate 4: Kc, Ks.

Candidate 4 card names: king of clubs; king of spades.

**Try without opening an answer:** Which candidate reuses a board card? Compare the best five for every legal candidate with yours. Does the number of legal rows establish how often each one checks?

**Private note:** First explanation: ______. Confidence: sure / partly sure / guessing. Prompt or help received: ______. Unclear step: ______.

<details>
<summary>Compare your explanation</summary>

1. Candidate 1 is impossible because the eight of diamonds is already on the board. A shared card excludes an opposing holding just as a private card does.

2. Your best five are 8-8-8-Q-7. Candidate 2 has 8-8-8-A-Q and wins with the ace kicker. Candidate 3 has Q-Q-8-8-J, and Candidate 4 has K-K-8-8-Q; both two-pair hands lose to your three eights.

3. Three legal rows establish three listed possibilities, not equal probabilities or observed checking rates. Which holdings reach this decision and choose to check remains a separate question.

**Likely misconception:** Ignoring shared-card blockers or turning a list of legal holdings into equal checking frequencies.

### S5-F: Four alternatives after a river check

In this fictional heads-up river exercise, the opponent checks to you last to act. Checking back is legal and would end betting. No money is wagered. Treat each candidate row as a separate possibility.

Private cards: 4c, 3d. Shared cards: As, Ks, Qs, Js, Ts.

Private card names: four of clubs; three of diamonds. Shared card names: ace of spades; king of spades; queen of spades; jack of spades; ten of spades.

Candidate 1 card names: ace of spades; two of clubs.

Candidate 2: Ah, Ad.

Candidate 2 card names: ace of hearts; ace of diamonds.

Candidate 3: 9s, 8s.

Candidate 3 card names: nine of spades; eight of spades.

Candidate 4: Qc, Qd.

Candidate 4 card names: queen of clubs; queen of diamonds.

**Try without opening an answer:** Which candidate conflicts with a known card? For every legal candidate, identify the best five and the showdown comparison. Suppose Candidate 4 is revealed after one check-through: what does that observation establish about which hands will check on a later, different board?

**Private note:** First explanation: ______. Confidence: sure / partly sure / guessing. Prompt or help received: ______. Unclear step: ______.

<details>
<summary>Compare your explanation</summary>

1. Candidate 1 is impossible because the ace of spades is already a shared card. The other three candidates do not repeat any known card.

2. Your best hand and every legal candidate's best hand are the board's ace-high straight flush. All three legal candidates tie you; neither a pocket pair nor lower private spades improves this board.

3. The reveal establishes the opposing cards in that one observed hand. It does not establish a checking distribution on a different board or a general rule that checked hands are weak.

### S5-G: Several rows with ace and king

In this fictional heads-up river exercise, the opponent checks to you last to act. Checking back is legal and would end betting. No money is wagered. The four rows are alternatives, not cards held together.

Private cards: Ah, Kd. Shared cards: Ac, 9s, 6h, 4d, 2c.

Private card names: ace of hearts; king of diamonds. Shared card names: ace of clubs; nine of spades; six of hearts; four of diamonds; two of clubs.

Candidate 1: As, Kh.

Candidate 1 card names: ace of spades; king of hearts.

Candidate 2: Ad, Kc.

Candidate 2 card names: ace of diamonds; king of clubs.

Candidate 3: As, Kc.

Candidate 3 card names: ace of spades; king of clubs.

Candidate 4: Qs, Qh.

Candidate 4 card names: queen of spades; queen of hearts.

**Try without opening an answer:** Are all four candidates physically possible, and how does each compare with your best five? Are Candidates 1, 2 and 3 one exact physical holding or three exact combinations under the ace-king label? Does listing four rows give each a one-quarter chance after the check?

**Private note:** First explanation: ______. Confidence: sure / partly sure / guessing. Prompt or help received: ______. Unclear step: ______.

<details>
<summary>Compare your explanation</summary>

1. All four candidates are legal. Your A-A-K-9-6 ties Candidates 1, 2 and 3, each of which also makes A-A-K-9-6. Candidate 4 has Q-Q-A-9-6 and loses.

2. Candidates 1, 2 and 3 are three different exact combinations of ace-king, despite sharing a broad hand label and the same final rank. Their repeated cards across alternative rows are allowed because the rows are not simultaneous holdings.

3. One-quarter per row would be an additional model assumption. Counting distinct combinations alone does not establish how often each reaches this river and checks.

**Likely misconception:** Confusing a broad hand label with one exact combination or assuming equally likely rows after an action.

In this fictional heads-up river exercise, the opponent checks to you last to act. Checking back is legal and would end betting. No money is wagered. Candidate 2: Kc, Tc.

Candidate 2 card names: king of clubs; ten of clubs.

Candidate 3: Kd, Kh.

Candidate 3 card names: king of diamonds; king of hearts.

Candidate 4: Qh, 9c.

Candidate 4 card names: queen of hearts; nine of clubs.

**Try without opening an answer:** Which candidates are physically possible using only cards actually shown? Can your guess about the folded player exclude Candidate 1? Compare each candidate with your best five, then explain whether the current check identifies one of them.

<details>
<summary>Compare your explanation</summary>

### S5-K: One later reveal from a river bet

In this fictional heads-up river exercise, the opponent bets 5 counters into a pot of 20. Both players had 50 counters behind before the bet, with no all-ins or side pots. No money is wagered. Private cards: Tc, Td. Shared cards: Jh, 8s, 4d, 3c, 2h.

Private card names: ten of clubs; ten of diamonds. Shared card names: jack of hearts; eight of spades; four of diamonds; three of clubs; two of hearts.

Candidate 1: Js, 9h.

Candidate 1 card names: jack of spades; nine of hearts.

Candidate 2: 8d, 8c.

Candidate 2 card names: eight of diamonds; eight of clubs.

Candidate 3: Ac, Kh.

Candidate 3 card names: ace of clubs; king of hearts.

<details>
<summary>Compare your explanation</summary>

3. One favorable showdown does not establish that calling was justified from the information available earlier. That judgment needs the call price and a defensible model of the possible betting hands and their frequencies.
