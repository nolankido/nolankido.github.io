### S2-A: Action after a button call

Six seats clockwise are first, next, cutoff, button, small blind and big blind. You are on the button. First folds, next raises to 7, cutoff calls 7 and you call 7. The small blind folds. The big blind posted 2 before the cards were dealt. Assume no all-ins.

Private cards: 9c, 8c. Shared cards: not dealt.

Private card names: nine of clubs; eight of clubs.

**Try without opening an answer:** Who still has an initial decision? Could a raise by that player make you act again? If everyone remaining reaches the flop without a further raise, which seat acts first?

**Private note:** First explanation: ______. Confidence: sure / partly sure / guessing. Prompt or help received: ______. Unclear step: ______.

<details>
<summary>Compare your explanation</summary>

1. The big blind still has an initial decision; posting a blind was forced, not a choice to call the raise.

2. If the big blind raises, the earlier players still in the hand may face a new decision.

3. If next, cutoff, button and big blind remain, the big blind acts first after the flop, clockwise left of the button.

**Likely misconception:** Assuming the button completes preflop action by calling.

### S2-B: A two-player flop after a button raise

Six players are dealt a hand. The first three seats fold. You open-raise on the button to 6. The small blind calls the extra chips; the big blind folds. The two of you reach the flop with at least 30 counters each still behind; neither player is all-in. No action on this flop has been described.

Private cards: Ac, 5s. Shared cards: not shown (the flop is dealt).

Private card names: ace of clubs; five of spades.

**Try without opening an answer:** Was the small blind's forced post already their initial decision? Who acts first and who acts second on the flop? If the first player checks, what may the second player do that would be unavailable while facing a bet? Explain the difference between preflop and postflop order.

**Private note:** First explanation: ______. Confidence: sure / partly sure / guessing. Prompt or help received: ______. Unclear step: ______.

<details>
<summary>Compare your explanation</summary>

1. Posting the small blind does not itself use the player's preflop decision; their later call does.

2. With only small blind and button active on the flop, the small blind acts first and the button acts second.

3. Facing a check, the button may check or make a legal bet; there is no unmatched wager to call.

**Likely misconception:** Assuming the button is first on the flop or that a check requires a call.

### S2-C: Two folds before the cutoff

This fictional hand began with six players. Seats clockwise, starting at the dealer button, are button, small blind, big blind, under the gun, hijack and cutoff. Blinds are 1/2, with no ante or fees. Every player began with 100 counters; there are no all-ins. You are the cutoff. Under the gun folds and the hijack folds. No other voluntary action has occurred.

Private cards: Th, 8d. Shared cards: not dealt.

Private card names: ten of hearts; eight of diamonds.

**Try without opening an answer:** Whose turn is next? Name, in order, the other players who still have an initial decision after yours. If you enter the pot, do the two folds establish the private cards or future choices of the players still behind?

**Private note:** First explanation: ______. Confidence: sure / partly sure / guessing. Prompt or help received: ______. Unclear step: ______.

<details>
<summary>Compare your explanation</summary>

1. The cutoff acts next, after the folds from under the gun and the hijack.

2. The button, small blind and big blind still have their initial decisions, in that order.

3. Those folds do not establish the private cards or future responses of the three players still behind you.

**Likely misconception:** Treating earlier folds as though the whole table had already yielded the pot or revealed the remaining hands.

### S2-D: A raiser and caller before the small blind

This fictional hand began with six players. Seats clockwise, starting at the dealer button, are button, small blind, big blind, under the gun, hijack and cutoff. Blinds are 1/2, with no ante or fees. Every player began with 100 counters; there are no all-ins. You are the small blind. Under the gun and the hijack fold, the cutoff raises to 8, and the button calls 8. The big blind has only posted its blind.

Private cards: Ad, Tc. Shared cards: not dealt.

Private card names: ace of diamonds; ten of clubs.

**Try without opening an answer:** Who acts next, and who still has an initial decision after you? If you call, is the current preflop round necessarily finished? If all four remaining players see the flop, give their full initial action order on that new street.

**Private note:** First explanation: ______. Confidence: sure / partly sure / guessing. Prompt or help received: ______. Unclear step: ______.

<details>
<summary>Compare your explanation</summary>

1. The small blind acts next, with the big blind still to take its initial decision.

2. Your call does not necessarily finish preflop action because the big blind can make a legal raise and return action to earlier players.

3. If all four see the flop, the small blind acts first after the fixed dealer button, followed by the big blind, cutoff and button.

**Likely misconception:** Assuming a small-blind call closes action because several other players already contributed.

### S2-E: A raise reaches the big blind

This fictional hand began with six players. Seats clockwise, starting at the dealer button, are button, small blind, big blind, under the gun, hijack and cutoff. Blinds are 1/2, with no ante or fees. Every player began with 100 counters; there are no all-ins. You are the big blind. Under the gun and the hijack fold, the cutoff raises to 6, and both the button and small blind fold.

Private cards: Kh, Jd. Shared cards: not dealt.

Private card names: king of hearts; jack of diamonds.

**Try without opening an answer:** Who has the next decision, and is any other initial response still pending? If you only call, does that complete preflop action? If you both see the flop, state the order there and explain whether acting last preflop makes you last postflop.

**Private note:** First explanation: ______. Confidence: sure / partly sure / guessing. Prompt or help received: ______. Unclear step: ______.

<details>
<summary>Compare your explanation</summary>

1. The big blind has the next decision; the cutoff already raised and every other seat has folded.

2. A call matches the only opponent's wager and completes this preflop round, assuming no new raise.

3. On the flop the big blind acts before the cutoff; the last initial preflop decision does not make you last on the next street.

**Likely misconception:** Carrying the big blind's last preflop turn into the postflop order.

### S2-F: Three seats reach the flop

This fictional hand began with six players. Seats clockwise, starting at the dealer button, are button, small blind, big blind, under the gun, hijack and cutoff. Blinds are 1/2, with no ante or fees. Every remaining player has at least 50 counters behind; there are no all-ins or side pots. You are the hijack. Both blinds and under the gun folded before the flop. Only you, the cutoff and the button remain. The flop is dealt and nobody has acted on it.

Private cards: 8h, 7s. Shared cards: not shown (the flop is dealt).

Private card names: eight of hearts; seven of spades.

**Try without opening an answer:** Who acts first, next and last on this flop? Which seats must be skipped? Would whichever player acted last preflop automatically get the first flop decision? Explain the rule you use.

**Private note:** First explanation: ______. Confidence: sure / partly sure / guessing. Prompt or help received: ______. Unclear step: ______.

<details>
<summary>Compare your explanation</summary>

1. The hijack acts first, skipping the folded small blind, big blind and under-the-gun seats clockwise after the button.

2. The cutoff follows the hijack, and the button has the last initial flop decision.

3. The new street starts with the first remaining player clockwise after the dealer button, not automatically with the last preflop actor.

**Likely misconception:** Starting a new street at a folded blind or at the last preflop actor.

### S2-G: Two remaining seats with the button folded

This fictional hand began with six players. Seats clockwise, starting at the dealer button, are button, small blind, big blind, under the gun, hijack and cutoff. Blinds are 1/2, with no ante or fees. Every remaining player has at least 50 counters behind; there are no all-ins or side pots. You are the hijack. The button, both blinds and under the gun folded before the flop. Only you and the cutoff remain. The flop is dealt and no flop action has occurred.

Private cards: Kc, 8h. Shared cards: not shown (the flop is dealt).

Private card names: king of clubs; eight of hearts.

**Try without opening an answer:** Who acts first and second? Which player has the last initial flop decision, and does that make that player the dealer button? What information does acting later provide, and what does it leave unknown?

**Private note:** First explanation: ______. Confidence: sure / partly sure / guessing. Prompt or help received: ______. Unclear step: ______.

<details>
<summary>Compare your explanation</summary>

1. The hijack acts first and the cutoff acts second, following the original clockwise order while skipping folded seats.

2. The cutoff has the last initial flop decision among the survivors, but does not become the dealer button during this hand.

3. Acting later lets the cutoff observe your action before deciding; it does not reveal your private cards or prove which holding you have.

**Likely misconception:** Moving the dealer button after folds or treating position as knowledge of an opponent's private cards.

### S2-H: A small-blind check on the flop

This fictional hand began with six players. Seats clockwise, starting at the dealer button, are button, small blind, big blind, under the gun, hijack and cutoff. Blinds are 1/2, with no ante or fees. Every remaining player has at least 50 counters behind; there are no all-ins or side pots. You are the big blind. Under the gun, the hijack and the cutoff folded before the flop. Only the small blind, you and the button remain. On the flop, the small blind checks. No other flop action has occurred.

Private cards: Qd, Td. Shared cards: not shown (the flop is dealt).

Private card names: queen of diamonds; ten of diamonds.

**Try without opening an answer:** Whose turn is next? Which player still follows your first decision? If you also check, is the betting round over, or can another action still create a decision for you? Explain.

**Private note:** First explanation: ______. Confidence: sure / partly sure / guessing. Prompt or help received: ______. Unclear step: ______.

<details>
<summary>Compare your explanation</summary>

1. The big blind acts next, immediately after the small blind's check in the remaining clockwise order.

2. The button still has its initial flop decision after yours.

3. Your check would not end the round because the button still has a turn and may bet, returning a decision to the earlier checkers.

**Likely misconception:** Skipping an unacted seat or ending a multiway round before the last player has had an opportunity.

### S2-I: A flop bet before the cutoff

This fictional hand began with six players. Seats clockwise, starting at the dealer button, are button, small blind, big blind, under the gun, hijack and cutoff. Blinds are 1/2, with no ante or fees. Every remaining player has at least 50 counters behind; there are no all-ins or side pots. You are the cutoff and made the last raise preflop. The big blind and button called; the other seats folded. On the flop the big blind bets 5. You and the button have not acted on the flop.

Private cards: Jd, 7c. Shared cards: not shown (the flop is dealt).

Private card names: jack of diamonds; seven of clubs.

**Try without opening an answer:** Who responds next and who follows that player? Does your preflop raise change the clockwise flop order or require opponents to check to you? If you call, could the remaining player still return the action to you?

**Private note:** First explanation: ______. Confidence: sure / partly sure / guessing. Prompt or help received: ______. Unclear step: ______.

<details>
<summary>Compare your explanation</summary>

1. The cutoff responds next, and the button follows; the big blind has already made the opening flop action.

2. Being the preflop raiser does not change seat order or require an earlier postflop player to check.

3. If you call, the button can still make a legal raise, which can return action to you after the big blind responds.

**Likely misconception:** Assuming the prior raiser owns the first postflop action or that calling before the button closes the round.

### S2-K: The action reaches the two blinds

Every player began with 100 counters; there are no all-ins. You are the small blind. Under the gun, the hijack, the cutoff and the button all fold. Only you and the big blind remain.

Private cards: 5s, 4d. Shared cards: not dealt.

Private card names: five of spades; four of diamonds.

**Try without opening an answer:** Who acts now and who still follows? If both blinds continue to the flop, who acts first on it? Should the dealer button move during this hand because only two players remain? Explain.

**Private note:** First explanation: ______. Confidence: sure / partly sure / guessing. Prompt or help received: ______. Unclear step: ______.

<details>
<summary>Compare your explanation</summary>

1. The small blind acts now, followed by the big blind in this initial preflop order.

2. If both continue, the small blind also acts first on the flop, with the big blind second.

3. The button remains at its original seat; a six-player hand reduced to two survivors does not acquire the special starting order of a table dealt heads-up.

**Likely misconception:** Applying the heads-up button exception midway through a six-player hand.

### S2-L: A raise and call before the cutoff

This fictional hand began with six players. Seats clockwise, starting at the dealer button, are button, small blind, big blind, under the gun, hijack and cutoff. Blinds are 1/2, with no ante or fees. Every player began with 100 counters; there are no all-ins. You are the cutoff. Under the gun raises to 8 and the hijack calls 8. No other voluntary action has occurred.

Private cards: Qc, 7s. Shared cards: not dealt.

Private card names: queen of clubs; seven of spades.

**Try without opening an answer:** Whose turn is next? Which players still have an initial decision after you, and can one of them change the wager? Is this an unopened pot for selecting a preflop reference? Explain which earlier action must be retained in the record.

**Private note:** First explanation: ______. Confidence: sure / partly sure / guessing. Prompt or help received: ______. Unclear step: ______.

<details>
<summary>Compare your explanation</summary>

1. The cutoff acts next, after the under-the-gun raise and hijack call.

2. The button, small blind and big blind still follow, and a legal raise by one of them can create another decision for earlier players.

3. This pot already has a voluntary raise and call; an unopened-cutoff reference omits that action and does not describe this decision.

**Likely misconception:** Using an unopened-pot reference simply because the current player occupies the cutoff.
