## Purpose and target audience

Get useful existing work in front of people who face everyday tasks and want to use AI without confusing fluency with accuracy. The primary invitation is to try **one free tool**, not to follow a brand, buy a product, or read the whole catalog.

- **First destination:** https://nolankido.com/technology/task-brief/
- **Companion evidence:** https://nolankido.com/technology/checking-ai-website-changes/
- **Editorial point of view:** https://nolankido.com/notes/keep-the-judgment/
- **Human next action:** produce one brief, compare its checks with the user's actual task, and keep the resulting text in their own files.

The task-brief builder is a deterministic five-field local template and makes no AI call. Six examples are illustrative, not measured uses. The website case is historical and records an extra HTML closing tag missed by other checks; it is not a productivity study or a guarantee about other AI systems.

## Release gate before any outside distribution

1. Open the two linked pages on a mobile device; verify the builder, examples, copy/download behavior, and working canonical URLs.
2. Verify the three primary claims by reading the live pages: no AI runs in the builder; the page assembles user inputs rather than generating an AI response; the recorded website case is a **specific 2026 release defect**, not a generalized efficacy result.
3. Approve voice, tone, personal ownership language, and any footage showing other people or personal information.
4. Record whether and where each message was posted, including the actual URL and platform timestamp. Do **not** mark a drafted or scheduled message as published.
5. Check any platform and community policies for self-promotion, links, and AI-generated material. Do not disguise an author promotion as an independent recommendation.

## X: initial evidence-led six-post thread

**Post 1 (hook):**

A good prompt is not the same as a good plan.

Before I hand a task to AI, I want five things clear: purpose, allowed inputs, the finished result, how I’ll check it, and what must not happen.

**Post 2:**

I put a free task-brief builder on my site. It assembles your own answers into a structured brief.

There’s no chatbot behind the button, no account, and no AI request. The point is to make the human decisions visible before generating anything.

**Post 3:**

Example: “Summarize this meeting” can become “Separate confirmed decisions, proposed actions, and open questions. Mark owners or dates TBD unless they appear in the supplied notes.”

That’s a better task specification, not a promise about the answer.

**Post 4:**

The key question is often “How will I check it?”

For a meeting summary: compare it with original notes and confirm ownership with the people involved. For an article: trace factual claims. For a website change: test behavior, not just the build.

**Post 5:**

There’s a concrete reason I care about different checks.

One AI-assisted change to my website passed a build and 347 unit/content tests. A later HTML5 parser still caught an extra closing tag. The defect was fixed before publication.

That’s one documented failure, not a claim that a test count proves safety.

**Post 6 (links):**

### X follow-up 1: explain why the check matters

The most valuable line in an AI task brief may be the one people skip: “How will I check it?”

“Make it accurate” is not a check.

“Compare each decision with the original notes, and mark unsupported owners or dates as TBD” is something you can actually do.

Free editable brief: https://nolankido.com/technology/task-brief/

### X follow-up 2: distinguish a passing test from evidence

I’ve seen the same distinction in my own website work:

A successful build shows that the files can be generated. An HTML parser examines structure. A browser test exercises behavior. A published-file check confirms what people receive.

They answer different questions.

I wrote up one actual defect and repair, including the limits of the evidence:
https://nolankido.com/technology/checking-ai-website-changes/

## Measurement ledger (private, aggregate only)

Use a private sheet or note with the following columns, one row per **actual** post:

`date_hst | platform | post_url | asset | actual_status | relevant_replies | shares | site_referral_visits_if_available | specific_obstacle_reported | correction_made`

- Status vocabulary: `draft`, `approved`, `scheduled`, `published`, `removed`. Use `published` only after checking the live post URL.
- Record *unknown* when access to analytics is missing; never convert missing data to zero.
- Umami intentionally excludes query strings/fragments and respects Do Not Track. Do not add new UTM or visitor-input tracking just for this effort.
- The Search Console baseline cannot measure referral visits or post engagement.
- More visitors are not the only goal. A report that a tool's required field is confusing is more actionable than an unsupported impression count.
