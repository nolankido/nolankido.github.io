## Assessment

The draft has a useful central argument: judge AI by the complete useful task,
including preparing, checking and correcting, rather than output speed. It also
has practical breadth, offers a simpler alternative and asks readers to retain
human judgment. It is not suitable for publication unchanged. The prose is
repetitive, its application blocks run together, and several claims go beyond
the cited evidence or use promotional summaries where an original source is
available. Its time-comparison table assigns zero checking time to the baseline,
which makes the comparison unfair when the old method also requires review.

The original was read from the supplied local attachment, with Pandoc preserving
its text for review. The converted draft has 4,403 whitespace-separated words,
including its reference list. Thirty-eight distinct cited URLs were extracted
from the RTF; repeated RTF fields account for the larger raw URL count. The
original attachment is unchanged. A revised article, rather than the raw
attachment or an uncorrected generated essay, is the public deliverable.

| Issue in the draft | Revision |

| Consultant task result described as 19% worse | Correctness comparison stated as about 19 percentage points, with task, sample, model and study dates |

| Resume advice promises screening-filter avoidance | Faithful wording, no invented experience and no hiring or screening guarantee |
| Examples present two-minute understanding and application multipliers as success measures | Observable finish conditions without fabricated outcome measurements |
| Baseline checking is zero; sequential weeks imply a clean intervention | Count review in both methods and disclose differences in task, order, familiarity and interruptions |

The revised title is **AI and personal productivity: what actually helps**,
published at `/living-well/ai-and-personal-productivity/`. The rendered article
has about 3,900 words including source notes, five main sections and eight
semantic subsections. Its role is a researched essay with practical examples,
not a firsthand field note, tool ranking, medical/legal determination or
validated household experiment. Gemini draft provenance and further AI
assistance are visible. Prompts and the time comparison are explicitly proposed
or fictional.

## Evidence and limits

Primary references were checked on October 14, 2026:

- Noy and Zhang, the published 2023 study abstract:
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37440646/. The sample and writing-task results
  are retained within that setting. The full experiments were not replicated.
- Dell’Acqua and colleagues, the 2026 paper on the 2023 consultant experiment:
  https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/dell-acqua-et-al-2026-navigating-the-jagged-technological-frontier_5c589c8c-fbb5-458f-b285-c944746cd717.pdf.
  Methods and the correctness comparison were checked. The result does not
  measure everyday household benefit.
- Lee and colleagues, Microsoft’s 2025 research abstract:
  https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/the-impact-of-generative-ai-on-critical-thinking-self-reported-reductions-in-cognitive-effort-and-confidence-effects-from-a-survey-of-knowledge-workers/.
  The article distinguishes survey evidence from measured deterioration.
- Liu and colleagues, preprint version 5:
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.04721v5. Abstract and version status were checked;
  the revised essay does not claim persistent real-life damage.
- CMS’s EOB guide, USDA leftovers guidance and its safe-temperature chart
  support the narrow paperwork and cooking boundaries. No reader’s bill,
  coverage or food was evaluated.
- OpenAI’s data-controls guidance, Anthropic’s consumer-training policy and
  Google’s Gemini Privacy Hub support the revised privacy discussion. There is
  no universal retention claim, price table or timeless settings tutorial.

The revised application prompts are original editorial suggestions. Research
findings are not represented as validation of those prompts. Broad adoption
figures, GDP forecasts, historical household-hours claims, jargon and claims
that tools always fail at expertise or can never take external actions were
removed because they did not improve the reader’s task or required a different
scope of evidence. Source notes state the reading and verification limits.
