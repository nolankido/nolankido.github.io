# Poker Foundations: connecting the course finish

Reviewed October 16, 2026, against published commit
`b55e003f43f48289ab791683ac7d1583eb79451e`.
This is a content and interface audit, not an observed beginner study.

## The remaining learning gap

The six short lessons now teach, model and ask for an explanation. The course
also has a guided hand, separate card and betting tools, mixed questions and a
checkpoint. Its guided hand supplies choices and feedback, while the checkpoint
mainly isolates one concept at a time. Neither asks the learner to independently
carry the same cards, position and contributions through a complete new hand.
Success on an isolated question is insufficient evidence of that transfer.

| Course outcome | Final-hand task | Changed later attempt |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Construct and compare five cards | Rebuild the hand as each board card arrives | A missed heart draw becomes a completed heart flush |
| Track action and additional chips | Account for blinds, pending players and the current round | Button becomes small blind; a posted chip reduces the preflop call |
| Use position, board and possible holdings | State what a check establishes and eliminate card conflicts | The same checked-turn observation still cannot exclude a stronger flush |
| Explain price, purpose and uncertainty | Give the response a value bet needs; calculate a final-call price | One-third-pot and half-pot bets produce different prices |

## Implementation

The existing checkpoint becomes the course finish. Hand A comes before the
original twelve individual situations. Each street asks for a spoken or written
explanation without answer choices. Feedback includes the reasoning, likely
misconceptions and specific repair destinations. Later streets, answers and the
result start closed. Hand B is inside a separate disclosure for a later visit.
Mobile feedback keeps the parent disclosures' horizontal padding flat so that
nested panels do not squeeze the explanation into a narrow column. Browser
checks measure usable text width as well as horizontal overflow.
These are two fixed hands; repeated exposure eventually becomes recall, so the
course explicitly asks for unfamiliar examples when checking further transfer.

Both examples conserve six original stacks of 100 counters. The first result
loses and the second wins. Neither is used to grade the decision. Candidate
holdings are alternatives, not a complete or frequency-weighted range. The
scripted actions are teaching facts and make no optimal-strategy claim.

The existing six-stage study card includes the same eight open prompts without
solutions or revealed opponents. No new page, script, saved progress, account,
email service or payment flow is introduced. The twelve existing situations,
answers, repair table and dedicated checkpoint workbook remain available.

## Basis and checks

The existing [IES practice guide](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/PracticeGuide/1),
revisited October 16, recommends worked examples, explanation, retrieval and
spacing. The inference here is that a connected attempt can expose gaps that
isolated recognition questions miss. This does not establish a learning effect
for these poker materials, a completion rate or an optimal return interval.

Mechanics were checked against the primary
[Hold'em rules](https://www.pokerstars.com/poker/games/texas-holdem/) and
[hand rankings](https://www.pokerstars.com/poker/games/rules/hand-rankings/)

disabled scripts and search exclusion of future streets and answers.

## Evidence needed before a paid pilot

Observe actual beginners attempt one hand without coaching. Record privately
where they lose the action, reuse a prior-round contribution, overstate a draw,
or confuse price with equity. Let them select one repair, then observe the changed
hand on a later visit. Compare explanations and needed hints, not opened panels
or the result of the fictional hand. Ask separately whether the format helps and
what paid instruction or feedback they would want. No learner observations or
willingness-to-pay findings are claimed here.
