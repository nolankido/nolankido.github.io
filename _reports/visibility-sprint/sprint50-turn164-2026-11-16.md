## Reader problem

Turns 162 and 163 delivered completed seven-step examples for two different
decision situations: A-K facing successive preflop decisions, and 6-4 offsuit
shoving near a money bubble. The third archived NolanPoker hand, a four-handed
pocket-sevens reshove, remained an exercise and narrated outcome rather than a
completed review a reader could copy into the blank hand-review worksheet.

This is particularly important at a final table. A button's opening range and
its calling range after a big-blind all-in are distinct, while payout steps and
the other players' stacks may change the tournament-value comparison. The
published report does not contain the information needed to calculate a
numerical chip-EV, fold-equity or ICM ranking.

## Added: completed seven-step pocket-sevens review

New `downloads/poker-hand-review-example-77.md` uses only the
source-documented Portland Meadows Episode 1 narrative. All seven sections
of the existing private worksheet are completed, with explicit limits.

1. **Situation and source confidence:** four players at the final table,
   15,000 small blind, 30,000 live big blind, separate 30,000 big-blind ante,
   about 585,000 reported chips and pocket sevens in the big blind. The stack
   conversion `585,000 / 30,000 = 19.5 BB` is conditional on the quoted
   amount being measured before posting. If the 585,000 included both forced
   contributions, `585,000 - 30,000 - 30,000 = 525,000` could remain
   behind, a conditional calculation rather than a verified live stack.
2. **Action before showdown:** cutoff folds, button opens to 75,000 total
   (2.5 BB), small blind folds and hero reshoves. Matching the 75,000 from
   a 30,000 posted live blind requires 45,000 more if the reported sizes
   are exact. The separate 30,000 big-blind ante is dead money, not
   additional live credit.
3. **Alternatives:** fold, flat, smaller reraise and all-in reshove are
   compared as possibilities, not ranked. A button that opens many hands
   might call a reshove with only some of them; the full opening and
   call-versus-reshove ranges are unknown and cannot be deduced from one
   showdown.
4. **Thought process:** the narrated description of the button as
   aggressive and the concern about playing a medium pair out of position
   are clearly labeled retrospective. No contemporaneous note exists in
   the available source account.
5. **Sensitivity and stopping:** asks about wide versus tight continuation
   ranges, exact stacks including the short small blind, payout steps,
   coverage, possible bounties, and whether a reference tool permits
   flats or smaller reraises. Without validated inputs, no numerical
   chip-EV or ICM verdict is asserted.
6. **Reported result, held until after the decisions:** the button
   calls with A-T offsuit and Nolan's sevens hold. The account reports
   a double-up and chip lead, but not a complete board. Separately,
   the later reported 1.4 million stack at a 60,000 big blind and a
   four-way deal with a 2,900-dollar payment are not inputs known at the
   original decision. The reported payment is distinguished from the
   advertised 5,100-dollar first-place prize.
7. **Next study:** one conditional question and a specific plan to record
   the button's opening and calling hypotheses separately, without
   inventing ICM outputs.

The archived version 7 source had been reviewed on November 3. Neither the
original tournament calendar date nor the newer public video's scene timing
was independently established. No hidden opponent identity, exact suits,
board cards, pot, opponent range, payout ladder or solver configuration is
fabricated.
