# Turn 128: Lead with the evidence on two standalone AI articles

Date: November 11, 2026

Baseline: `585f5550e22aa1e19982a74963080ac530395620`

## Reader issue and scope

Live desktop review after Turn 127 found that both source-grounded evidence
summaries were readable, but appeared only after two or three introductory
paragraphs. At a 1,363-pixel-wide desktop viewport with 925 pixels of height,
the cards began below the initially visible screen. Readers therefore had to
scroll past a substantial narrative introduction to discover the boundaries of
the evidence. This is a documented layout observation, not an analytics result
or evidence of visitor dissatisfaction.

A second pre-change live inspection at a 1,707-by-932 browser viewport measured
the laptop card's top at 1,256 pixels from the unscrolled viewport top; the
introductory paragraph began at 665 pixels. This confirms that the existing
card followed long introductory copy on the public page.

## Change

- Moved the existing **Evidence at a glance** `aside` on the local-AI gaming
  laptop guide directly after its section heading, ahead of the three longer
  opening paragraphs. The summary preserves the distinction between laptop
  experience, historical Ubuntu CPU-runner results, and unmeasured laptop
  performance. The original introduction remains intact immediately after it.
- Moved the existing summary on the independent review-reply evidence audit
  directly after its question heading. The summary continues to distinguish
  the three publicly archived outputs from synthetic negative controls,
  documented false negatives, and business outcomes that were not measured.
- Preserved every claim, link, evidence anchor, heading ID, original article
  paragraph, published URL, metadata date, and shared styling. No new
  statistical or technical result is claimed.
- Added `test_turn128_early_evidence.py` to enforce this source and rendered
  HTML document order, including negative fixtures that postpone or duplicate
  the summary. Registered the regression in the manual local release checklist.

This change does not alter the home page, create new tracking, add a scheduled
task, incur paid API charges, or enable GitHub Actions. The card remains a
native HTML summary that works without JavaScript. Earlier presentation tests
continue to check the narrow-screen and print CSS contracts.
