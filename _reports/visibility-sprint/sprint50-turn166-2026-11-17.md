# Visibility sprint Turn 166: Outcome-blind transfer practice for poker hand review

Date: November 17, 2026 (America/Phoenix)
Baseline main: `e7ed0472d51d8826fc55a04e90f8ad9177e972aa`

## Reader problem

The hand-review method now has three completed case studies from narrated
poker decisions. Those teach the distinction between observations,
calculations, opponent hypotheses, action alternatives and outcomes.
However, readers can simply read the completed examples. They have not
been offered a **new, unseen case** to demonstrate that they can transfer
the method before viewing the checking answer.

This turn adds a question-first exercise with a separate checking key.
The case is fictional, with no player privacy issue, no actual outcome,
and no claimed optimal action. Nothing is submitted to the website.

## New user-facing resources

### Question sheet: `downloads/poker-hand-review-practice-01.md`

A six-handed fictional tournament with 14 remaining and 10 paid,
2,000/4,000 blinds and a separate 4,000 big-blind ante. Hero has
80,000 chips before forced posting with Q-spades/J-spades in the
big blind. The cutoff starts with 96,000 and raises **to 10,000**.
The small blind folds, leaving 2,000 in the pot. A separate,
hypothetical branch asks about a reraise **to 24,000**, assuming
the cutoff immediately folds.

The complete seven-stage practice asks the reader to classify
stipulated facts, derived calculations, assumptions and unknowns;
reconstruct the live/dead contributions; distinguish a call from
a raise-to amount; identify an uncalled refund; keep cutoff
opening and continuation hypotheses separate; and name what
is missing for chip-EV versus tournament prize-value analysis.

**The question sheet never supplies the numerical answers.**
There are no hidden villain cards, full board, winner, actual
call frequency, computed ICM values or real hand result. It
links the answer key only **after a visible Stop here barrier**
at the end of the seven questions.

### Checking key: `downloads/poker-hand-review-practice-01-key.md`

A distinct public Markdown file holds an objective, independently
checkable accounting ledger:

| Check | Amount |
| --- | ---: |
| Chips behind after 4k live big blind and 4k dead ante | 72,000 |
| Pot before big blind acts | 20,000 |
| Additional chips to call 10k raise-to | 6,000 |
| Pot if hero calls | 26,000 |
| Chips behind after call | 66,000 |
| Additional chips to raise to 24k total | 20,000 |
| Chips behind immediately after raising | 52,000 |
| Unmatched 24k minus 10k returned if cutoff folds | 14,000 |
| Awarded pot after uncalled refund | 26,000 |
| Final hero stack in the assumed raise/fold branch | 92,000 |
| Net chip gain relative to the 80k starting stack | 12,000 |

The key separates the 14k uncalled **refund** from the
26k **awarded pot**, including the hero's own matched bet
and dead ante. It explains that the 6k/26k immediate pot-price
fraction, about 23.1%, is not a solved multistreet call
strategy or enough to rank QJs in the tournament. An observation
of two raises in six hands does not supply an opponent range.
Exact tournament payout steps and remaining stacks are unknown.

The key has a five-item self-check for procedural and
arithmetic correctness, **not** a score for choosing a
profitable action. No user input, cookies, network requests or
player data are collected.
