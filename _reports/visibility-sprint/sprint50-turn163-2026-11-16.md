## Reader gap

The hand-review guide introduced a complete, source-bounded A-K example for
**two successive preflop decisions** in Turn 162. The other documented
Portland Meadows decision, a 6-4 offsuit small-blind shove with 13 players
remaining and 10 paid, was available only as a short prompt and later
narrative. It illustrates a different learning task: distinguish a
near-bubble payout-pressure hypothesis from a documented opponent range,
then stop when effective stacks or ICM inputs are missing.

The primary risk was post-hoc reasoning. The big blind's actual hole cards
and the favorable outcome are known to the viewer, but neither could
justify the small blind's earlier choice in isolation.

## Implementation

### New worked example

Added `downloads/poker-hand-review-example-64o.md`, mirroring the seven
steps of the private blank review sheet.

1. **Situation:** the archived narration reports a small blind with
   6-4 offsuit, **13 remaining / 10 paid**, blinds
   **8,000 / 15,000** and a separate **15,000 big-blind ante**.
   The approximate 148,000 stack converts to
   **148,000 / 15,000 = 9.87 BB** as arithmetic, but is
   pre-posting only if that was the stack-count convention.
   Conditional on that interpretation, posting 8,000 leaves
   roughly 140,000 behind; neither count is independently verified.
2. **Actions:** button folds, small blind shoves and big blind calls,
   as narrated. The example does not invent a precise shoved
   amount, earlier contributions or complete contested pot.
3. **Alternatives:** folding, completing, smaller raising and
   shoving are compared as conditional action families, not
   ranked. A stronger claim requires a justified full opponent
   calling range, pot, effective stacks, payouts and the available
   action tree.
4. **At-table thinking versus narration:** the described
   tight-aggressive opponent read was recalled later. The
   example does not promote it into a verified contemporaneous
   thought or objective opponent frequency.
5. **Sensitivity and stopping:** the worked review asks how wider
   and tighter calling ranges, opponent coverage, other field
   stacks, payouts and blind/ante conventions change the
   hypotheses. It refuses a numerical chip-EV or ICM verdict
   when the inputs cannot be established.
6. **Result:** only after the earlier decision analysis does it
   disclose the narrated big blind's **A-K suited**, a four
   appearing and the 6-4 hand winning. No full board or
   individual 6-4 suits are invented. The outcome does not
   prove the shove correct.
7. **Next study:** a single question about payout-value
   sensitivity and a link back to the generic private worksheet.
   It also points to the contrasting A-K two-decision example.

The archived version 7 source packet was reviewed November 3,
2026. The exact tournament calendar date and the newer YouTube
cut's timing remain unverified. No solver, risk-premium, fold-equity,
pot-odds or ICM result is invented.
