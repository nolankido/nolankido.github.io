# Turn 142: Correct AI project deep-link arrivals earlier

Date: November 12, 2026

Baseline: `64c1d81eb4dedd3488e6316a37a359d7b7485b78`

## Observed problem

Turn 141 introduced a visibly outlined, scoped native fragment destination
for three practical AI project pages. A direct Chrome check at a 320-pixel
viewport found the Preflight example heading temporarily covered by the
roughly 149-pixel sticky header at an approximately 1.75-second observation,
before it corrected by the three-second check. The script's only adjustment
waited another 1300 milliseconds after page load and font readiness.

This was an observed timing issue on one direct arrival, not evidence that
every browser hid the heading or that search and engagement suffered.

## Improvement

- Keep waiting for page load and font readiness, then check the selected
  heading on the next animation frame. If the heading is under the sticky
  header or near the viewport bottom, bring it into view promptly.
- Preserve the 1300-millisecond second check to account for late layout
  shifts, including elements that move after the first correction.
- Preserve the existing exact fragment and case-page allowlist, visibility
  threshold, instantaneous scrolling, and native focus behavior. Keyboard,
  pointer, wheel, touch or page-exit interaction cancels both the pending
  animation frame and delayed timer. Hash changes also cancel stale checks.
- Keep the shared script scoped to the Review Reply Writer, Preflight and
  Dinner on Time case pages. Rebuild only their versioned script references.
  No content claims, article dates, canonical URLs, schema facts, tracking,
  personal data, paid services, CSS or automated workflows changed.
