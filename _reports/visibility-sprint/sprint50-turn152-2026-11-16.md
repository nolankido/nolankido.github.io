# Visibility sprint Turn 152: Find the right short-stack lesson

Date: November 16, 2026 (Pacific)

Baseline commit: `7dc0ae35bdbcd73f5bfdd26b3aec79cccadbb831`

## Why this improvement

The free Poker hub had an existing 10–20 BB link in its decision-study
path. Two separately published November 15 guides for 3–5 and 6–10 big
blinds were present in the site manifest and sitemap, but not in this
prominent reader-choice section. A visitor holding a much shorter stack
should not have to search the entire library to reach the appropriate
worked examples.

## Implemented

Added one short, native paragraph below the existing Poker goal routes
at `/poker/#reading-routes`. It links, in increasing stack order, to:

1. `/poker/three-to-five-bb-tournament-decisions/` for shoves and calls.
2. `/poker/six-to-ten-bb-tournament-decisions/` for opens and rejams.
3. `/poker/short-stack-decisions/` for 10–20 BB context and ICM.

The paragraph explicitly says these are worked examples, not universal
shove charts. It uses the site's existing `poker-entry-note` design and
normal links, without JavaScript, new font assets, tracking, or services.

Edited the authored Poker hub and its committed generated HTML together.
The main Poker content revision remains November 16, the date of the
previous substantive change and this one. The guide URLs, dates, article
text, site metadata, canonical identities, existing video links, and
player-record caveats were not modified.

**Not executed in this environment:** the full Python site builder,
HTML5 parser, full historical unit suite, and interactive Chromium
browser suite. This report does not represent those tests as passing.
The pre-existing failures documented in Turn 151 have not been repaired
or reevaluated by this scoped reader-navigation change.

## What it does not establish

The improvement supplies clearer internal paths to existing study content.
It does not prove increased clicks, search rankings, retention, AI-assistant
citations, improved playing decisions, correct opponent ranges, or optimal
tournament strategy. No private analytics were published or used.
