# Turn 99: Put a useful first action before the project gallery

Date: November 10, 2026

Baseline: `e9b9769428c2e314878d3c0759135edf91df13a5`

## Reader problem

The homepage introduces Nolan, offers a shortcut to three bounded
evidence records, and then displays the entire three-project gallery
before the existing "Start with something useful" choices. A first-time
reader looking for an immediate task must pass that longer showcase
before reaching a free tool, an inspectable repair, or an explanatory
essay. This is a structural usability observation, not measured visitor
behavior or a claim about search performance.

## Improvement

- Moved the existing "Start with something useful" section to follow
  the evidence records immediately and precede the full project gallery.
- Retained the three existing choices in their original order: build a
  task brief, inspect a recorded website correction, or read the human
  decision principle. The content, destinations, caveats and no-account
  or no-AI-run disclosures were preserved exactly.
- Kept the secondary reading and tool links inside the existing native
  disclosure. No new home links, remote scripts, tracking, design assets
  or additional first-screen promotional cards were introduced.
- Preserved the established five hero actions, source evidence block,
  project gallery, four topic destinations, canonical URL and Person ID.
- Advanced only the homepage's genuine significant-content revision
  to November 10 in its manifest, generated WebPage structured data and
  sitemap entry. The homepage's publication date was not fabricated or
  applied to unrelated articles.
- Added `_tests/test_turn99_home_useful_first.py` to detect a missing,
  duplicate, moved-back, relabeled or script-injected action section,
  and to verify that source and compiled homepage blocks agree. Wired
  this test into the default manual local release checklist.

The site remains a personal introduction with evidence and practical
work visible. No private analytics, user records, unsupported outcomes,
new business claims or subscription products were published. No GitHub
Actions, cron jobs or paid APIs were invoked.
