# Turn 156: Evidence-first review exercises on three documented hands

Date: November 16, 2026 (Arizona)
Baseline main: `6d7029a2ce5e9a3b514794243f96dda29ffca52f`
Scope: Two existing, separately authored Portland Meadows companion articles

## What needed improvement

Turns 151–155 connected the Poker homepage, NolanPoker videos, source-bounded
written companions and 6–20 BB study guides. The three hand records still
mostly presented facts, narration and retrospective judgment as reading
material. Readers could reach the associated guides, but there was no
compact, direct activity asking them to identify what the evidence
did and did not establish.

## Changes

Added one short **Reader exercise** immediately after the existing hand
fact ledger in each of three documented sections:

1. **Episode 1, 6-4 offsuit near the bubble** (`#hand-64o-record`,
   disclosure `#hand-64o-review`). The visible prompt asks the reader
   to ignore the favorable outcome, recompute the reported stack in BB,
   identify 13 remaining versus 10 paid and list decision-changing
   unknowns. Opening the native summary reveals the reported 148,000 /
   15,000 = approximately 9.87 BB (conditional on pre-posting) and
   distinguishes an A-K showdown from a known calling range. It does
   not rank shove/fold strategy.

2. **Episode 1, pocket sevens four-handed** (`#hand-77-record`,
   disclosure `#hand-77-review`). The reader lists calling, smaller
   raising and shoving as described in the archived narration, then
   names missing button-range, table-stack and payout inputs.
   Revealed text emphasizes that a call by A-T offsuit and a double-up
   do not establish the best reshove decision.

3. **Stand Off, the new A-K decision after two shoves**
   (`#standoff-second-decision`, disclosure
   `#standoff-second-review`). The reader separates the initial
   15,000 flat-call from the approximately 46,000 additional amount
   at risk, asks which ranges and payouts would be needed and checks
   the role of the distinct 6,000 big-blind ante. The visible answer
   retains the important distinction between a 10.2 BB stack *before*
   calling and what remained for the subsequent choice. It does not
   infer a solver or ICM verdict from the eventual kings/queens result
   or later hotel recollection.

All three use semantic `aside`, a visible prompt, and a natively
interactive `details class="poker-details"` disclosure with
`summary`. They are collapsed by default, work without a
JavaScript dependency, and reuse existing Poker styles and focus
behavior. No new sections with H2 headings or changes to the reading
outline were introduced.
