# Visibility sprint Turn 26: Evidence-first Technology entrance

Reviewed: November 4, 2026. Parent commit: `e7d9fdb8ab000e996734a02af83cf65132411a58`.

## Decision and purpose

The Technology hub is the most natural nonbranded search entry into Nolan Kido's real-world AI work. The prior introduction contained a general promise, two paragraphs, five visible section links, a separate new-case notice, and a contact route before readers reached the three existing project cards. A visitor looking for proof, a usable tool, or specific applications had to parse that hierarchy first.

This turn changes the **first choice**, not the corpus. Present a simple promise, a real author, and three routes with explicit evidence labels. Keep the depth available without making every destination compete for attention on arrival.

It serves the three website goals:

1. **Public discovery:** a descriptive visible heading and specific, internally linked material help search arrivals understand the page. Source, headings, and local Find index now agree. This is search readiness, not measured search growth.
2. **Credibility:** the first starting point is a documented public identity-metadata correction with a before/after record and an offline structural checker; the intro links back to a source-backed personal About page. It avoids claiming that traffic, AI citations or business results were proven.
3. **Recognizable association:** the introduction explicitly connects practical AI to small-business tasks, writing, poker-video production and everyday planning without pretending that each topic has the same type or quality of evidence.

## Accessibility and content contracts

The three starting points are semantic `article` elements grouped under a clearly labelled `div` with existing responsive `identity-readings` styles. Their links are native `a` elements and remain visible without JavaScript. The secondary jump list is retained in a native `details` with a keyboard-usable `summary`; it is not a custom JavaScript toggle. The existing five link labels and targets are retained exactly.

The case fragment `#identity-claim-audit` and projects fragment `#technology-projects` still exist on the same Technology page. The free task-brief builder is at the existing `/technology/task-brief/`. The human-judgment method, small-business pilot, contact route and reading guides remain reachable. No unmeasured outcome has been turned into a claim.

This follows [Google's people-first content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) for descriptive titles, original evidence and clear author information. Native disclosure behavior is consistent with the [W3C disclosure pattern](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/disclosure/). Neither reference promises a ranking benefit.

## What to measure before another redesign

Use comparable 28-day windows, keeping raw analytics outside the public repository:

- **Findability:** branded queries for Nolan Kido and nonbranded practical-AI queries reaching Technology, Home and About.
- **Comprehension:** ask several new readers to identify who Nolan is, one verifiable example, what the tool does, and a secondary interest after a five-second glance. Record their actual answers, not the editor's impression.
- **Path choice:** observed movement toward the case, tool and project pages, when available from legitimate aggregated analytics. A jump to a fragment is not automatically tracked, and no event tracking was installed.
- **Outcome:** treat more clicks or better comprehension as evidence only when actually measured. This turn has no independently measured improvement.
