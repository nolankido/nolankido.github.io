# Visibility sprint Turn 33: Stand Off Episode 2 standalone source-backed companion

Reviewed November 4, 2026. Starting website main commit: \`04a452d5806799778d7ae040549ffceec78871af\`.

## Decision

The previous turn published a concise Stand Off A-K source note on the Poker hub. A November 3, 2026 archived Episode 2 v9 script, source-verification memo, production notes and QA report were available in the owner's private library. Those support enough of the sequence and narrated reasoning for a substantive standalone written article, but the currently public YouTube upload was not independently played, listened to, or frame-matched against v9 in this session. The public YouTube page was throttled in the available public web check. Thus the article must **not** publish chapter timestamps for the current cut, claim independent video/graphic validation, or infer a solver/ICM answer.

**Decision:** publish one canonical, original, source-bounded six-section video companion. Reuse the existing manually owned Episode 1 design and deterministic standalone builder rather than adding an unapproved draft to the empty poker catalog or creating a duplicate AI-generated listicle.

## Public content released

Primary URL: https://nolankido.com/poker/episodes/portland-meadows-standoff/

The article is organized as a first-person account with six stable sections:

1. **Event setting:** $200 Stand Off, 30,000 starting chips, twelve planned rounds, survivor ICM-based payouts as the player described them. Archived clock source reported 17 of 78 remaining in round ten, with 3,000/6,000 blinds and 6,000 ante. Official calendar date and detailed payout model are not independently established.
2. **First decision:** A-K on the button, with the v9 source still labeling A-clubs and K-diamonds. The opener made it 15,000, middle player called, Nolan flat-called rather than jamming. The reported about 61,000 stack left around 46,000 after the initial 15,000 investment. Suits are attributed to the archived original, not independently checked against the currently published upload.
3. **Second decision:** Big blind jam, initial opener reshove, middle caller fold. With both all-in players covering him as narrated, Nolan had to decide whether to put in around 46,000 more. That was a new tournament decision, not a continuation of the original call without reassessing risk. The big blind could have closed the initial action for another 9,000, from 15,000 raise-to less 6,000 posted blind. The separate 6,000 big blind ante is not another blind wager.
4. **Showdown:** Narrated big blind K-K, original opener Q-Q, A-K eliminated in round ten without cashing. No full board, opponent suits, other exact stacks or event prize numbers are invented.
5. **Hotel reassessment:** Nolan initially said he could not fold, then reconsidered how the big blind's willingness to jam rather than call might narrow its range. This is explicitly a retrospective hypothesis, not the opponent's independently known range or numerical chip-EV/ICM proof.
6. **Source and checking:** The archived production files remain private. The existing public source-claim ledger, offline fact gate and editorial truth-to-screen worksheet are linked, with an explanation that a clean check does not validate the video/audio.

The current public upload remains https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I75GOBVXij4. No video iframe, external thumbnail, private opponent identity, exact current-cut chapter offsets, fictional board, net profit, official WSOP media status or solver verdict was added.
