# A checkable everyday-AI worked guide

Baseline: `9a92735d39c139adac8ba41a2adc4aeef35e609f` (PR 44).
Review date: October 12, 2026. Scope: nolankido.com only.

## Assessment and priorities

The existing site already has connected author identity, descriptive metadata, usable tools, a real website-checking case, task-based guide discovery, portable worksheets, and reusable biographies. Repeating those changes or inflating Nolan's biography would not fill the remaining credibility gap.

The most useful new on-site contribution is a checkable explanation of an everyday AI task. The existing verification examples focus on code and calculations. A reader using AI to summarize planning notes needs to see how a correct-looking number or date can still form part of a false claim, and how omitted conditions and permissions can change the next action. This is a distinct practical lesson, not a second general essay about being careful with AI.

Priority order:
1. Provide the source, flawed draft, annotations, repaired version and reusable method together, so the reader can examine the reasoning rather than accept an authority claim.
2. Give that work a specific, descriptive search destination, and connect it to the existing Technology, Writing, Resources, search and subscription paths.
3. Preserve the distinction between an authored teaching example and a recorded real-world result throughout the publication system.
4. Establish domain-specific Search Console access and genuine outcome evidence before making traffic, demand, productivity or authority claims.

GSC Wizard was read again in this session. The connected properties still do not include nolankido.com. No unrelated property's metrics were used. No authenticated query baseline, search-volume estimate, Google URL inspection, sitemap submission or ranking movement was obtained. Missing property access is not evidence of zero traffic or non-indexing.

## Implemented content

New canonical route: `/technology/check-ai-summary/`.
Search title: `How to Check an AI Summary: Worked Example | Nolan Kido`.
Visible title: `Check an AI summary against its source.`
Publication date: October 12, 2026. No existing publication dates are changed.

The guide contains seven numbered fictional source passages, a deliberately flawed summary, six native answer disclosures with exact source links, a repaired summary, a six-step review method, an optional comparison prompt, and an exact downloadable checklist. The exercise teaches checks for certainty, conditions and task ownership, the meaning of amounts, permission to act, unsupported deadlines, and consequential omissions.

All people, notes and drafts in the exercise are invented for teaching. The flawed draft is not a captured AI response. The page makes no model-comparison, benchmark, measured learning, time-saving or personal-result claim. It distinguishes fidelity to the supplied source from external truth/currentness and permission to use or send the result.

`downloads/ai-summary-review.txt` is a blank private-use checklist, not an input form. Its full text appears in the guide. No new runtime JavaScript, model connection, uploads, storage, analytics events or external services are introduced. Native disclosures and links work without JavaScript. The existing site's disclosed analytics and fonts remain unchanged.

## Discovery and publishing

Technology's verification section and Resources link to the guide; Resources also offers the checklist. The guide appears in the Writing hub and once in both the Practical AI and combined writing feeds. It is included in the sitemap and internal search, with connected Nolan Kido Article authorship, a canonical address, visible/structured breadcrumbs, publication date and descriptive metadata.

A new explicit `worked_guide` publication type prevents authored exercises from being labeled as recorded case studies. A record claiming both types is rejected. The existing real website case keeps its original label and body. The three existing work-gallery cards remain intact, as do Home, About and the reusable biographies.

The search-intent hypothesis is a reader asking how to check an AI summary against its source. The page does not claim to be an automatic hallucination detector or an accuracy guarantee. Descriptive metadata and crawlable links help expose the work; Google decides indexing, ranking and displayed snippets. Feeds are distribution routes, not a claimed ranking factor.

## What this does not establish

Publication and passing tests do not establish Google indexing, traffic growth, improved real-user Core Web Vitals, independent recognition, measured personal credibility or successful real-world outcomes. The next stronger evidence would be an approved, genuine task record with before/after artifacts and the complete checking effort, paired with actual site search data. No credentials, testimonials, new profile claims or private-project details have been invented to fill that gap.
