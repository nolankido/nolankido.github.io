# Poker Foundations: free first-course concept

Prepared October 15, 2026. This is a course design and implementation record,
not a report of observed learners or measured learning gains.

## The promise

Learn to follow a no-limit Texas Hold’em hand and explain your next decision.
The course serves complete beginners and people who recognize the rules but
lose track of the action. A learner can practice with a deck, counters or paper.
No wager, playing account, email address or purchase is needed.

The course is already available through the existing learning-path URL. This
release gives the existing material a clearer identity, entrance and finish.
It does not describe new videos, individual coaching or enrollment as available.

## The learning route

Start with the card foundations when needed. Build a best-five hand, follow a
scripted hand and rehearse a betting round. These are supporting activities,
not three mandatory prerequisites for someone who already understands them.

| Core lesson | Observable task |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Cards, actions and the pot | Identify the best five, whose turn it is and the additional amount owed. |
| 2. Your starting hand in context | Describe position, prior action and relevant stacks before evaluating two cards. |
| 3. Your first decisions after the flop | Separate the current made hand from a draw and describe the action. |
| 4. The turn, river and showdown | Rebuild the best five and separate a call price from evidence of sufficient equity. |
| 5. Your first range | Distinguish known cards, possible holdings and assumptions about frequency. |
| 6. Betting with a purpose | Describe the responses a bet needs and compare the bet with checking. |

The canonical lesson order, titles, goals and repair destinations remain in
`_source/poker/beginner-course.json`. The homepage syllabus is rendered from
that data rather than maintaining another copy of the curriculum.

For each visit: choose one task, give an explanation, check it, record one thing
to revisit. Use the lesson's short first pass and one practice situation. Save
the ordinary lesson URL in the existing private-use study card or paper notes.
The site does not save progress or issue completion credentials.

## A meaningful finish

Implemented October 16: the existing checkpoint now begins with two connected
final hands. Explain Hand A at four street-by-street stops before revealing the
result. Hand B changes position, draw completion and call price for a later
attempt. The existing individual situations support precise repairs. Both sets
of hand prompts are included without solutions in the six-stage study card.
These are fixed self-checks, not a validated assessment or automatic score.

After the six lessons, attempt the beginner checkpoint with the lessons closed.
Use the specific repair link when an explanation fails. On a later visit, try
the changed situation. Finishing means reaching a useful self-check and choosing
a next task; opening every page does not establish competence or profitable play.

Next steps are mixed beginner practice, first-session preparation, following a
video, or a format-specific study route. Long essays and external courses are
optional references rather than another compulsory beginner syllabus.

## Website organization

- Poker home: a clear free-course entrance followed by independent visitor paths.
- Free course: audience, outcomes, starting choices, six lessons and a finish.
- Practice: explained beginner exercises before the more demanding decision labs.
- Guides and resources: searchable references, essays and curated external sources.
- Poker with Nolan: genuine published stories and episode companions, when available.

The shared Poker navigation exposes the free course and highlights that section
throughout its lessons, practice room and checkpoint. Existing URLs and fragment
targets remain available. Existing personal-site navigation and branding remain.

## Preparation for a later paid course

Keep this free course useful and complete. A later paid program can add Nolan's
original recorded instruction, connected hand walkthroughs, deeper practice and
clearly bounded feedback. Develop and observe learners using one complete sample
module before committing to a full production schedule.

An optional practice email series could later reuse this public curriculum.
Email delivery requires a real configured subscription service and explicit
opt-in. This release adds neither an inactive signup form nor a waitlist claim.
No paid course, checkout, availability date or learner outcomes are advertised.

## Verification and limits

Verify the native links, existing fragment targets, single current navigation
location, metadata, build determinism and unchanged exercise logic. Check mobile
and desktop layouts, keyboard journeys, disabled-script access and accessibility
through the repository's browser suites before publication.

This remains a content and interface review. Before expanding the paid offering,
observe real beginners attempting unfamiliar hands and evaluate whether they can
explain the relevant decisions after a delay. Audience interest and willingness
to pay require separate evidence.
